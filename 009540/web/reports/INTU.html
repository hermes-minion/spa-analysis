1. The setup

  • The SaaSpocalypse has taken Intuit down 47% in a year and 58% from its high. The shares now trade at 14.4x forward earnings and 13.5x EV/EBITDA — a valuation last seen in the 2011-12 era — for a business that still grew revenue 13.9% in FY2026 with an 81% gross margin and 40% FCF margin.
  • The bear case is AI disintermediation of tax and accounting. If an agent can categorise expenses, file taxes and reconcile books, the value of QuickBooks/TurboTax seats falls. Intuit's counter is that it owns the data, the compliance workflows and the accountant channel, and is embedding its own agents (Intuit Assist) across the stack.
  • FY2026 (ended 31-Jul-2026) was strong: revenue $21.45B (+13.9%), net income $4.57B, EPS $16.46, FCF $8.66B. Q4 revenue $4.35B (+13.6% YoY). There is no fundamental deterioration in the reported numbers — only in the multiple.
  • Four franchises, one ecosystem: TurboTax (consumer tax), QuickBooks (SMB accounting), Credit Karma (consumer finance marketplace), Mailchimp (SMB marketing). Cross-sell and the "mid-market" upstack are the growth engines.
  • Balance sheet is fine: cash $7.2B, debt $8.4B, net debt $1.22B; current ratio 1.5; ROE 23.6%, ROIC 22.9%. Share count down 2.1% YoY.
  • Sentiment is extreme: 50-DMA $318, 200-DMA $351, price $342 — the stock has stabilised above the 50-day and is retesting the 200-day. Short interest 3.3%. A decent Q1 FY2027 print could force a sharp re-rating.

2. Business and moat

Intuit monetises recurring compliance and financial workflows. QuickBooks is the system of record for millions of small businesses (subscription + payments + payroll + lending); TurboTax is the consumer tax franchise (unit sales plus paid upgrades); Credit Karma is an advertising/marketplace business; Mailchimp is SMB email/automation.

The moat: (1) data network effects — transactions, categorisation and tax outcomes improve with scale; (2) switching costs — migrating books and history is painful; (3) regulatory complexity — tax codes are a natural barrier; (4) channel — accountants and bookkeepers recommend Intuit. AI can lower the cost of bookkeeping, but it also lowers Intuit's cost to serve and can expand the market to businesses that never hired a bookkeeper.

The risk is pricing: Intuit has raised prices aggressively, and AI-native competitors (Seed, Finally, Digits, OpenAI-based agents) can undercut on simple workflows. The mid-market and tax-compliance layers are stickier than basic bookkeeping.

Scenario math. Forward EPS ≈ $23.8. Bear: AI agents erode SMB seats, growth slows to 4-5%, EPS $22, multiple 12x → $264. Base: growth decelerates to 8-10%, EPS $24, multiple 18x → $432. Bull: agents expand the SMB market and Intuit's take rate holds, EPS $26, multiple 22x → $572. Probabilities 30/45/25 → ~$416 (+22%).

At 10.5x FCF, the stock is discounting a permanent growth collapse. Even the bear case is only -23% from here, while the base/bull skew is favourable — an asymmetry the market rarely offers in a 20% ROIC franchise.

5. Catalysts

  • Q1 FY2027 results (mid/late Nov 2026): guidance for the new fiscal year and any AI-agent monetisation disclosure. Zacks expects a beat.
  • Tax season 2027 (Feb-Apr): TurboTax unit and ARPU trends; the first read on AI-assisted filing.
  • QuickBooks mid-market and payments attach: the growth engine that justifies a higher multiple.
  • Buybacks: $8.7B FCF funds ~3-4% annual share reduction.
