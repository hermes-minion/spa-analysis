0. Classification (mandatory)

Profile D — cash-burning. FY2025 revenue $127.7M (−19%), operating margin −58.2% excluding impairments, FCF −$42.5M and SBC 18.3% of revenue. $376.5M of net cash funds several years, but the $100M ATM and prior dilution make the share count the first variable to model. Business model: AI decision-support software and services for defence, intelligence and logistics; low capital intensity, negative FCF. Metrics that matter: EV/sales, cash runway, dilution and organic growth; P/E and EBITDA are not representative.

1. Executive summary

  • Three-quarters of the market value is gone, and dilution — not demand — is why. BBAI is −73.4% over 52 weeks at $1.91, within 2% of its 52-week low of $1.88 [1]. Shares went from 251.6M (Dec 2024) to 437.0M (Dec 2025) to 481.4M (Nov 2026), +91% in 23 months, after ~$706M of 2025 equity issuance, a $272.1M all-cash acquisition and conversion of the 2029 notes at $3.55 [2][3][4]. Revenue did not grow over the same period.
  • FY2025 was the trough; FY2026 is an inflection, but the growth is bought. Q3 revenue $43.6M +31.5% YoY (third straight growth quarter); FY2026 guidance raised to $150–165M (+23% at the $157.5M midpoint) — the first raise in company history [5][6]. Strip Ask Sage ($21.2M of 9M revenue, $8.9M in Q3) and 9M organic revenue is −6.7%; Q3 is the first positive organic quarter (+4.6%) [4] (my calculation).
  • Margins improved; operating leverage did not. Q3 gross margin 31.2%, +878bp, on Ask Sage's software mix [5]. But 9M SG&A rose 33% (80% of revenue, from 69%) and R&D 55%, so 9M operating loss ex-impairments widened to −$76.0M from −$62.7M [4]. Adjusted EBITDA −$9.1M in Q3 versus −$9.4M a year earlier: better margin, same dollars [6].
  • The balance sheet is the bull case; the dilution machinery is the bear case — both are true. $399.0M of cash and securities against $22.5M of debt, which becomes $17.7M of notes maturing 15 Dec 2026 [4]. Net cash is 41% of market cap. Against it: a 100M-share ATM, undrawn (21% of shares), 1.0bn shares authorised (from 500M in June 2026, leaving 519M unissued), and 32.4M anti-dilutive instruments [4].
  • Physical-AI purity is ~20% of revenue and will not exceed ~30% in three years. Computer vision (Pangiam biometrics at 24+ US airports, CT threat detection, CargoSeer, ConductorOS edge) is real physical AI. But Ask Sage (~19% of revenue) is an enterprise genAI platform with no physical interface, and ~61% is legacy decision-analytics services. This is a services company with a CV division and a genAI division, not a Physical AI pure-play (§3).
  • Not cheap on the right denominator. At $543M EV: 3.45x FY2026E sales and 12.7x TTM gross profit, versus a defense-services median of ~1.3x and ~6.4x [1][7]. That premium requires 20%+ growth; organic growth is ~0% and FCF is −51% of revenue.

Verdict: BEARISH — conviction Medium. 12-month fair value ~$1.55 (−19%). The business has stabilised and the cash is real, but 3.45x forward sales for a negative-FCF services company with a 21%-of-shares ATM overhang is not defensible. The short side is crowded (28.4% of shares out), so this is a fundamental underweight, not a tactical short; a take-out or two quarters of double-digit organic growth would break the thesis (§14).

Customers and economics. The US government is ~90% of revenue: $100.7M of $114.8M in 9M FY2026, $114.7M of $127.7M in FY2025 (89.8%), $143.1M of $158.2M in FY2024 (90.4%) [2][4]. Non-US-government and commercial is 10%; substantially all revenue is generated in the United States [4]. Contract mix is shifting to software: FFP was 51% of 9M FY2026 revenue ($58.2M) versus 24% in 9M FY2025, T&M 45%, cost-reimbursable 4% [4] — the Ask Sage subscription model showing through, and the best structural argument in the bull case.

Backlog quality is weak. Management leads with $330.1M backlog at 30 Sep 2026 (+33% YTD, +22% QoQ) [4]. But funded backlog is only $76.5M (23%), unfunded $163.7M (50%), priced options $87.4M (26%). Funded backlog covers 5.8 months of revenue, and GAAP remaining performance obligations are just $5.7M [4]. The $330M is a pipeline estimate, as the 10-Q states.

Segments/geography. One operating segment (CODM = CEO), no segment P&L [4]. Geography is effectively 100% United States.

3. Physical AI exposure

SENSING COMPUTE/SILICON DATA SOFTWARE/PERCEPTION SYSTEMS/DEPLOYMENT (none) (none) (customer-owned; Pangiam CV models 24+ US airports, CBP no proprietary (CT / X-ray / face) ports of entry, Army sensor corpus) ConductorOS edge runtime programmes Ask Sage (non-physical)

BBAI occupies layers 4–5 (AI platforms and applications), as the CEO described [6]. It owns no sensors, no silicon and no proprietary physical-world data; government customers supply the camera, CT and X-ray feeds, BBAI runs models on them and returns a decision. That is a software/perception position with no hardware or data lock-in — the weakest structural slot in the stack, but also the least capital-intensive.

~20% of FY2026E revenue is Physical AI — biometrics, aviation/cargo threat detection, edge autonomy.

Reasoning for 2029 (my model, not guidance): ECAC APIDS certification makes Pangiam Threat Detection one of only two third-party software solutions on the approved list, addressable across 44 member states and sold per screening lane alongside CT OEMs — a defensible wedge that could triple that line from a small base [9]. CBP biometric mandates and 24+ airports give veriScan a regulatory tailwind; CargoSeer sits in customs modernisation. But Ask Sage grows faster off a similar base, so the physical-AI share rises only to ~31%. Honest range 25–35%; I would not underwrite above 30%.

End markets. Defence/national security ~55–60% of revenue (DoD combatant commands, Army logistics, classified programmes); homeland security and border ~20% (CBP Targeting and Analysis Systems, two five-year subcontracts via SAIC and Unissant won in Q3) [6]; aviation security ~7%; trade and customs ~5%; critical infrastructure/industrial ~8% (shipyards, ports, manufacturing, hospital flow — Children's Minnesota renewed) [6]; robotics, humanoids and autonomous driving 0% — no product, no design wins, no revenue.

Scepticism check. The "AI" label does heavy lifting. Ask Sage is a distribution business — it resells third-party frontier models behind an accreditation; BigBear owns the accreditation and connectors, not the models. The physical AI is genuine (3D CT and face-based identity are perception systems on real sensors with independent certification) but small: ~$32M of revenue, which even at 10x sales is $320M against a $919M market cap. The other ~$125M is being valued at ~4.8x sales, and that is where the argument breaks.

4. Competitive position & moat

BBAI is a sub-scale prime subcontractor in a market dominated by companies 20–100x larger. 579 employees, $142M TTM revenue — $245k revenue per employee, which is services economics [1][2]. It primes some work and subcontracts to SAIC, Unissant and similar primes on the rest.

Source of advantage Strength Assessment
Security accreditation Strong, narrow FedRAMP, DoD IL5/IL6, Top-Secret authority for Ask Sage; CMMC Level 2 (2026) [6]. Real barrier for genAI in classified environments — but Booz Allen, Palantir and Azure Government hold equivalents.
Independent certification Strong, narrow ECAC APIDS Standard 1 plus Dutch national approval; one of two third-party solutions on the ECAC list [9]. The most defensible asset owned.
Programme incumbency Medium 20+ year CBP relationship, recompetes won in Q3. But the government can terminate for convenience and recompete at will [2].
Proprietary data Weak No owned sensor corpus, no fleet, no crowdsourced equivalent. Models improve on customer-owned data.
Scale / cost Weak $142M revenue vs Booz Allen's $11.0B and Leidos's $17.8B [7]; cannot bid the largest IDIQs alone.
Brand Weak-medium CEO Kevin McAleenan (former Acting CBP Commissioner) opens doors in homeland security — a relationship asset, not a moat.

Competitors. Palantir (the customer's default "AI for government" alternative) and Booz Allen are the two that matter; CACI, Leidos and SAIC compete on mission systems and are also BBAI's own primes; Accenture Federal and Deloitte on services; Idemia, Thales, Smiths Detection and SureScan in airport biometrics and CT screening (partners and competitors); and agency in-house development, the 10-K's first-listed competitor category. BBAI's differentiator is being small enough to be agile and cleared enough to be trusted — a real niche with no pricing power, as a 31% gross margin proves.

Customer concentration. Top six customers were 59% of FY2025 revenue, the largest at 16% ($20.7M) [2]. In Q3 FY2026 five customers were 51% of revenue (largest 15%) [4]. Customer-level concentration is better than most small-cap govtech names, but agency concentration (CBP/DHS plus DoD/IC) is far higher than the letters suggest, and one lost recompete would be material.

What the numbers say.

  1. Revenue has not grown in five years. $155.2M (FY2023) → $158.2M (FY2024) → $127.7M (FY2025) → ~$157.5M (FY2026E). A 0.5% three-year CAGR, 1.6% from FY2021's $145.6M, and FY2026 revenue will be below FY2024. The +23% headline is recovery from a depressed base plus an acquisition.
  2. All 2026 growth is Ask Sage. 9M revenue +$14.4M while Ask Sage contributed +$21.2M, so organic fell 6.7% [4] (calculation); the 10-Q cites "lower volume on the Army programs and significant one time contracts… that did not recur" [4]. Q3's +4.6% organic is the one genuinely encouraging datapoint.
  3. Gross-margin gains stop at the gross line. 22.3% → 32.6% is a real mix shift, corroborated by FFP rising from 24% to 51%. But SG&A rose $22.9M and R&D $6.5M, so 9M operating loss ex-impairments widened from −$62.7M to −$76.0M. Every incremental gross-profit dollar was spent twice on opex.
  4. Adjusted EBITDA has not improved in dollars in three years (−$3.2M, −$2.4M, −$35.1M, TTM −$40.8M) and sits near −$10M/quarter with no visible path to positive inside 24 months. FCF is the ugliest line: TTM −$72.7M (−51% margin), with 9M operating cash flow of −$49.8M against −$20.1M a year earlier; the gap to adjusted EBITDA is working capital, mainly a $17.8M receivables build. SBC is the one improving cost line, falling from 18.3% to 11.1% of revenue (~$18M/yr, ~2% annual dilution).

Runway is not a solvency question: $399M against ~$67M of TTM free cash outflow is ~6 years on operating burn alone — less if M&A continues — and the December note repayment is trivially covered. The real question is how much cash is spent on dilutive M&A before breakeven; management calls M&A a top-two priority and closed two deals in nine months [6].

Dilution is the defining risk, and it is structural:

  • +91% share count in 23 months (251.6M → 481.4M): ~185M shares issued in 2025 to raise roughly $706M ($72.6M Q1, $294.7M Q2, $339.0M Q3) [3], plus ~38M shares issued in Q1 FY2026 on conversion of the 2029 notes at $3.55 (55M cumulatively with 2025 conversions) — 46% below the price the notes were struck at [4].
  • A 100M-share ATM with Jefferies (31 Jul 2026), entirely undrawn at 30 Sep 2026 [4]. At $1.91 that is $191M and 21% dilution, and its existence caps every rally: a 20% move up becomes an incentive to sell stock.
  • 519M authorised-unissued shares after the June 2026 charter amendment — legal capacity to triple the count.
  • 32.4M anti-dilutive instruments (12.25M public warrants at $11.50, 3.77M RDO warrants, 1.66M note shares, 12.7M RSUs, 1.2M options, 0.7M ESPP); fully diluted ~513M [4].
  • Impairment is now annual: $85.0M (FY2024), $70.6M (FY2025), $76.7M (Q3 FY2026) — $232M in three years, roughly the whole Pangiam price plus 40% of Ask Sage goodwill within nine months [4]. The clearest evidence of poor capital allocation in the file.
