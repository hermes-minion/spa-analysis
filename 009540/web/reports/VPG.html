0. Classification (mandatory)

1. Executive summary

  • A 90-year-old precision-components franchise briefly re-rated as a humanoid-robotics stock, which has given most of it back. $29.08 (20 Nov 2025) → $151.78 intraday (30 Jun 2026) → $50.90 (13 Nov 2026): +29.6% YTD and +75% above the low, but −66% from the peak (Yahoo daily prices). Earnings broke it: Q2 FY2026 (5 Aug) missed and the stock fell ~25% in a day; Q3 (4 Nov) missed EPS by 36% and it fell 8%.
  • Orders are genuinely strong; the P&L is not. Q3 FY2026 adjusted revenue $87.4M (+9.6% YoY), orders >$103M, book-to-bill 1.17 — the eighth consecutive quarter ≥1.0 — backlog $151M vs $108.2M at YE2025 (Q3 release; Q3 call). But GAAP operating margin was 3.2%, 9M operating income $2.8M on $253.4M (1.1%), 9M net income −$1.5M.
  • The margin problem is structural and identifiable. Segment operating income was $35.4M (14.0% of revenue) in 9M FY2026; $31.1M of unallocated G&A plus restructuring cut consolidated operating income to $2.8M (10-Q, Note 10). SG&A has been $107–110M a year against $307–355M of revenue since FY2023 (FY2025 10-K). That is both the operating-leverage thesis and its risk.
  • Humanoid revenue in Q3 FY2026 was $300,000; bookings $200,000. Nine-month humanoid sales are ~$1.2M against a management-affirmed $4M FY2025 baseline — going down, not up. Management's own model assumes humanoid grows ~50%/yr from 2025 levels, which arithmetic puts at ~$20M by 2029, ~5% of revenue. The narrative runs an order of magnitude ahead of the P&L.
  • The real AI exposure is infrastructure, not physical AI. Management estimates 20–30% of the Sensors segment serves AI demand — precision resistors into semiconductor front-end/back-end equipment and data-centre fibre optics. That is ~8–12% of company revenue: a good, design-in business, but not robots perceiving and acting.
  • Valuation is not distressed. At $50.90: 1.80x EV/2026E sales — above the entire FY2021–FY2025 range of 1.01–1.66x — ~21x 2026E adjusted EBITDA, ~94x FY2026E adjusted EPS of $0.54, 2.03x book. TTM free cash flow was $1.5M. A 66% drawdown from a bubble is not the same as cheap.
  • The balance sheet is unambiguously good: net cash $64.9M (~10% of market cap), $59M undrawn on a $75M revolver to 2029, no convertibles, SBC just 0.6–0.9% of revenue, share count flat.

Verdict: NEUTRAL — conviction Medium. Base-case 12–24 month value ~$52.7; probability-weighted ~$54.9 (+8%). The order book and the AI-infrastructure resistor franchise make this worth owning eventually; today's price already capitalises the recovery, and the humanoid option is worth single digits, not the $100+ the market briefly paid.

2. Business overview

What they sell

VPG makes precision measurement and sensing components and systems: Bulk Metal® foil resistors, foil strain gauges, load cells and force transducers, onboard vehicle-weighing systems, and specialised measurement systems for steel mills, materials research and crash testing. It was spun out of Vishay Intertechnology on 6 July 2010; the foil technology dates to Felix Zandman's work in the early 1960s (FY2025 10-K, Item 1).

Correction to the briefing note: the segments are not Foil Technology / Force Sensors / Weighing & Control — those names were retired. Since the 2024 reorganisation VPG reports Sensors, Weighing Solutions and Measurement Systems (10-K Note 13). Modelling the old structure double-counts.

Revenue is 100% product — components, modules, systems — sold through field application engineers and distributors. No SaaS, no licensing, no service annuity. 3. Physical AI exposure

Where it sits in the stack

SENSING COMPUTE/SILICON DATA SOFTWARE/PERCEPTION SYSTEMS/DEPLOYMENT Foil strain gauges none none none none Force/torque cells Precision resistors (component supplier to robot OEMs, load-cell makers, semi-cap, steel mills)

VPG is a pure sensing-layer component supplier — no compute, no data asset, no perception software, no systems integration. That is not a criticism: sensing is where physical AI is thinnest and where 60 years of metallurgy and calibration data matter. But it caps value capture. Content is $400–500 per humanoid at tens of units/week, falling to $150–250 at hundreds+ (Q1 call). At 100,000 robots a year and $200 of content, the entire industry's pool is $20M.

4. Competitive position & moat

Bulk Metal® foil is a genuine proprietary materials technology. VPG makes its own resistive alloy, bonds and photolithographically etches it, achieving temperature coefficients as low as ±0.2 ppm/°C and single-digit ppm/year stability — an envelope thick- and thin-film cannot reach. The same foil is the sensing element in the strain gauges. The foil is the moat, and it is vertical: VPG makes the material, not just the part. That matters because the applications are mission-critical and low cost-of-failure — a resistor in ATE pin electronics, a strain gauge in a flight-test bridge, a load cell in a rolling mill. Customers optimise for a measurement they can trust for a decade.

But the moat is narrow in dollars. The 10-K concedes: "Competition in the markets where we sell the bulk of our products is extremely fragmented… there are no competitors with the same product mix and proprietary technology as ours" — which is another way of saying no one else wants the whole portfolio, and every niche has a specialist.

Customer concentration is genuinely low: no single customer exceeds 10% of net revenue (10-K). This is a material advantage over most small-cap sensor names — there is no single-programme cliff.

Honest assessment. The moat protects the gross margin (38–42%), not the operating margin. Design-in cycles and proprietary foil are strong; scale and pricing power are weak ($307M revenue vs HBK, TE, Sensata; a price-increase programme only now being installed, and management concedes it cannot reprice backlog). The corporate cost structure — $35M of unallocated G&A — is sized for a much larger company, and that, not competition, suppresses returns.

What the numbers say

  1. Revenue has not recovered. FY2025 was 13.6% below FY2023 and TTM $333.9M is still 6% below the FY2023 peak; 11.6% TTM growth is a recovery off a depressed base, not a trend line.
  2. Gross margin is leaking: 42.3% → 41.0% → 38.9% → 39.3% (9M FY2026 adjusted). Mix (Weighing dilution), FX and un-recovered manufacturing inflation. The company's own target is 46.5% — a 700bp gap requiring mix shift, not just volume.
  3. Fixed costs are the whole story. SG&A $106.8M → $107.5M → $109.6M against revenue that fell 13%; unallocated G&A $33.5M → $35.4M; 9M FY2026 unallocated G&A $31.1M, +16% YoY, funding the new CBPO/COO organisations. Management's "~$5M annual incremental cost" understates the run-rate rise.
  4. FX is recurring, not one-off. Unfavourable rates cut operating profit by $3.4M YoY in Q3 and $8.1M in 9M FY2026, against 9M operating income of $2.8M. Strip FX out and the business is meaningfully more profitable — but the headwind has persisted six quarters and belongs in the base case.
  5. Tax is a mess. 9M effective rate 246.7% on a small pre-tax base; management guides a ~45% operational rate for FY2026. Q3's 48% rate alone cost $0.03 of EPS.
  6. Cash conversion is poor at the trough: TTM FCF $1.5M; inventory +$7.4M and receivables +$7.5M absorbed 9M operating cash. Q3's $5.5M is one quarter, not a trend.

14. What would change my mind

Bullish (would move me to Buy): 1. Two consecutive quarters of ≥42% adjusted gross margin and ≥8% adjusted operating margin in H1 FY2027, attributed to volume and price rather than FX. That proves the operating leverage is mechanical — and at 8% operating margin the stock is worth ~$65–70 on 14x EBITDA. 2. A humanoid purchase order above $5M annually, with disclosed unit volumes, from customer #1 or #2. The only evidence that would justify more than 6% purity in 2029. 3. Q1 FY2027 revenue above $95M against the "$90M or better" guide, with lead times shortening — proof the capacity spend is working. 4. FY2027 guidance of +10–12% with an explicit margin bridge to the 15% target, plus a named CFO with public-company experience. 5. A price near $40 (≈1.4x EV/sales, ≈15x 2027E EBITDA) — the January 2026 level — which would make risk/reward asymmetric without needing the narrative.

Bearish (would move me to Sell): 1. A fourth humanoid schedule slip, or disclosure that customer #1 has dual-sourced the sensor content — the nomination letter would then be revealed as a prototyping award. 2. Sensors orders below $40M in any quarter, breaking the eight-quarter book-to-bill streak and signalling the AI-infrastructure pulse has peaked. 3. FY2027 revenue guidance below +8%, or a Q1 FY2027 print below $85M. 4. Adjusted gross margin below 38% for two consecutive quarters with FX blamed again. 5. A large stock-funded or revolver-funded acquisition while the equity is down 66% and the CFO seat is empty.
