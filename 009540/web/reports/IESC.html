1. The setup

  • A 55% three-month collapse in a business that is still growing 40%. IESC is down 55% in three months and 57% from its high, yet Q3 2026 revenue was $1.243B (+39.6% YoY) and EPS $3.79 (+99%). The market is de-rating electrical/mechanical contractors exposed to data-center capex, not reacting to the results.
  • The financials are excellent: TTM revenue $3.99B (+18.2%), net income $454M, EPS $11.26, FCF $228M, ROIC 41%, ROE 44.5%, net cash $311M. The company has grown revenue at a 30% three-year CAGR with minimal dilution.
  • The business mix: IES provides electrical and mechanical contracting, communications/technology infrastructure and residential services. Data centers and industrial projects are the growth engine; residential is the cyclical buffer.
  • Valuation has reset to 13.5x forward earnings from over 25x, with net cash and a 3.4% FCF yield. For a business with 40% growth and 40% ROIC, that is cheap unless the cycle is over.
  • The risk is the same as FIX/STRL/VRT: data-center construction is a capex cycle, and the market is pricing a peak. IESC's 55% drawdown is the market's recession call.
  • Sentiment: far below the 50-DMA ($269) and 200-DMA ($293); short interest 3.8%; no dividend. A single quarter of stable bookings would likely trigger a sharp rebound.

2. Business and moat

IES Holdings operates through regional electrical/mechanical contractors (IES Commercial, IES Residential, IES Communications). It designs and installs electrical systems, data/communications infrastructure, HVAC and plumbing for data centers, warehouses, hospitals, schools and homes.

The moat is local labour density, licensed electricians, and long-standing relationships with general contractors and hyperscalers. The business is asset-light and cash-generative, with customer prepayments funding working capital.
