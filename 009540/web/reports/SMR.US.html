NuScale Power Corporation (NYSE: SMR) — Equity Research Note

Price: $4.66 (NYSE close, 13 Nov 2026) · Market cap: $2.00B · EV: $1.08B · Revenue TTM: $2.7M · FCF: -$608M · Net cash: $927M Verdict: Bearish (Medium conviction) · 12-month probability-weighted value: ~$3.8 (-18%) Screening note; data as of 13-Nov-2026. Research, not investment advice.

1. The setup

  • The first NRC-approved SMR design, now a broken stock. SMR is 71% down YTD and 91% below its 5-year high. The market cap is $2.0B but the enterprise value is only $1.08B because the company holds $933M of cash against $6.6M of debt.
  • Revenue has collapsed. TTM revenue is $2.7M versus $31.5M in FY2025; Q3 2026 revenue was $0.28M versus $8.24M a year earlier. The collapse reflects the wind-down of legacy engineering work and the absence of new module orders.
  • Cash burn is the clock: TTM FCF -$608M against $927M of net cash — roughly 18 months of runway. Share count is up 38.9% YoY; another raise is likely before the first order converts.
  • There are real commercial milestones: the first reactor module moved into production (Nov-2026), a TVA deal is in discussion, and the RoPower (Romania) project remains the lead international deployment. But announcements are not revenue.
  • A securities-fraud class action has been filed, adding legal overhang to an already fragile narrative.
  • Positioning is extreme: short interest 21.3% of shares out and 22.7% of float, beta 2.4, price far below the 50-DMA ($7.01) and 200-DMA ($10.13). The stock is a high-beta bet on nuclear sentiment.

2. Business and moat

NuScale designs the NuScale Power Module (77 MWe light-water SMR), the first design to receive NRC certification. Revenue comes from engineering services, licensing and project development; the long-term model is selling modules and providing fuel/services through ENTRA1, its commercialization partner.

The moat is regulatory: NRC approval is a multi-year, capital-intensive barrier that few competitors have cleared. The weaknesses are commercial: no serial orders, a customer base that depends on government support, and a cost structure that consumes cash faster than projects develop.

5. Catalysts

  • TVA deal conversion into a firm module order — the single most important event.
  • RoPower/ENTRA1 milestones and DOE funding.
  • Q4 2026 results (Feb 2027): cash burn and backlog.
  • Class-action developments and any disclosure remediation.
  • First module production progress — a positive but not yet monetised.
