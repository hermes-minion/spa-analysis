0. Classification (mandatory)

Profile B — profitable but cyclical. TTM revenue $808.8M, non-GAAP gross margin 70.9%, non-GAAP operating margin 41.1% and Q3 FCF $77.6M; net debt $797M after the AMI acquisition. The communications/industrial cycle drives the P&L; trailing growth is above trend and should not be capitalised as structural. Business model: fabless FPGA and low-power programmable logic, sold into communications, industrial, automotive and data centre. Metrics that matter: EV/sales, non-GAAP P/E, EV/FCF, inventory days and book-to-bill.

1. Executive summary

  • The re-rating is earnings-driven, not narrative-driven. Q3 FY2026 revenue $291.0M, +118% YoY / +45% QoQ; Q4 guided to ~$335M (+130%); FY2026 ~$1.0B (+90%) with non-GAAP EPS ~$2.50. Management guides ≥60% growth in FY2027 (~$1.6B) and >30% CAGR for five years, citing backlog into 2028 (Q3 release, 2 Nov 2026; Q3 call).
  • The engine is the AI data-centre control plane, not robots. Compute & Communications was 59.1% of Q3 revenue, +132% YoY; newly acquired AMI (closed 27 Jul 2026) added 13.4%; Industrial & Embedded — the only physical-AI segment — was 27.5%, +35% (10-Q, 3 Nov 2026). I put genuine physical-AI revenue at ~18–20% today (18% in Q3 FY2026; range 15–21%), rising to ~28% by FY2029.
  • AMI changed the shape and the balance sheet. $1.59B cash-and-stock ($582.8M stock, $733.5M cash, $272.2M AMI debt refinanced), funded by a $925M term loan at 5.76%. Net cash of +$92M became net debt of −$797M; goodwill $1,369M; tangible book now negative (−$512M, −$3.60/share).
  • Margins are elite non-GAAP and thin GAAP. Q3 non-GAAP gross margin 70.9%, operating margin 41.1%, adjusted EBITDA margin 45.0% — versus GAAP operating margin 8.2% and GAAP EPS $0.08 against non-GAAP $0.72, because SBC was 17.3% of revenue and AMI purchase accounting added $12.9M of amortisation.
  • Valuation discounts several good years. 20.6x TTM EV/Sales, 16.9x FY2026E, 10.5x FY2027E; 44.9x FY2026E and 26.0x FY2027E EPS ($4.32); 69x TTM EV/FCF. The stock is 28% below its $157.01 high but +71% over 12 months.
  • Insiders sold the rally; the CEO bought the dip. ~$5M of officer/director sales at $105–$155 between May and September; CEO Ford Tamer bought 9,790 shares at $101.78–$103.40 (~$1.0M) on 4 Nov 2026 — the only open-market purchase in six months.
  • Consensus is uniformly bullish and stale on FY2026. 10 Strong Buy / 3 Buy / 1 Sell, average PT $156.19 (median $160, range $95.44–$200). Consensus FY2026 EPS of $2.14 lags the company-implied $2.50.

Verdict: NEUTRAL — conviction Medium. Probability-weighted fair value ~$122 (+8%). An excellent franchise in its best year ever, now priced as an AI-infrastructure compounder with no cyclical interruption. I would own it 25–30% lower, or on evidence the industrial/physical-AI leg is durable rather than a restock.

2. Business overview

Lattice sells low-power, small-form-factor programmable logic and, since July 2026, platform firmware. The pitch is not raw compute — it is instant-on, deterministic, sub-watt control and connectivity next to sensors, actuators and main processors.

3. Physical AI exposure

Where it sits in the stack

Lattice is a component supplier at the sensing–compute boundary, plus an emerging control-plane software vendor. It sells no perception software, systems or robots.

SENSING COMPUTE / SILICON DATA SOFTWARE/PERCEPTION SYSTEMS Camera/radar/ Small & mid FPGA None owned. Radiant, sensAI, None. Sells lidar/encoders (fabric, I/O, DSP), No data asset. Sentry, Automate, chips and → Lattice bridges hard PCIe/SerDes, mVision, Drive, firmware into and fuses the integrated flash, Prompt; AMI OEM systems. sensor streams hardware root of trust. BIOS/BMC.

Lattice makes the nervous system, not the brain: determinism (microsecond latency), instant-on configuration, sub-watt power, small package, hardware root of trust. Those properties matter more in a robot joint, a vision camera or an AI rack's boot path than raw TOPS — and they win sockets a GPU cannot economically serve.

4. Competitive position & moat

Lattice does not disclose share. The working structure is that AMD (Xilinx) and Altera hold roughly 80% of the total FPGA market, Microchip most of the remainder, and Lattice a high-single-digit share — but Lattice is the clear leader in the sub-$10 small-FPGA niche where power, instant-on and package size beat logic density. Management's "gaining share in small FPGA" claim is consistent with 90% FPGA revenue growth against a mid-single-digit market (claim, unverified).

Source of advantage Strength Why
Low-power / small-form-factor silicon Strong Nexus with integrated flash and hardened functions is hard to match at the same power and package; AMD/Altera optimise density, not milliwatts
Software and solution stacks Medium-strong Radiant, sensAI, Sentry, Automate, mVision, Drive and Prompt lower the RTL barrier and raise switching costs
Design-win inertia Strong 5–10 year production lives; AEC-Q100 / ISO 26262 re-qualification is prohibitive for a $3 part

Company-specific questions. (a) Inventory: the FY2023–24 correction is over — FY2024 revenue −31%, FY2025 +2.7%, Q3 I&E +35% with ~70% guided for Q4. Channel inventory is deliberately lean; Lattice is building its own ($120.6M vs $89.2M at FY2025 year-end) — right if demand holds, a write-down risk if not. (b) Edge AI: sensAI, mVision, Drive, plus the NXP i.MX 95 + Nexus PCIe companion solution (10 Nov 2026). (c) Mid-range: Avant is a 2028 story; new products were >70% YoY and ~25% of FPGA revenue in 2026. (d) Premium valuation: §7.

What the numbers say

  1. Fastest growth in the company's history. Revenue roughly doubled in six quarters ($145.8M in Q4 FY2025 to a guided ~$335M in Q4 FY2026). Nothing in the prior decade resembles it.
  2. Gross margin rose through the ramp (68–69% to 70.9% non-GAAP) despite AMI's lower-margin mix and expedite fees — pricing is firm and content per socket is rising.
  3. Operating leverage is the story. Non-GAAP operating margin 26.2% → 41.1% in six quarters on ~60% opex growth against ~140% revenue growth; adjusted EBITDA margin 45%.
  4. GAAP earnings are a different company. Q3 GAAP operating income $24.0M (8.2%) and EPS $0.08 versus non-GAAP $0.72: SBC ($50.3M), acquired-intangible amortisation ($12.9M) and $25.9M of acquisition/restructuring charges. SBC is $158M TTM against $44.7M of net income.
  5. Cash conversion is good, not yet exceptional. TTM FCF $242.6M (30% margin), but Q3 FCF margin fell to 26.7% as capex stepped up to $17.4M and working capital absorbed the ramp.

14. What would change my mind

Bullish (would move me to Buy): 1. FY2027 guided above 60% with Q1 and Q2 both beating, and evidence C&C is broadening beyond two or three hyperscalers (named new customers or a design-win count). 2. I&E sustaining >50% YoY for two more quarters with channel inventory flat — that would make the industrial/physical-AI leg a second engine and lift my purity estimate toward 25%. 3. A named humanoid, AMR or defence programme with 2028 volume disclosed — the biggest single re-rating lever for the physical-AI narrative. 4. Net leverage below 1.0x by mid-2027 while still growing >40%, making the equity structurally cheaper than EV/Sales implies. 5. A 25–30% drawdown to ~$80–85 on unchanged estimates: ~8x FY2027 sales and ~19x EPS, where risk/reward flips decisively.

Bearish (would move me to Sell): 1. Any quarter with C&C growth below 40% YoY, or management describing bookings as "shortening" — the tell for AI capex digestion, and brutal for a 20x-sales stock. 2. I&E growth falling back below 20%, confirming the physical-AI leg is cyclical restock rather than secular. 3. A distributor loss or receivable problem (two distributors = 69% of revenue, 83% of AR). 4. AMI failing to exit FY2027 at a >$300M run-rate, or gross-margin dilution persisting past 2027 — the deal would look like an 8x-sales value destroyer. 5. Insider selling resuming at scale above $130 without matching purchases, or a suspended buyback to preserve cash.
