2. Company snapshot

LifeMD's revenue is shrinking while it cuts marketing and moves into branded GLP-1s. Q3 2026 missed its own guidance, and full-year guidance was cut for the second time this year (Q3 2026 release, SEC 8-K).

What it does: US virtual care plus its own pharmacy. A 50-state affiliated medical group, lab services, a nationwide pharmacy with home delivery and a patient care center cover 200+ conditions: primary care, men's health (RexMD), women's health, weight management and hormone therapy. It sold WorkSimpli in late 2025 and is now a pure telehealth and pharmacy company.

3. Step-by-step analysis plan

Work through these in order. The goal is one question: would you buy LFMD at about $2.20 today, knowing what you know now? Your $5.50 cost is sunk; it only matters for tax.

  1. Write down your original thesis. Note why you bought at $5.50: growth, GLP-1 upside, the pharmacy, a takeover? Mark each reason as still true, weakened or broken.
  2. Read the primary documents.
    • Q3 2026 8-K and the 10-Q: risk factors, liquidity, any going-concern wording
    • The 12 Nov earnings call transcript: what management said on Q4, enterprise and GLP-1 economics
    • The Avenue Capital term-loan filing: rate, maturity, covenants, and whether warrants are attached
  3. Check liquidity and runway.
    • Start: $21.9M cash + $12.5M loan = about $34.4M
    • Burn: Q3 net cash use was $3.2M; Q4 guidance is −$2M to −$4M adj. EBITDA, plus about $0.78M of preferred dividends a quarter
    • Answer you need: can they reach positive cash flow without selling new shares in 2027? An equity raise near $2 would be heavily dilutive
  4. Test unit economics.
    • Subscribers (349,700) versus marketing spend ($18.5M): is the base stable now that spend is lower?
    • Revenue per subscriber, renewal of the longer plans, cost per acquisition
    • Answer you need: does each new patient pay back its acquisition cost, and how fast?
  5. Separate margin quality from accounting. Branded GLP-1s booked at gross value lower the gross margin percentage. Track gross profit in dollars ($36.4M in Q3) and contribution after marketing instead.
  6. Score guidance credibility. 2025 guidance was cut from $268–275M to $250–255M, then to $192–193M. 2026 guidance was affirmed in May, then cut in August and again in November. Treat Q4 ($46–49M) as the test: a hit rebuilds trust, a miss confirms the pattern.
  7. Map competition and regulation. Compounded GLP-1 sellers, other telehealth platforms, and drug makers' own direct-to-patient channels all compete for the same patients. Check what the Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly deals give LifeMD that rivals don't have.
  8. Review capital structure and governance. Preferred ranks ahead of you; insiders own about 18.4% (MarketBeat); class action and control weaknesses are open; there was an executive separation this year.
  9. Value it with scenarios. Use the bear, base and bull cases in section 5, and update them each quarter.
  10. Check trading and technicals. Volume, the $1.62 low, the 200-day average, and short interest around results.
  11. Check fit with your own portfolio. Position size as a percentage of your portfolio, what else the money could do, and your 2026 tax position (annex).
  12. Decide and set triggers. Pick a strategy from the annex, write down the triggers in section 5, and set calendar reviews.

What 250 shares changes in practice: one option contract is 100 shares, so the position covers two contracts with 50 shares stranded; a covered call at the $3 strike pays a few dollars of premium on a $2 stock, which does not cover the commission. Trim and repair are theoretically available, but at $549 of market value the ticket is too small to scale in and out — one decision (hold, or sell in a single order) is cheaper and clearer than a ladder of small trades.

Option strategies in practice: check LFMD's option chain in your broker first. At around $2 per share, premiums are small, spreads can be wide and some strikes may not trade. Use limit orders only.

Spanish IRPF points for this position

  • A loss on sale counts in the savings base (base del ahorro) and first offsets your capital gains of the same year. Any remaining loss can offset up to 25% of your positive investment income (dividends, interest), and the rest carries forward 4 years (Agencia Tributaria).
  • Repurchase rule (art. 33.5 f/g LIRPF): if you buy LFMD within the window before or after a loss sale, the loss is deferred until you sell those new shares (AEAT manual).
  • The window is 2 months for EU regulated markets and 1 year otherwise. For Nasdaq shares this has been disputed: some sources apply one year (OCU), while recent DGT rulings V0778-25 and V0951-25 are reported to apply two months to US markets with EU equivalence (AllInvestView). Read the rulings before relying on two months.
  • The loss is calculated in euros, using the exchange rate on each purchase date and on the sale date. Dollar moves since you bought change the size of the tax loss.
  • The harvestable loss here is $826 gross on 250 shares, not thousands: enough to absorb a small part of 2026 gains, and only usable in 2026 if the sale settles before 31 December.
  • To use the loss against 2026 gains, the sale must settle in 2026; leave a few days before 31 December.
