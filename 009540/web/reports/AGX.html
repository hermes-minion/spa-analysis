1. The setup

  • A cash-rich EPC contractor whose backlog is slipping. Argan builds natural-gas power plants and industrial facilities through Gemma Power Systems and related subsidiaries. Revenue is growing (TTM $1.19B, +25.8%), but the market is focused on the backlog trend — Zacks flagged "backlog slips further" in November.
  • The balance sheet is the standout: $1.03B cash, $11M debt, net cash $1.02B — 24% of the market cap. Ex-cash, the operating business trades at ~18x earnings.
  • Q2 FY2027 (Jul-2026) revenue was $384M (+61.5% YoY), EPS $3.76 — a strong quarter. TTM EPS is $12.66, and the stock is down 47% in three months as the power-plant cycle de-rated.
  • The business is lumpy: a handful of large EPC contracts drive revenue; backlog and award timing matter more than quarterly growth. The company has no debt and pays a small dividend ($0.50/quarter, 0.9% yield).
  • Valuation is not cheap despite the drawdown: 22.2x forward earnings and 17.7x EV/EBITDA for a cyclical contractor, even after netting cash. The market is paying for the balance sheet and the power-demand story.
  • Sentiment: below the 50-DMA ($362) and 200-DMA ($518); short interest 7.7%; the average target is $570. The stock is a sentiment-driven small-cap with a fortress balance sheet.

2. Business and moat

Argan's main subsidiary, Gemma Power Systems, provides engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) for gas-fired power plants, LNG terminals and industrial projects. Other subsidiaries provide telecom infrastructure and consulting.

The moat is EPC execution experience and a debt-free balance sheet that lets it bond large projects. The risk is contract concentration and the cyclicality of power-plant construction; a single delayed award can move revenue by 20%.
