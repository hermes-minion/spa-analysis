1. Executive summary

  • This is now a growth-again story trading at a growth-stock price. Revenue went $226M (FY2024) → $285M (FY2025) → $391M (FY2026, +37.2%), and TTM revenue is $417.8M (+20.0% YoY) as of the July 2026 quarter (statistics). FY2027 consensus is $441M (+12.9%) and guidance is "10–15%". At $60.12 the stock is 5.7x EV/TTM sales, 5.4x EV/FY2027E sales, ~75x FY2027E adjusted EPS and ~246x TTM FCF. That is a premium multiple for mid-teens growth.
  • The reported NXP process is the single largest swing factor in the equity. The FT reported on 31 Jul 2026 that NXP was in talks to buy Ambarella, with other bidders possible, when the market cap was ~$3.25B (SiliconANGLE, 31 Jul 2026). On 30 Oct 2026 several outlets reported Ambarella had moved into exclusive talks with NXP (The Fly/Seeking Alpha headline). No 8-K, no merger agreement and no company confirmation exists as of 13 Nov 2026 — I checked the EDGAR filing index. Treat as an unconfirmed media report.
  • The market is not pricing a deal. The shares are $60.12, 18% below the ~$73.6/share implied by the pre-rumour $3.25B market cap, and 38% below the 52-week high of $96.36. The stock fell 2.3% on the day the "exclusive talks" story ran, on 3.3M shares. If a deal were viewed as likely at a premium, the spread would not be this wide. OPINION: the market is either discounting the report or pricing a low-ball number.
  • "Physical AI" is a real but overstated label. Automotive is only ~22% of revenue (EE Times, 11 Nov 2026); IoT was ~80% of FY2026 revenue, split roughly 50/50 CapEx-driven (enterprise security, body cams, video conferencing) and consumer (action/360 cameras, drones, wearables) per the CFO (Q4 FY2026 call). The largest end customer in FY2026 was Arashi Vision (Insta360) — a consumer camera maker — and one distributor, WT Microelectronics, was ~70% of revenue (FY2026 10-K). Robotics + drones + industrial automation is, by my estimate, ~9% of revenue.
  • The income statement is still GAAP-loss-making and cash-consumptive. GAAP operating margin was −7.5% in Q2 FY2027 and −14.9% TTM; stock-based compensation is 21% of revenue; TTM free cash flow is $9.7M (2.3% margin) because inventory rose from 99 to 157 days and payables fell. Consensus FY2027 FCF is roughly zero to negative (forecast).
  • The product pipeline is genuinely interesting and is the reason to own it standalone. An $800M+ ten-year LTA with Hanwha, 15+ robotic design wins worth >$100M lifetime, a $13B automotive opportunity set for FY2027–32, the 2nm CV8 semi-custom SoC (production H1 FY2028), the X7 standalone AI accelerator, and a lift in the five-year SAM from $8.5B (FY2027) to $22.9B (FY2032), a 20% CAGR (Q2 FY2027 call; Q1 FY2027 call). All of these are management claims, not contracted revenue, except the Hanwha LTA which is contractual but back-end loaded.
  • Insiders are selling into strength and nobody is buying. In the six months to 13 Nov 2026 there were 20 open-market sales totalling $8.65M and zero open-market purchases; the CEO alone sold 6 tranches for $5.24M, including at $90.08–$92.05 in late May 2026 (Form 4s, CIK 1280263).

Verdict: NEUTRAL — conviction Medium. 12-month probability-weighted value ~$68 (+13%). The takeover process, the Hanwha LTA and the 2027–28 product cycle put a floor under the equity around $45–50; the 75x adjusted P/E, 21% SBC load, negative FCF and consumer-customer concentration cap the re-rating. I would turn constructive below ~$48 standalone, or on a confirmed deal at ≥$80.

2. Business overview

What they sell

Ambarella designs fabless, low-power AI systems-on-chip (SoCs) and the software stack that runs on them. The chip integrates the image signal processor, the CVflow neural-network accelerator, CPUs, video encoder and system functions on one die — the architectural bet being that at the edge, unlike in the data centre, "everything on one chip" beats "a collection of discrete parts" on power, latency and cost (Citi TMT conference, 9 Sep 2026).

Product family Node What it is Status (Nov 2026)
CV2 (CV22/CV25/CV28) 10nm Second-gen CVflow, CNN-era Still shipping in volume; the bulk of the ~$1B cumulative edge-AI revenue (management, Feb 2026)
CV5 / CV52 5nm AI vision SoC, 8K Mature ramp
CV7 / CV72 / CV75 5nm Third-gen CVflow, transformer-capable Steep production ramp; the main growth driver in FY2027
CV3-AD 5nm Central domain controller for L2+ to L4, sensor fusion + path planning Design wins with Continental, Bosch; L4 truck program with Aurora (management commentary) — no disclosed high-volume OEM win
N1 / N1-655 5nm Edge AI SoC running LLMs up to 34B parameters on-device In production; edge-infrastructure and robotics wedge
CV8 2nm Semi-custom AI SoC (first LTA product), Samsung Foundry Tape-out Jan 2026; production H1 FY2028
X7 n/a First standalone AI accelerator (co-processor for ARM/x86 hosts), 4–5W, ~4MB memory for LLM workloads Sampling now; design wins expected in edge infrastructure
Cooper — Unified SDK across the whole SoC portfolio (200+ model architectures in production) The real lock-in asset

Revenue is product revenue (SoC units), plus a small, undisclosed amount of NRE/development funding and software licensing. There is no SaaS line, no data business and no recurring software attach. Blended SoC ASP was ~$15 in Q1 FY2027 (management); ASP rises with AI performance because die size rises, not because pricing power improves.

To whom, and how they get paid

Ambarella sells to ODMs and OEMs, overwhelmingly through distribution. The channel structure is the most important and least-discussed fact in the file:

  • WT Microelectronics (Taiwan) = ~70% of FY2026 revenue, 63% in FY2025 and 53% in FY2024; 60.7% in Q1 FY2027 and 60.2% in Q2 FY2027 (10-K; earnings calls). WT is a non-exclusive logistics/fulfilment partner, not an end customer.
  • Hakuto (Japan) = 11% of Q2 FY2027 revenue.
  • Top 10 end customers ≈ 67% of FY2026 revenue. The largest end customer was Arashi Vision (Insta360), supplied indirectly through WT and several ODMs (FY2026 10-K).

Segments and geographic mix

Ambarella reports one segment — it collapsed the old IoT/Automotive disclosure in FY2026, which reduces transparency. End-market colour now comes only from earnings calls: IoT ~80% of FY2026 revenue; automotive ~22% (EE Times, Nov 2026). The mix is drifting up on commercial-vehicle telematics: management put IoT at "about three-quarters" in Q1 FY2027 and automotive at ~30% in September 2026, so the current run-rate is probably 25–30% automotive.

3. Physical AI exposure

Where it sits in the stack

SENSING COMPUTE/SILICON DATA SOFTWARE/PERCEPTION SYSTEMS/DEPLOYMENT Camera/radar CVflow SoCs (CV2→CV8) None (no data asset) Cooper SDK, AI ISP, Reference designs; (sold to, not by) + X7 accelerator customer-owned data ADAS/radar algorithms perception modules; fabbed at Samsung domain-controller BOMs

  • Sensing: Ambarella does not make sensors. It processes their output. It has adaptive-AI-radar software for 4D imaging radar but no radar chip.
  • Compute/silicon: this is the whole company. Single-foundry dependence on Samsung Foundry for leading edge — management says Ambarella is Samsung's only "exclusive" leading-edge customer, and Samsung publicly named NVIDIA and Ambarella as its 2nm customers (Q1 FY2027 call — management claim; I did not verify the Samsung release).
  • Data: none. This is the structural difference versus Mobileye's REM. Ambarella has no proprietary data asset, no fleet, no map. Its "data" story is a labelling/auto-annotation service sold to customers.
  • Software: Cooper SDK, AI ISP, ADAS/radar algorithms. Real and sticky, but it is an enablement layer, not a monetised stack — it exists to sell more silicon.
  • Systems: reference designs and perception modules; increasingly semi-custom ASICs (CV8) where the customer defines the spec.

Verdict on stack position: a compute-layer pure-play with software as a moat-enabler, not a revenue line. It is one layer, not four.

% of revenue tied to Physical AI

The company says "the Physical AI markets … represent a vast majority of our total revenue today" (Q2 FY2027 call). That is true only under a very loose definition. I separate three tiers:

Tier FY2027E revenue % Definition
Tier 1 — autonomy/action loop (robots, drones, AMRs, ADAS/L2+ domain controllers) ~$55M ~12% Perceives and acts; autonomous decision
Tier 2 — physical-world inference, human in the loop (enterprise/public security, body cams, telematics/ADAS cameras, industrial vision) ~$250M ~57% Perceives the physical world; a human or simple rule acts
Tier 3 — consumer imaging (Insta360-class action/360 cameras, consumer drones, wearables) ~$135M ~31% AI-assisted capture; not physical AI in any meaningful sense
Total ~$441M 100%

Broad Physical-AI purity (Tier 1 + Tier 2) ≈ 70% today. Strict autonomy-linked exposure ≈ 12%.

Reasoning for the split (my model, not company data): FY2026 IoT was ~80% of revenue and IoT splits "roughly 50/50" CapEx vs consumer (CFO, Feb 2026) → consumer ≈ 40% of total. I haircut that to ~31% for FY2027 because automotive and enterprise are growing faster than consumer. Automotive is 22% (EE Times). Robotics + drones + industrial: management disclosed 15+ robotic design wins with lifetime revenue >$100M and 30+ customers in the pipeline (Q1 FY2027); lifetime revenue of $100M spread over a typical 3–4 year life is $25–35M/yr, and drones added a full quarter of production revenue in Q4 FY2026. I use ~9% (~$40M) for robotics/drones/industrial, plus ~3% for the ADAS/autonomy slice of automotive.

Three years out (FY2030E): I model revenue ~$660M (~14.5% CAGR, in line with consensus' 14.95% three-year growth forecast). Mix shifts to roughly: security 27%, consumer 16%, automotive 28%, robotics/drones/industrial 20%, edge infrastructure 10%. That gives broad purity ~80–84% and strict autonomy-linked ~20%. The direction is right; the pace depends almost entirely on robotics, which is currently a $40M business with small, fragmented customers — the CEO himself calls it "autonomous driving seven years ago."

Scepticism check. Ambarella's "full-stack Physical AI platform provider" language (CEO, Sep 2026) is marketing layered on a genuine but narrower asset: a very good low-power vision SoC franchise with an unusually broad customer base (370+ unique customers, 50M+ units shipped — company figures). The Physical-AI narrative is being priced; the Physical-AI revenue is ~12% under a strict definition. Investors should underwrite the security-camera and consumer-camera business they actually have, and treat robotics/edge infrastructure as free options.

4. Competitive position & moat

Market position

  • ~50M cumulative AI SoCs shipped and 370+ unique customers with products in production (Q2 FY2027 release; Q4 FY2026 call).
  • 12–15 AI SoCs spanning ~1 TOPS to "hundreds of TOPS-like" performance, with a claim of up to 100B-parameter support in future parts (Citi, Sep 2026).
  • ~$1B cumulative edge-AI revenue, mostly CV2 (management, Feb 2026).
  • In security cameras, Ambarella is the reference premium supplier; in consumer action/360 cameras it is the incumbent in the high end (Insta360 is the largest end customer); in automotive it is a challenger, not an incumbent.

The moat: real at the low end, thin at the top

Source of advantage Strength Why
Performance per watt Strong The core, defensible claim. The CEO's framing — customers start on a GPU for generation one, then hit power/thermal walls and move to Ambarella — is credible and is corroborated by design-win patterns in battery-powered devices (body cams, drones, doorbells).
Cooper unified SDK Strong-ish One software stack across 12+ SoCs means a customer can port a product up or down the portfolio without a rewrite. This is a genuine switching cost and is why the semi-custom/LTA model works.
Video/ISP quality Strong 20 years of image-processing IP; AI ISP (neural denoise) is a real differentiator in low light.
Design-win stickiness Medium 12–18 months to revenue in IoT, 3–4 years in automotive OEM; once designed in, the SoC lives for the product's life. But IoT product life is only 12–24 months — the lock-in expires fast in the biggest segment.
Scale/cost Medium Fabless at ~$440M revenue; Samsung gives it leading-edge access that a sub-$500M company would not otherwise get, but it has no cost advantage over Qualcomm or Chinese vendors.
Pricing power Weak ASP rises only because die size rises ("it's not that we're going to increase our gross margin" — CEO). Gross margin has drifted from 62.7% (FY2022) to 58.5% TTM.
Data / network effects None No proprietary data asset.

Company-specific questions

(a) The NXP/M&A process. Reported 31 Jul 2026 (FT), with "other industry players" possible; Qualcomm reported interested 9 Oct 2026; a "third bidder" reported 16 Oct; NVIDIA reported as a possible third bidder 21 Oct; exclusive talks with NXP reported 30 Oct 2026. No 8-K, no confirmation, no disclosed price. The only EDGAR filing dated 30 Oct 2026 was a Vanguard Portfolio Management Schedule 13G/A (3,107,820 shares, ~7.0%) — a routine index-holder amendment, not a deal document. The one hard datapoint is the pre-rumour market cap of ~$3.25B (~$73.6/share), before a 16% pop. OPINION: a strategic buyer's logic is straightforward — 58% of NXP's semiconductor revenue is automotive (EE Times) and it needs camera-perception silicon plus an edge-AI software stack; Ambarella is the cheapest scaled merchant asset with Samsung 2nm access. A price of 6–7x FY2027 sales ($2.6–3.1B EV, $65–75/share) is the plausible zone, and the FT reported that bids would likely value Ambarella above its pre-rumour market cap.

(b) China exposure. Not disclosed as a percentage anymore. Chinese consumer brands (Insta360) are a large share of the consumer bucket, and WT's Taiwan billing obscures the true end-demand geography. Management says automotive demand is "all Western" and that China-for-China localisation is a structural headwind it cannot fix (Citi, Sep 2026).

(c) The LTAs are the most important new fact. The Hanwha agreement (>$800M over >10 years, covering physical security, operational automation, life sciences and robotics) is the first of its kind in edge-AI semis and reframes Ambarella from a spot-pricing SoC vendor to a multi-generation platform partner. If two or three more follow, the revenue durability argument changes materially. But: the revenue is spread over a decade, back-end loaded, and dependent on Hanwha winning in its own markets.

What the numbers say

  1. The turnaround is real, and it is decelerating. Revenue compounded 31% a year from FY2024 to FY2026, but growth has fallen from +63% (Q3 FY2025) to +13.2% (Q2 FY2027) as the easy comparisons roll off. Guidance for FY2027 is 10–15%; consensus +12.9%.
  2. Gross margin is leaking slowly and steadily (GAAP basis): 62.7% (FY2022) → 60.5% (FY2025) → 59.2% (FY2026) → 57.7% (Q2 FY2027). Non-GAAP tells the same story (62.7% → 60.7% → 59.3%). Mix (more automotive/telematics, more semi-custom), rising wafer and packaging costs and competitive pricing are the causes. Management's long-term target is 59–62% — i.e., the current level is the bottom of the range, not the middle.
  3. Operating leverage is arriving but from a deep hole. GAAP operating margin improved from −30.9% to −7.5% over eight quarters on roughly flat OpEx ($56–58M/qtr non-GAAP). Every incremental dollar of revenue now drops through at ~59% gross margin. That is the bull case in one sentence.
  4. The non-GAAP profit is entirely an SBC construct. Q2 FY2027 non-GAAP net income of $8.2M becomes a GAAP loss of $6.7M after $22.7M of SBC. At 21% of revenue, SBC is roughly equal to the entire non-GAAP operating profit plus change. On a fully loaded basis the company is still loss-making.
  5. Cash conversion has broken down. FY2026 FCF was a healthy $58M (14.8% margin). Then inventory went from 99 days (Jan 2026) to 145 (Apr 2026) to 157 (Jul 2026), and payables fell $26M — producing −$29.6M FCF in Q1 FY2027 and −$7.1M in Q2, and TTM FCF of just $9.7M. Management frames the inventory build as "to better service our customers in the face of a number of new product cycles." That may be true; it is also exactly what a company says before a demand air-pocket. This is the single number I would watch most closely on 24 November.
  6. Q2 FY2027 contained a $9.0M one-time credit. R&D fell to $50.6M from $59.7M because Ambarella released a deposit liability after an automotive autonomy customer cancelled a development project. Excluding it, the GAAP operating loss would have been ~$17M, not $8.1M. The non-GAAP numbers exclude it, correctly — but the headline "operating loss halved" is flattered.

  • Runway: not a solvency question. $260M of net cash against a ~$230M annual non-GAAP OpEx base and positive (if volatile) operating cash flow gives 3+ years of runway even in a severe downturn, and the company has no debt covenants.

Verdict: fortress-ish balance sheet, no financing risk, but the equity is being slowly diluted at 3%+ a year while free cash flow is roughly zero. Net cash covers 10% of the market cap — supportive, not transformative.

Reading the table. Ambarella carries the second-highest EV/sales in the peer set (after NVIDIA) and also the second-highest growth (after NVIDIA) — so the multiple is internally consistent rather than an anomaly. On growth-adjusted terms it sits mid-pack: EV/sales ÷ TTM growth = 0.28 for AMBA, 0.15 for NVDA, 0.54 for SYNA, 0.23 for INDI, 0.48 for MBLY. The problem is not the relative multiple. It is that AMBA is unprofitable on a GAAP basis with ~zero free cash flow, whereas QCOM earns a 20% FCF margin and NVDA ~42%. You are paying a growth multiple for revenue that does not yet convert into cash.

The strategic-comparable anchor is the most useful one. onsemi agreed to buy Synaptics for $5.7B all-cash (revised 1 Oct 2026; originally $7B all-stock on 25 Jun 2026) — roughly ~5.2x EV/sales (equity $5.7B plus ~$0.51B net debt) for $1.2B of revenue growing 8% at a 46% gross margin with negative operating income. Ambarella grows faster (13–20%) at a higher gross margin (59%). Applying 5–7x FY2027 sales gives $2.2–3.1B EV, or $55–75/share — bracketing today's price. The stock is already trading at a strategic-buyer multiple.

14. What would change my mind

Bullish signals (would move me to Buy): 1. A definitive merger agreement at ≥$78/share (≥6.9x FY2028 sales) with a credible regulatory path. That is the cleanest exit and would validate the strategic value of the franchise. 2. A second large LTA of ≥$500M over 5+ years with a Western OEM or Tier-1, proving Hanwha is a template rather than a one-off. This is the single most valuable non-M&A development. 3. Q3 FY2027 beat with a Q4 guide above $120M AND inventory days falling below 130. That would show the working-capital build was demand-driven, not a channel stuff, and would restore the FCF story. 4. A named high-volume L2+ automotive OEM win. Management has talked about this "wildcard" for years; a $200M+ lifetime design win at a Western OEM would move automotive from 22% to 30%+ of revenue and lift the mix. 5. Robotics/drones revenue disclosed above 15% of revenue with a named humanoid or AMR program in production.

Bearish signals (would move me to Sell): 1. NXP formally walks away (or the exclusivity lapses with no deal) — the stock loses its floor and re-tests $48. 2. FY2028 guidance below 10% growth or a Q4 FY2027 guide implying a sequential decline beyond normal seasonality. At 75x earnings there is no room for a growth scare. 3. Inventory days above 170 with another negative FCF quarter. Two consecutive years of cash burn in a "growth" story is a de-rating event. 4. Gross margin below 58% on a non-GAAP basis — that would break the 59–62% target and signal that semi-custom/automotive mix is structurally dilutive. 5. A large customer loss or a disclosed channel change at WT/Insta360, or evidence that Chinese SoC vendors are taking the enterprise-security socket.
