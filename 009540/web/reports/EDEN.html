1. The setup

  • A regulated-benefits compounder at a distressed multiple. Edenred is the global leader in employee benefits (Ticket Restaurant), fleet & mobility cards and complementary solutions. The stock is 59% below its 5-year high but +34% YTD as the market reassesses the French regulatory overhang.
  • The valuation is deep value: 11.2x forward earnings, 7.0x EV/EBITDA, 5.6x P/FCF, 5.2% dividend yield. For a business with 18.5% ROIC and ~30% operating margins, that is a clear discount to quality peers (Edenred's own history is 20-25x).
  • The numbers are stable: TTM revenue €2.75B (+0.8%), net income €500M, EPS €2.06, FCF €1.05B (38% margin). Growth is low single digit, but the cash conversion is exceptional.
  • The regulatory issue is the crux: France has been reforming the ticket-restaurant system (fee caps, digitalisation, competition), and similar pressures exist in Italy and Brazil. The market fears a structural margin hit; Edenred has so far absorbed it.
  • The balance sheet carries the float: cash €3.32B, debt €5.08B, net debt €1.76B. The debt funds the float/prepaid balances, so it is less risky than the raw number suggests; Altman 0.27 is an artefact of the financial-liabilities structure.
  • Sentiment: above the 200-DMA (€23.6) but below the 50-DMA (€27.3); the stock fell 13.5% in the last month. Average target €31.24.

2. Business and moat

Edenred issues and manages prepaid benefit and mobility solutions: meal vouchers, fuel/fleet cards, gift cards and corporate payments. Revenue comes from issuer fees, merchant commissions and float income. The model is a two-sided network: employers distribute benefits, employees spend them at affiliated merchants.

The moat is merchant acceptance (millions of outlets), employer relationships and regulatory licences. The risk is regulation: governments can cap fees or mandate interoperability, which compresses the take rate.

5. Catalysts

  • French ticket-restaurant reform implementation — clarity would remove the overhang.
