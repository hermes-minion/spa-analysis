Quantinuum Inc. (NASDAQ: QNT) — Equity Research Note

Price: $35.40 (Nasdaq close, 13 Nov 2026) · Market cap: $9.4B · EV: $7.5B · EV/Sales TTM: 256x · FCF: -$362M · Net cash: $1.90B Verdict: Bearish (Medium conviction) · 12-month probability-weighted value: ~$31 (-12%) Screening note; data as of 13-Nov-2026. Research, not investment advice.

1. The setup

  • The highest-quality pure-play quantum asset, at the highest price. Quantinuum (Honeywell + Cambridge Quantum) IPO'd on Nasdaq on 4-Jun-2026 at $60/share, raising $1.68B at a $15.7B valuation. The stock now trades at $35.40 — 41% below the IPO price and 57% below its post-IPO high — but still at 256x EV/sales.
  • The technology is the best-in-class trapped-ion platform: highest reported two-qubit gate fidelities, a clear path to logical qubits, and a hardware-plus-software stack (TKET, InQuanto, Quantum Origin). Honeywell remains the controlling shareholder, which provides credibility and a captive industrial customer base.
  • The financials are early-stage: TTM revenue $29.3M, roughly flat versus FY2025 ($30.9M) after a lumpy Q1-2025 that included a large one-off; FY2025 itself grew 34.6%. Gross margin is 46.7%, net loss -$135M, FCF -$362M. Q3 2026 revenue was $9.05M (+233% YoY) — a sharp acceleration, but off a tiny base.
  • The cash pile buys time: $1.94B of cash/investments and $32M of debt → ~5 years of runway at the current burn. That is the key difference versus IonQ (1.3 years).
  • The valuation is the problem, not the company: at $9.4B market cap and $7.5B EV, investors pay ~$7.5B for a business with $29M of revenue. Even assuming 60% annual growth for five years (to ~$300M) and a generous 25x sales, EV would be $7.5B — today's price already discounts flawless execution.
  • Technical picture: price below the 50-DMA ($42.45), no 200-DMA (listed <6 months), 2.2% of shares out short but 14.3% of float. IPO lock-up expiry is a dated overhang.

2. Business and moat

Quantinuum sells quantum computing hardware (H-series trapped-ion systems), cloud access, and software (TKET compiler, InQuanto chemistry, Quantum Origin cryptographic keys). Customers are governments, research labs, banks and industrials. Honeywell's backing gives it engineering discipline and a balance sheet.

The moat is the trapped-ion fidelity lead and the IP portfolio (Honeywell's ion-trap heritage + Cambridge Quantum's software). The risk is that quantum computing is a research race with deep-pocketed competitors (IBM, Google, IonQ, PsiQuantum, Microsoft) and an uncertain commercial timeline. No one has demonstrated a commercially useful, fault-tolerant quantum advantage at scale yet.
