0. Classification (mandatory)

Profile C — growth with declining cash burn. Q3 FY2026 revenue $42.8M, GAAP operating margin +0.9% (first positive quarter) and FCF −$11.8M; FY2025 FCF −$74.9M. Liquidity is $231M with no maturities before 2033. GAAP net income is not yet durable; valuation rests on EV/sales and the path to positive FCF. Business model: satellite-constellation operator plus analytics/software (Spectra, Assured), sold to government/defence and international sovereign customers. Metrics that matter: EV/sales, adjusted EBITDA, backlog, capex per satellite and FCF.

1. Executive summary

  • The business inflected in Q3 FY2026, and not by a little. Revenue $42.8M (+118% YoY, +29% QoQ), gross margin 78.8%, first positive GAAP operating income ($0.4M) in the quarterly record back to 2021, record adjusted EBITDA $12.8M (29.9%) and positive operating cash flow (+$3.6M) which management says contained no customer prepayments (Q3 release, 5 Nov 2026). FY2026 guidance raised for the second time to $140–150M revenue (+31–41%) and $18–24M adjusted EBITDA.
  • The mix has quietly become international-sovereign, not NRO. 9M FY2026: international governments $53.4M (55%), US federal $41.9M (43%), commercial $1.7M (2%) (10-Q) — versus 48%/49% a year earlier. Genuine diversification, but it swaps US intelligence budgets for a handful of foreign defence ministries, two of which are each 14% of revenue.
  • Backlog is the number that does not corroborate the growth story. $355.0M at 30 Sep 2026 versus $345.3M at 31 Dec 2025 and ~$390M at 31 Dec 2024 ("record", Q4 FY2024 call). Only $64.3M converts in FY2027 — 32% coverage of the $198M consensus, versus 53% for FY2026 ($77.3M of year-end backlog against the $145M now guided). MY OPINION: growth is real but short-cycle and must be re-won annually. That is a lower-quality model than a defence backlog business, and the market is pricing it as the latter.
  • "AI" is a feature, not a revenue line. The segment is now "space-based intelligence & AI services" ($73.8M, 76% of 9M revenue), but BlackSky discloses no imagery/analytics split and bundles analytics into subscription pricing (10-Q; Q3 call). MY ESTIMATE: ~20% of revenue is attributable to AI-differentiated analytics today (unverified), rising to ~30–35% by 2029. The rest is capacity, collection and satellite hardware — high margin, but a commodity input over time.
  • Dilution is the most under-priced part of the story. Shares +17.1% YoY to 41.4M. In 9M FY2026 the company sold 4.2M shares through its ATM at a $39.41 average — $165.0M gross against a $250M programme dated 22 May 2026 (10-Q; 8-K). Add a $185M 8.25% convertible struck at $36.78 and 1.43M in-the-money warrants at $17.61: fully diluted is ~48.1M shares, not 41M.
  • FCF breakeven is a 2028 event, not 2027. 9M FY2026 FCF −$48.9M on $46.5M capex; TTM −$70.8M. With $50–60M guided capex and ~$15.3M of convert interest, FCF breakeven needs ~$65–75M of adjusted EBITDA — ~$300–350M of revenue at 20–22% margins. This arithmetic, not the demand story, caps the multiple.
  • The $1B France/UAE consortium is the largest option and is unquantified. On 9 Sep 2026 BlackSky became exclusive very-high-resolution EO provider to a 50-satellite, $1B initiative led by Marlan Space, Loft Orbital and Mistral AI, announced in Paris with President Macron (release). FACT: only "a small initial contract to get started" has been signed; definitive agreements are pending and management declined to size it (Q3 call).
  • Cheap against a peer group that has gone vertical; expensive against its own cash flows. 7.6x TTM EV/sales, 6.9x FY2026E and 5.1x FY2027E, versus Planet Labs 12.3x and Rocket Lab 38.2x. BlackSky has the highest gross margin in the peer set (73.4% vs Planet 55.5%) and the worst FCF margin (−53.6%).

Verdict: NEUTRAL — conviction Medium. Base case $27.9 (+17%); probability-weighted $27.6 (+16%). The operating inflection is real and the constellation genuinely capital-efficient, but at 5x FY2027 sales with negative FCF, 17% dilution and thin backlog coverage the equity already discounts a successful 2027. I would need either the France/UAE agreement sized above $150M, or evidence that FCF breakeven is a 2027 event, to turn constructive.

3. Physical AI exposure

Where it sits in the stack

SENSING COMPUTE/SILICON DATA SOFTWARE/PERCEPTION SYSTEMS/DEPLOYMENT Own Gen-3 sats Own ground segment; Own archive + Spectra: tasking, Sovereign satellite 35cm EO + 1m SWIR on-orbit edge hourly revisit of multi-INT fusion, deliveries, ground (own design/build) compute; 3rd-party ~90% of world GDP automated vehicle/ segments, training launch aircraft/vessel detection

BlackSky occupies layers 1, 3 and 4, owns no silicon, and does not actuate anything physical. It is "physical AI" as machine perception of the real world sold by subscription — not physical AI in the robotics sense of closing a control loop.

Genuinely AI-shaped: automated detection (vehicles, aircraft, vessels) generated at the edge rather than by analysts (Nov 2026 release); autonomous tasking and tip-and-cue — the constellation schedules itself, and from 2028 AROS satellites "detect and hand off" to Gen-3 (10-K); and latency as product — first light <20 hours after launch, revenue operations within 24, customer live <12 hours after signature.

Reasoning. 100% of revenue touches physical-world sensing — there is no non-geospatial business to strip out, and that easy number is not the useful one. The useful number is how much is AI rather than imagery. Scepticism check. The "world's largest artificial intelligence infrastructure in space" language describes other people's AI — Mistral AI is the AI partner in the France/UAE consortium; BlackSky supplies optical payloads. In its own P&L, AI is an attachment rate on subscriptions, not a software line with software margins. The bull case is a data-and-capacity case wearing an AI costume.

4. Competitive position & moat

BlackSky is a niche player in a consolidating market: five Gen-3 satellites (fifth launched Sep 2026, first light <20 hours), two more before year-end 2026, an eighth in early 2027, targeting a "baseline Gen-3 constellation" complete in 2027 (Q3 call). Planet Labs operates ~200 satellites and ICEYE a SAR constellation. BlackSky's answer is that it does not need global daily coverage: it monitors "the most strategic, high-value locations... where approximately 90% of the world's GDP occurs" at up to 15 passes a day (10-K). That is a deliberate capital-efficiency trade: ~$15M/quarter of capex buys hourly revisit of a few thousand sites versus Planet's ~$93M TTM capex for daily global coverage.

Source of advantage Strength Why
Owned end-to-end stack (design → build → operate → Spectra → API) Medium-strong Gen-3 is designed and built in-house and commissioned in <24h. Vertical integration is why GM is 79% not 55%. Hard to copy quickly; not impossible.
US programme incumbency (NRO EOCL, NGA Luno A/B, Space Force) Medium Qualification barrier is real — but EOCL revenue declined in FY2025, so incumbency is not protecting price or volume.
Assured capacity product Medium Non-preemptible tasking is a genuine innovation for defence buyers who cannot queue behind a commercial customer. It is a contractual moat, and contractual moats erode once a rival has spare capacity.

Analytics / IP Weak Spectra is well-regarded, but there is no evidence of pricing power or switching costs. Customers buy capacity; software is the delivery mechanism.

MY OPINION: the moat is real at the margin and absent at the core. A 79% gross margin and 90% incremental contribution prove a genuinely efficient architecture — but they also prove that the marginal cost of one more image is near zero for anyone with a satellite overhead. The durable asset is the sovereign relationship and the ground/software integration, not the picture.

What the numbers say

  1. Revenue was flat for two years, then broke out. $94.5M → $102.1M → $106.6M → ~$145M guided (+36%). The 2024→25 stall was NRO mix; the 2026 breakout is Gen-3 capacity plus international Assured subscriptions. The growth is one year old.
  2. Operating leverage is genuine, not accounting. Space-based intelligence cost of sales was $4.9M in Q3 FY2026 versus $4.3M a year earlier on revenue up $17M — a >90% incremental margin, with cash opex up only 17% to $21.4M. This is the strongest fact in the bull case: the constellation is paid for and each incremental subscription dollar is nearly pure gross profit.
  3. Margins are flattered by depreciation rolling off. Gen-2 became fully depreciated in Q1 FY2026, so D&A fell to $7.5M from $7.9M despite revenue doubling (10-Q). That tailwind ends when the next Gen-3 tranche enters service.
  4. Q3 net income of $18.5M is not an earnings number — it includes a $19.7M non-cash derivative gain on warrant and Sponsor Share remeasurement. Strip it out and Q3 was roughly break-even; the honest metric is adjusted EBITDA of $12.8M.
  5. Revenue is brutally lumpy. Q1 FY2025 +22%, Q2 −11%, Q3 −13%, Q4 +16%, Q1 FY2026 −30%, Q2 +50%, Q3 +118%. Q1 FY2025's +22% was one $9.0M mission-solutions milestone; Q1 FY2026's −30% is that milestone not repeating. Any single quarter extrapolated either way will be wrong.
  6. SBC is modest by small-cap space standards — 11% of revenue in Q3, down from 18.6%, versus Mobileye at 20%. Partly because the equity is the compensation.

14. What would change my mind

Bullish signals (would move me to Buy): 1. France/UAE definitive agreement signed before mid-2027 and sized above $150M to BlackSky (≥15% of the $1B programme). That converts the largest option into backlog and proves the sovereign channel can land a programme, not just pilots. 2. FY2027 guidance ≥$195M with adjusted EBITDA ≥$40M and capex guided to ≤$45M at the Q4 FY2026 print (late Feb 2027). That combination puts FCF breakeven in FY2028 and justifies 8–9x sales. 3. Two consecutive quarters of positive free cash flow in 2027 — the cleanest signal that the constellation is funded and the business self-sustaining. 4. Backlog growing faster than revenue for two quarters, with FY2027-convertible backlog above $120M. That answers the book-to-bill objection directly. 5. An eight-figure (≥$10M) Assured contract with a new ministry of defence. Seven-figure wins are $1–3M; the model needs bigger units.

Bearish signals (would move me to Sell): 1. A further ATM draw of >$100M below $25, or any raise accompanied by a cut to FY2027 guidance. That confirms equity is the funding source of last resort. 2. France/UAE quietly descoped or pushed past 2027 — watch for the absence of an announcement by the Q1 FY2027 call and language drifting from "definitive agreements" to "continuing discussions". 3. FY2027 revenue guidance below $175M, or FY2027-convertible backlog below $60M. That breaks the growth narrative and leaves the stock at 4x sales with no FCF. 4. Mission Solutions gross margin below 40% for a full quarter, indicating a fixed-price overrun on the Q4 sovereign deliveries. 5. Loss of a Gen-3 satellite or a launch failure pushing the baseline constellation into 2028. At five satellites, single-asset risk is not theoretical.

Verification flags

  • The France/UAE value to BlackSky is unknown. The $1B is total consortium investment across 50 satellites with radar, optical and other sensors from multiple vendors; BlackSky is exclusive only for the very-high-resolution electro-optical portion. Management explicitly declined to size it. Any model capitalising a share of $1B is speculation.
  • AI-analytics revenue share (~20%) is my estimate, not a company figure. BlackSky reports one bundled segment and has never disclosed an imagery/analytics split.
  • FY2029 mix in §3 and all FY2027–28 figures in §12 are my models. No multi-year guidance exists; consensus FY2027 revenue is $198.1M and EPS −$0.28.
  • FY2023 adjusted EBITDA is n/v — not disclosed in the filings I read.
  • International customers are unnamed. The 10-Q identifies only "Customer B", "Customer C" with a "rest of world" tag. Country attribution is inference.
  • Book-to-bill (~1.1x for 9M FY2026) is my derivation from Δbacklog + revenue, not a reported metric; the company warns backlog is "subject to large variations from quarter to quarter."
  • Q3 FY2025's $19.6M base is not comparable — it is the trough between Gen-2 end-of-life and Gen-3 capacity. Two-year stacked growth (Q3 FY2024 $22.6M → Q3 FY2026 $42.8M) is +90%, or ~38% CAGR; the +118% headline overstates underlying momentum.
  • Net debt of $7.1M excludes $18.3M of restricted cash; including it the company is $11.2M net cash. The difference matters only for EV precision.
