0. Classification (mandatory)

Profile A — profitable and mature. FY2026 revenue $43.12B (−2.6%), gross margin 53.1%, operating margin 20.1%, FCF $8.65B (20.1%) and net debt $7.43B (1.4x EBITDA). FY2026 GAAP EPS ($12.70) is inflated by a $5.7B tax benefit; use non-GAAP EPS ($10.54) and FY2027E ($9.59). Business model: fabless semiconductor platforms (QCT) plus patent licensing (QTL); data centre is an emerging third leg. Metrics that matter: P/E, EV/FCF, FCF yield, QTL margin and handset/auto mix.

1. Executive summary

  • FY2026 was the year the narrative broke and the rebuild started. Revenue fell 2.6% to $43,120M and non-GAAP EPS fell 12% to $10.54, driven by a 13% decline in QCT handsets to $24,072M and the loss of Apple modem content (Q4 FY26 release, 4 Nov 2026). GAAP EPS of $12.70 is not comparable — it contains a $5.7B income-tax benefit ($5.32/share) from releasing a valuation allowance booked in Q4 FY2025.
  • The two structural overhangs that capped the multiple are now resolved. In the September quarter Qualcomm renewed long-term QTL licences with Apple, Huawei and Transsion at unchanged royalty rates, and pushed the next major renewal cycle out to FY2030–FY2033 (10-K Note 2; Q4 call, 4 Nov 2026). Management states the Apple modem transition is "fully incorporated" in planning and that the June 2026 quarter was the trough for China Android.
  • Non-handset is inflecting faster than guided. QCT automotive hit $1,990M in Q4 (+89% YoY), a first $2B quarter, and management now guides to an ~$8B annualised run-rate exiting FY2026 — $1B above its own guidance of one quarter earlier. IoT was $1,888M (+4%), with the industrial/networking/robotics bucket +20%. Combined auto+IoT FY2026: $13,136M, 35% of QCT.
  • Data centre is now the swing factor, and it is being capitalised on trust. Management reiterates $5B FY2027 and $15B FY2029 data-centre revenue, >$650M in the December quarter, exiting FY2027 at a ~$7B run-rate, anchored by two hyperscaler custom-silicon customers (AWS, plus one) and an AWS agreement with a stated potential of up to $60B over ten years (Q4 call). This is a claim, not contracted backlog; only "healthy order coverage" is disclosed.
  • But the P&L gets worse before it gets better. Q1 FY2027 guidance is $10.4–11.2B revenue and $2.15–2.35 non-GAAP EPS, and consensus FY2027 EPS is $9.59, down 9%, on revenue of $45.42B (forecast, 13 Nov 2026). Custom silicon is margin-dilutive by design; management now frames the baseline gross margin as 48–50%, not the 55–56% of FY2024–25.
  • Physical-AI purity is low and, counter-intuitively, falling. I estimate ~10% of FY2026 revenue (≈$4.7B) is genuinely physical AI — ADAS/AD content plus robotics and industrial-automation compute. That rises to ~17% (~$11B) by FY2029, but data centre (0% physical AI) supplies more than half of the $27B of non-handset growth. Qualcomm is a first-rate enabler of physical AI attached to a handset-and-licensing annuity, not a physical-AI pure-play.
  • Valuation is fair, not cheap. At $172.47 the stock is 13.6x trailing GAAP EPS, 18.0x FY2027E EPS, 21.3x P/FCF and 4.45x EV/sales — mid-range against its own five-year history (EV/sales 3.1–5.0x) and more expensive than NVIDIA on forward earnings despite negative growth.

Verdict: NEUTRAL — conviction Medium. Base-case 12-month fair value ~$190 (+10%). The licence renewals and the automotive run-rate de-risk the downside; the FY2027 EPS decline, 48–50% gross-margin framework and unproven $15B data-centre target cap the upside. I would turn constructive on evidence of data-centre gross margin, not data-centre revenue.

2. Business overview

What they sell

Qualcomm sells semiconductor platforms and patent licences. FY2026 revenue split: equipment and services $36,895M (86%), licensing $6,225M (14%) (10-K).

Other / reconciling $356M — — — QSI strategic investments; non-reportable Data Center, QGOV, Modular

Within QCT (FY2026): handsets $24,072M (−13%), automotive $6,004M (+52%), IoT $7,132M (+8%) (Q4 FY26 release). QTL is 13% of revenue but 43% of consolidated pre-tax income — the economic centre of gravity of the company.

To whom, and how they get paid

  • QCT is a fabless product business: TSMC, Samsung and GlobalFoundries for wafers; Micron and Samsung for memory; Amkor, ASE and SPIL for assembly/test. Qualcomm owns only RFFE/filter manufacturing (China, Germany, Singapore) (10-K). Economics are NRE-light and volume-heavy; in automotive the design-win → SOP cycle is 3–5 years with a 5–8 year production life.
  • QTL is a pure toll road: per-device royalties on 3G/4G/5G SEPs at ~72% EBT margin, no COGS to speak of. Licences with key OEMs now run to dates between FY2030 and FY2033 (10-K Note 2).
  • Data Center (non-reportable in FY2026, folded into QCT from Q1 FY2027): custom XPU/ASIC engagements, High Bandwidth Compute (HBC) accelerators, the Dragonfly C1000 Arm CPU, and Alphawave-derived optical/wired connectivity. Modular (acquired 28 Jul 2026 for $2.6B, paid in shares) contributes the Mojo language, MAX serving stack and Modular Cloud — positioned explicitly as "the portable alternative to NVIDIA's software stack."

Two customers/licensees each exceeded 10% of FY2026 revenue: 22% and 20%, with the 10-K naming Apple and Samsung as the ≥10% parties. Qualcomm's centre of mass is compute/silicon, moving into software (Modular) and systems (integrated modules, robotics reference designs). It owns almost no proprietary data asset — the one layer where Mobileye and Tesla have structural advantage. In automotive it is a merchant silicon + platform supplier competing with NVIDIA and Mobileye; in robotics it is selling a "brain-to-fingertip" reference design around IQ10 with >100 engagements including NEURA, Figure and KUKA, plus "several drone OEMs" (Investor Day 2026, 24 Jun 2026 — management claim).

Scepticism check. Qualcomm's Investor Day language — "own the solution, the silicon, the software and the stack for physical AI", a "$300B addressable market becoming $1T within a decade", "over 1 million robots deployed" — is marketing wrapped around a real but modest revenue base. The robotics business is, on my estimate, ~$0.5B a year: ~1% of revenue. Value it as an option, not a business.

Moat assessment

QTL is the real moat (9/10). $5.6B of revenue at 72% EBT margin, renewed with Apple, Huawei and Transsion at unchanged rates, no major renewals until FY2030–2033, and ~$100B of cumulative R&D behind the portfolio. The renewal is the single most important de-risking event of the last three years and the market has not fully repriced it.

QCT's moat is cyclical and design-win based (7/10). Automotive switching costs are genuinely high — safety qualification, 5–8 year programs, and an 8x content step from Gen 3 to Gen 5 create a revenue annuity. But in handsets the moat is one product cycle deep: Apple has already left, Samsung has its own Exynos, and MediaTek is a credible price/perf substitute every generation.

Data centre has no moat yet (3/10 as a franchise). Two customers, one of which (AWS) required at-warrant issuance — the only such structure management says it has. HBC is a real architectural idea (compute placed under a DRAM stack, ~0.3 pJ/bit in the lab vs a 0.5 target), but Broadcom owns the custom-ASIC relationship layer and NVIDIA owns the software layer. Modular is the hedge, and it is pre-revenue at scale.

Arm litigation remains live: the jury found Qualcomm did not breach the Nuvia ALA in Dec 2024 and the court entered judgment in Qualcomm's favour in Sep 2025, but Arm appealed and the Third Circuit heard argument on 21 Oct 2026 with no ruling yet (10-K). An adverse outcome would touch the Oryon CPU roadmap that underpins AI PCs, automotive and the C1000 server chip.

What the numbers actually say

  1. The mix is improving; the P&L is deteriorating. Automotive went from $2.9B (FY24) to $6.0B, and IoT held at $7.1B, yet total revenue fell because handsets dropped $3.7B. Non-handset carries structurally lower gross margin than flagship Snapdragon, and data-centre custom silicon is explicitly gross-margin dilutive, operating-margin accretive.
  2. Gross margin has broken its range. 55–56% for eight straight quarters through Q4 FY2025; 50.8% in Q4 FY2026. Management's new framing: price increases restore the baseline to 48–50% "at the low end of that range", with data centre taking it lower before the full benefit lands in H2 FY2027.
  3. FCF fell 33% to $8.65B — operating cash flow $11.1B (−20%) plus capex doubling to $2,494M. Inventory rose $2.1B to $8,640M, reflecting memory cost inflation and data-centre build.
  4. Capital returns exceeded free cash flow: buybacks $7,817M + dividends $3,854M = $11,671M, or 135% of FCF, funded by drawing cash from $10.2B to $7.7B and, in November 2026, by new debt.

6. Balance sheet & dilution risk

FACTS (27 Sep 2026, 10-K): cash and equivalents $4,636M + marketable securities $3,045M = $7,681M; short-term debt $2,492M + long-term debt $12,620M = $15,112M; net debt $7,431M. Debt is 100% unsecured fixed-rate notes ($15.1B principal, $2.0B maturing in 2027, remainder 2028–2053) plus $499M of commercial paper at 3.84%. Undrawn $4.0B revolver to Aug 2029. Current ratio 1.86; debt/EBITDA ~1.4x; Altman Z-score 5.58.

Post-balance-sheet event (12 Nov 2026): Qualcomm priced $4.0B of new notes — $750M 5.000% 2028, $750M 5.150% 2029, $1,000M 5.350% 2031, $750M 5.600% 2033, $750M 5.800% 2036 (8-K, 13 Nov 2026). Pro-forma net debt ≈$11.4B, roughly 1.8x trailing EBITDA. Not alarming, but the direction of travel has changed: Qualcomm is now borrowing to fund data-centre investment and buybacks.

Dilution — the important nuance. Headline buybacks look enormous: 48M shares repurchased for $7,817M in FY2026 (average ~$163/share) on top of $8,791M in FY2025. Yet shares outstanding went 1,074M → 1,072M — a net retirement of two million shares. The reason is stock-funded M&A: 11M shares for Alphawave ($1.8B of the $2.3B price), 18M shares for Modular ($2.6B) and 4M exchangeable shares consumed almost the entire repurchase. Weighted-average diluted shares did fall 2.9% (1,105M → 1,073M), which is the number that flatters EPS. Read-through: on a net basis $7.8B bought 2M shares. The buyback is real capital return, but it is not shrinking the equity base.

SBC is rising, not falling: $2,648M → $2,783M → $3,452M, i.e. 8.0% of revenue in FY2026 vs 6.3% in FY2025. Management attributes $428M of the increase partly to replacing the FY2026–27 annual cash incentive with a two-year equity award for non-executive leadership. GAAP-to-non-GAAP EPS gap in Q4 FY2026 was $1.00/share, of which $0.99 was SBC.

Runway: not a solvency question. $7.7B cash, $4.0B undrawn revolver, $8.7B annual FCF, and $19.6B of remaining buyback authorisation (10-K). The question is allocation quality, not liquidity.

14. What would change my mind

To turn bullish: 1. QCT gross margin ≥51% in the March or June 2027 quarter, with management attributing it to price rather than mix. This would prove the 48–50% framework is a floor, not a new normal, and would put FY2028 EPS above $12. 2. Data-centre revenue ≥$1.5B in a quarter during FY2027 with a disclosed gross margin, plus a third named hyperscaler. Two customers is a project; three is a franchise. 3. Automotive ≥$2.3B in a quarter (i.e. >$9B run-rate) with the $65B design-win pipeline converting on schedule — evidence the auto annuity can carry the multiple while DC matures.

To turn bearish: 4. Q1 FY2027 data-centre revenue below $500M, QCT EBT margin below 18%, or handset revenue below $4.5B with management blaming price elasticity rather than memory. Any of the three would break the FY2029 $40B non-handset architecture. 5. An adverse Third Circuit ruling in Arm v. Qualcomm, or a new licence dispute with a major Chinese OEM. Either would re-open the two risks the market just finished pricing out.
