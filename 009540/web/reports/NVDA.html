1. The setup

  • A governance shock meets a cheap multiple. On 12-Nov-2026 Jensen Huang resigned as President, CEO and director after reports of extramarital affairs, including with a woman US officials suspect of spying for China. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO; Harvey Jones is the first-ever board chair. The stock is down 26% in a month and 32% from its high.
  • The operating business is still extraordinary: TTM revenue $303B (+40% vs FY2026), net income $192.9B (63.7% net margin), FCF $127B (41.9%), ROIC 92%. Q2 FY2027 revenue was $96.2B (+106% YoY), EPS $2.46.
  • The valuation has compressed to 13.5x forward EPS — the cheapest NVDA has been since the AI cycle began — because the market is pricing both the leadership shock and a peak in AI capex.
  • The Q3 FY2027 print is on 17-Nov-2026, three days after this note. It is the single largest near-term binary: guidance for the January quarter and any comment on the CEO transition will set the tone for the whole AI complex.
  • The franchise is intact: CUDA, the GPU roadmap (Blackwell/Rubin), networking (NVLink, Spectrum-X) and the installed base. The bear case is not that Nvidia loses the technology lead; it is that AI capex normalises and the multiple de-rates further.
  • Balance sheet: $62.5B cash, $38.9B debt, net cash $23.6B; Altman 34.9. Buybacks reduced shares 1.0% YoY.
  • Sentiment: below the 50-DMA ($215) and 200-DMA ($204); short interest only 1.2%; the average target is $283. This is a fallen consensus long, not a broken company.

2. Business and moat

Nvidia designs GPUs, networking and AI systems (DGX/HGX) and the CUDA software stack. Revenue comes from data-center AI (the vast majority), gaming, professional visualization and automotive. The moat is the CUDA ecosystem, the cadence of new architectures, and the systems-level integration (GPU+NVLink+networking+software).

The risks are (1) customer concentration in a few hyperscalers, (2) export controls on China, (3) custom ASICs (Broadcom/Marvell) taking share, and (4) the possibility that AI training demand peaks.

5. Catalysts
