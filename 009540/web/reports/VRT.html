1. The setup

  • The purest listed play on data-center power and cooling — and it has just been cut in half. VRT is 46% below its 5-year high and 31% lower in three months, despite Q3 2026 revenue of $4.31B (+61% YoY) and EPS of $1.95 (+91%). The AI-infrastructure de-rating hit the whole chain; Vertiv's organic growth "softness" (vs acquired growth) gave the bears a specific gripe.
  • The business is compounding at an extraordinary rate. TTM revenue $13.12B (from $8.01B two years ago), net income $2.10B, FCF $3.15B (24% margin), ROIC 43.8%, ROE 46.6%. Backlog and orders are tied to hyperscaler and colocation build-outs.
  • Valuation is still not cheap. At 21.7x forward EPS and 24x EV/EBITDA, VRT trades at a premium to industrial peers — justified by growth, but with little room for a demand air-pocket. The 46% drawdown has reset sentiment, not the multiple to bargain levels.
  • The product mix is the moat: thermal management (liquid cooling, CDUs, chillers), power distribution (busway, UPS, switchgear), IT management and services. Liquid cooling is the fastest-growing piece as GPU racks move from 10kW to 100kW+.
  • Balance sheet is clean: cash $3.71B, debt $3.36B, net cash $355M. Share count +0.8% YoY (modest dilution). Altman 6.1, Piotroski 5.
  • Sentiment: below the 50-DMA ($231) and 200-DMA ($272); short interest 2.9%; HSBC lists VRT among "buy the dip" names; Samsung is reportedly shopping for a US data-center cooling asset — strategic interest in the space remains high.

2. Business and moat

Vertiv sells the plumbing and electrical backbone of data centers: UPS, power distribution, busway, thermal management (including liquid cooling), racks and monitoring software, plus a global service network. Customers are hyperscalers, colocation providers (Equinix, Digital Realty) and enterprises.

The moat is (1) engineering and qualification — data-center power/cooling is mission-critical and slow to qualify; (2) installed base and service — recurring maintenance and retrofits; (3) scale in liquid cooling — Vertiv acquired CoolIT and has the broadest CDU/liquid-cooling portfolio. The risk is that the market is cyclical and concentrated: a handful of hyperscalers drive orders, and competitors (Schneider, Eaton, nVent, Asian suppliers) are investing heavily.

5. Catalysts

  • Q4 2026 results (Feb 2027): orders/backlog and organic growth are the key metrics; the "organic softness" critique needs to be answered.
  • Liquid-cooling wins at NVIDIA/AMD next-gen platforms (GB300/Rubin-class racks).
  • Hyperscaler capex guidance: the single biggest swing factor.
  • M&A: Samsung's reported interest in a US cooling asset signals strategic demand; Vertiv is more likely a buyer than a target.
