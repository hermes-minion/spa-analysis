1.2 The SMR case in five lines

NuScale owns the only two NRC-approved SMR designs in existence — a real moat — and nobody has filed to build one with it (pre-application only; the strongest competitor's design, GE Vernova's BWRX-300, just received a construction permit at TVA in 14 months). Its flagship RoPower project is stalled on risk-sharing terms four days before this writing (Romanian press: >80% of conditional-FID requirements unmet). Its cash pile ($1.89B) makes it solvent for years, but ~27% share growth in nine months and a $750M ATM armed at $6 make dilution the dominant near-term variable; and its commercialization agreement pays ENTRA1 up to ~$3.4B across future milestones for orders not yet placed. At $5.95 the stock sits between our base and bull cases: not obviously cheap, not obviously broken — a coin-toss that pays ~+14% in the bull and −55% in the bear. Requires: a US COLA filing and/or RoPower financial close to get interesting; avoidable below ~$4.5 if you want the asymmetry.

1.3 The OKLO case in five lines

Oklo has done something no peer can claim — built and started a reactor (Groves) in ~11 months — and raised the largest war chest in advanced nuclear ($3.0B), giving it ~5 years of runway. But its commercial fleet has no NRC application pending (the 2020 COLA was denied; the company is "evaluating the timing and form" of future applications), its customer stack is LOIs/frameworks (Meta's prepayment mechanism is the only executed instrument; zero binding PPAs), and its stock — even after −83% — still prices an enterprise value of ~$2.6B, equivalent to ~29 units (~2.1 GWe) of fully-operating capacity at our modeled economics, or ~5.5× our probability-weighted deployment value. The founders' monthly sales (two tranches without 10b5-1 cover) and three fully-sold ATMs at $97→$63.5→$44→$30 complete the picture. At $29.7 you are paying bull-case-plus prices for a story that has earned base-case respect. We would need ~$14–17 to underwrite it; the next raise price, not the next press release, is the stock's tell.

1.4 What different investment conclusions require

  • To buy SMR today: you must believe a US COLA is filed by 2027 and RoPower closes, and that NuScale nets ≥$40M/module after the ENTRA1 toll — then bull ($6–8.5) is reachable and entry at $5.95 leaves thin but real upside. Falsified by an empty docket and a dead Romanian project by mid-2027.

  • For both: this is a "wait for evidence or wait for a lower price" situation. The sector crash has removed the mania but not the premium: SMR trades ~1.7× our expected deployment value, OKLO ~5.5×. Patience is the position.

3.1 NuScale Power (NYSE: SMR)

What it is. A reactor-technology and licensing company: it designs the NuScale Power Module (NPM) — a light-water SMR, 77 MWe gross per module in the current "US460" configuration (6 modules = 462 MWe plant) — and monetizes it through technology licenses, engineering services, and future equipment supply, with modules manufactured by Doosan Enerbility in South Korea [VF, 10-Q]. It does not own power plants; it does not (currently) earn electricity revenue. Its exclusive commercialization partner is ENTRA1 Energy, a private company that leads project development and customer contracting (see §5 for the economics — NuScale pays ENTRA1 milestone fees).

NuScale's own Regulatory Engagement Plan (Aug-2025) describes a future client pursuing up to 12 modules (~920 MWe) — a plan, not an application [VF, ML25238A096]. The first COLA referencing an SDA would also be first-of-a-kind procedurally: there is no regulation yet for assessing SDA→COLA design changes [VF].

Commercial status. One international customer structure (RoPower, Romania, 6 modules) and one large-but-nonbinding US framework (ENTRA1–TVA, "up to 6 GW / up to 72 NPMs", signed Sep-2025). The company's own 10-Q states no pre-EPC contract is expected "until [RoPower has] secured financing" [VF]. Revenue has collapsed to ~zero ($0.6M in H1-26) because the FEED-2 work that generated 2024–25 revenue is complete [VF].

Financial condition (details in §6). $1.89B liquid, no debt, ~$230–280M/yr underlying burn, ~27% share-count growth in nine months (89.7M shares sold at ~$11.14 via the 2026 ATM), a fresh $750M ATM armed (Aug-11), and a contingent obligation structure (up to ~$2.87B of additional ENTRA1 milestone payments) that is unique in the sector [VF]. Two securities actions (class action re ENTRA1 disclosures, derivative stay) pending [VF].

Management. CEO John Hopkins, CFO Ramsey Hamady. The board added Dr. Dale Klein (former NRC Chairman) and Stuart Harshaw as directors in June 2026 [VF]. Track record to date: technically excellent licensing execution (two design approvals, reviews completed ahead of schedule), commercially zero signed construction orders in the four years since certification, and a distribution of economics to ENTRA1 (15% milestone pool expensed = $507M) that only makes sense if module volumes eventually flow in multiples of what has been contracted. The class action adds an ENTRA1-disclosure dimension. Assessment detail in §11.

The equity story in one sentence: you are buying the option value of the only NRC-approved SMR designs, funded today by a cash pile raised in a falling market, with the company's own commercialization structure taxing future orders — the question is whether any order appears before the next financing window, and at what price.

3.2 Oklo Inc. (NYSE: OKLO)

What it is. A build-own-operate advanced-nuclear developer: fast-spectrum "Aurora" powerhouses (~75 MWe class for grid power, designed for factory repeat builds), intended to be owned by Oklo and monetized via long-term power sales; plus a fuel-recycling ambition (convert used fuel into usable energy), plus a real isotopes business-in-formation (Oklo Isotopes; Atomic Alchemy acquisition; the Groves test reactor). It also sells engineering services through two small June-2026 acquisitions (ARMEC, Creative Engineers) — the source of essentially all of its $1.2M H1-26 revenue [VF].

Demonstrated operational capability — the Groves milestone. On 2026-08-05, Oklo's Groves Isotope Test Reactor (Lockhart, Texas) achieved first criticality under DOE's Reactor Pilot Program — built from a greenfield private site in ~11 months, components (including fuel) manufactured or procured, operating programs developed in-house; DOE counts Groves as the fifth advanced reactor to go critical under the program this summer (joining Antares, Valar, Deployable Energy, Aalo) [VF, DOE + company PR]. This is the strongest verified execution datapoint either company holds. It proves Oklo's construction and startup muscles for a small, low-power machine. It does not license, build, or validate the 75 MWe commercial Aurora, which requires an NRC license and sells a wholly different product (grid electricity).

Regulatory status — the nuance that decides this story. Oklo's actual posture is not what the market often assumes, and it cuts both ways. There is no pending NRC combined license application: the company's earlier Aurora COLA (docket 52-049, filed 2020) was denied without prejudice in January 2022, and as of this report Oklo is only in pre-application engagement with the NRC (pre-application docket 99902095; Phase-1 readiness assessment completed, Phase-2 audit under way, Principal Design Criteria topical report approved in 2026). The company states verbatim it is "evaluating the timing and form of future NRC applications" in light of the Part 53/Part 57 frameworks [VF, 10-Q]. What has started — construction of the INL demonstration unit — proceeds under DOE authorization (Reactor Pilot Program: OTA → NSDA approved Mar-2026 → PDSA approved Jun-11-2026 → final safety analysis DSA and startup authorization remaining), which the company says "provides full authority to construct and operate the powerhouse" on the federal site. DOE authorization covers the INL demonstration; the commercial fleet (Pike County and beyond) requires NRC licensing — and no application for it is pending. Additional held assets: DOE site-use permit at INL (2019, first ever), an NRC materials license for the Idaho radiochemistry lab (Mar-2026), and 5 metric tons of HALEU-equivalent material recovered from EBR-II fuel; commercial fuel supply (Centrus LOI, Jun-2026) is not yet under definitive contract [VF].

Commercial status. A large announced-but-nonbinding demand stack (Switch 12 GW MSA, Equinix, hyperscalers, government), anchored by two concrete commercial structures: the 1.2 GW Pike County (southern Ohio) campus with a Meta prepayment agreement (Jan-2026) — a mechanism for Meta to prepay for power/fund deployment, amount undisclosed [VF] — and a $25M customer prepayment (2024, unknown counterparty) tied to a right of first refusal on future capacity [VF]. No binding PPA with disclosed terms has been signed as of this report; RPO "not material" [VF]. Full pipeline table in §5.

Financial condition (details in §6). $3.0B liquid (largest balance sheet in advanced nuclear), no funded debt, zero going-concern issues, but burn inflecting with construction (guided 2026: OCF −$120–150M + capex $400–500M = $520–650M of total cash use). Raised ~$2.2B of gross equity in 2026 across three ATM programs, each fully sold, at average prices of $95 → $55.6 → (spot $30); a fresh $1B ATM is armed [VF]. Founders and CFO have sold monthly through the decline (~$50M+ combined by the founders; no open-market buys) [VF, Form 4s].

Management. Co-founders Jacob DeWitte (CEO) and Caroline Cochran (COO), ~11.1% combined (13D group, Jul-2026); CFO R. Craig Bealmear. Track record: demonstrated nuclear startup execution at Groves; NRC application actually filed (unlike its closest peer); but also a commercial timeline that has slipped (earlier public targets pointed to a 2027 first plant; current stated target 2028+) and a financing cadence that has shifted the burden decisively onto public shareholders as prices fell. Assessment detail in §11.

The equity story in one sentence: you are buying the most integrated build-own-operate bet in advanced nuclear — the only one with a reactor actually running today — at an enterprise value that ascribes ~$2.6B to a commercial fleet that does not yet have a license, a binding customer, or a fuel-supply contract.

4.3 Business models compared — and what "economics" means for each

  • NuScale = licensing/tech model (asset-light). Revenue streams: design & licensing fees, engineering services during development/construction, equipment supply (via Doosan), later fuel & services annuities. Capital intensity at NuScale is low; the capital intensity lives with customers/project companies — which is precisely why NuScale cannot book revenue until someone else finances a plant. Its cost structure is ~$230–280M/yr of opex and — unique to it — milestone payments to its commercialization partner (up to ~$3.4B pool for 72 modules; $507M already expensed). Estimated per-module economics to NuScale: ~$25–60M NPV band (no primary disclosure — NOT FOUND; anchored on the size of the disclosed ENTRA1 payments and plausible equipment-scope margins).
  • Oklo = IPP/owner model (asset-heavy, higher ceiling, higher capital burden). Revenue begins only when its own plants sell electricity; the payoff per unit is the full merchant/contracted margin on 40–60 years of output (modeled ~$60–150M NPV per unit at $80–100/MWh with $200–300M capex — highly sensitive to both inputs). Oklo must fund construction itself (plus fuel infrastructure), hence the ~$2.2B of equity raised in 2026 and the expectation of project-level capital next. Its vertical integration (fuel recycling, isotopes) is real optionality but also multiplies execution surfaces.
  • The structural comparison: NuScale earns sooner if someone else builds (fees start pre-construction); Oklo earns more if it builds (owns the asset) but nothing before steel and licensing. For equivalent deployed capacity, NuScale's equity value is a toll, Oklo's is an equity stake. The market has historically paid more for the stake (OKLO premium) — this report's arithmetic says the stake's premium is the more fragile part.

10 — Red-team risk assessment: the strongest bear cases, the strongest bull cases, and what would falsify each

10.1 The strongest evidence-based bear thesis — SMR (NuScale)

"The most successful licensing portfolio in SMRs, attached to a business model in which the company pays its own sales partner for hope."

  1. The commercialization chain has not moved one link in 3.5 years. NRC certified the 50 MWe design in Jan-2023 and approved the 77 MWe US460 standard design in May-2025 — yet no customer has filed a combined license application to actually build one [VF, NRC docket 99902078 remains a pre-application docket; nothing docketed as of Oct-14-2026]. A design approval is a hunting license, not a business. The first COLA to reference an SDA is first-of-a-kind with unresolved regulatory questions (no rulebook for SDA→COLA design changes) — i.e., the hard part of licensing has not even started.
  2. The ENTRA1 architecture pays out on announcements, not on revenue. $507.4M expensed (15% Milestone-1) triggered by a non-binding 72-module agreement; $259.9M of that settled in cash in H1-26; up to ~$2.87B more contractually triggered on binding PPAs/OEM docs — payable by NuScale to ENTRA1 regardless of whether the modules are ever built economically. Contractually, ENTRA1 "retains sole and full discretion to select, contract with, or purchase from NuScale" [VF, 10-Q] while NuScale needs ENTRA1's consent to pursue opportunities without it. This is, economically, a toll booth placed on NuScale's own commercialization by a private counterparty whose experience and qualifications are the subject of a securities class action (Truedson, amended Jul-10-26, adding ENTRA1 as defendant [VF]).
  3. Revenue is going to zero, not up. H1-26 revenue $0.6M vs $21.4M H1-25; the Fluor FEED-2 work ended and nothing replaced it (done at a negative gross margin after a June-2026 price settlement with Fluor [VF]). FY26 consensus revenue ≈ $24M and falling [3P].
  4. RoPower is stalling at the financing gate — by the company's own language: "We do not anticipate that RoPower will enter into a pre-EPC contract until they have secured financing… once a pre-EPC contract is entered into, we will recommence work" [VF]. Romania's Feb-2026 investment-decision approval authorizes the search for financing — not construction. Until Romanian/European money closes, NuScale's flagship project generates zero work.
  5. Dilution arithmetic is brutal at $6. Shares +27% in nine months (337.9M→429.7M); a fresh $750M ATM sits armed (Aug-11); filling it at $6 adds ~125M shares (+29% of the post-issue count). Every raise at these prices converts financial runway into share-count expansion faster than it converts hope into contracts.
  6. Governance drag: two live securities actions (federal class action re ENTRA1 disclosures + derivative suit stayed behind it [VF]); a securities-fraud investigation disclosed by Pomerantz LLP on Oct-13 (T3, unverified); Fluor exited completely (no controlling sponsor left); insiders own 0.33%. Offsetting: the board added Dr. Dale Klein, a former NRC Chairman, as a director in June 2026 [VF].
  7. Valuation: at spot, bear −55%, bull only +14%. You are paid ~1:4 on the skew, in the wrong direction.

10.2 The strongest evidence-based bear thesis — OKLO

"A $5.7B market cap for a company whose only approved deliverable is a pilot test — funded by selling stock into a falling market, at a cadence that says the plan costs more than it looks."

  1. No commercial plant is licensed or built — and no NRC application is even pending for one. The 2020 Aurora COLA was denied in 2022; Oklo today is an NRC pre-applicant ("evaluating the timing and form of future NRC applications" [VF]) while the INL demonstration proceeds under DOE authorization — a pathway that covers the federal-site demo but not the commercial fleet. The one reactor that runs — Groves at Lockhart, Texas — is a 15 MWt low-power isotope test reactor built under DOE's Reactor Pilot Program (first criticality 2026-08-05, ~11 months from groundbreaking [VF, DOE/company]). That is a genuine, impressive execution milestone and the strongest single bull datapoint in this report — but a DOE-authorized test reactor on private land is a categorically different licensing and engineering problem from an NRC-licensed 75 MWe power plant selling electricity on the grid; the company's own PR frames Groves as establishing "the operational foundation" for those future deployments, i.e., the future part has not happened yet.
  2. The customer pipeline is nonbinding and top-heavy in one theme. Switch's "12 GW master agreement", Equinix, hyperscaler MOUs — these are MSAs/LOIs, not PPAs with take-or-pay; not one dollar of contracted revenue sits in the filings (RPO "not material" [VF]). The strongest binding artifact is a $25M refundable-style prepayment tied to a right of first refusal [VF].
  3. Capital consumption is inflecting while the stock falls. H1-26 burn −$192M; FY26 consensus FCF −$522M; capex into construction ($126.9M H1, CIP $165M). Three ATM programs in twelve months raised $2.2B at steeply declining prices ($96.95 → $63.51 → ~$44 → spot $30): each new dollar costs 2–3× the shares of the prior dollar. A $1B program sits armed today.
  4. Founders and CFO have sold in near-monthly tranches through the entire decline — with two tranches explicitly not under 10b5-1 plans [VF, Forms 4; see §3], while open-market insider purchases total zero. Management is diversifying out of its own story while telling the market to fund it.
  5. The "greens" require manufacturing-grade cost discipline that has never existed in first-of-a-kind nuclear. At vendor-typical overrun rates (2–3× overnight cost is the historical norm — Vogtle, Flamanville, Olkiluoto), a $200–300M unit becomes $500–900M and the $90–150M/unit NPV becomes negative. Oklo's own filings carry no committed construction cost, no committed fuel contract (Centrus is an LOI), and a $1.68B Tennessee fuel-roadmap that is explicitly not committed [VF].
  6. Valuation: at spot you pay $2.57B of enterprise value for the option — ~50% above the modeled bull ($19.8). The reverse math (125 units implied by the average sell-side target) should be read as evidence about the sell-side, not about OKLO.

11.2 Conclusions (conditional, not a rating)

  1. The structural difference is who bears construction risk. The stocks crashed 83–89% because they were priced for outcomes that are still years unproven; at today's prices they remain priced for outcomes years unproven.
  4.
