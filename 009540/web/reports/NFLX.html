1. The setup

  • A growth stock that became a value stock — and the market is still adjusting. NFLX is down 46% over 12 months and 54% from its high, now trading at 17.3x forward EPS and 16.6x EV/EBITDA. The de-rating reflects a reset in expectations: subscriber/engagement growth is maturing, content spend is rising, and the 2026 guidance was cut on 21-Oct.
  • The numbers are still good: TTM revenue $49.7B (+10.1%), net income $14.8B (29.7% margin), FCF $11.4B (22.9% margin), ROIC 34%. Q3 2026 revenue $12.87B (+11.9%), EPS $0.87 (post-split).
  • The problem is the growth algorithm. Revenue growth has slowed to ~11-12%, and BofA flags declining viewing hours per subscriber, higher content spend and a more active M&A posture as the three concerns. The era of 20%+ subscriber growth is over; the next leg must come from pricing, advertising and password-sharing monetisation.
  • The valuation already discounts a lot. At 17x forward earnings and 4.5% FCF yield, NFLX is cheaper than the S&P 500 for the first time in a decade. The question is whether 10-11% growth is the new normal or the start of a slide.
  • Balance sheet: $8.2B cash, $17.5B debt, net debt $9.3B; Altman 10.8. Share count down 2.1% YoY. No dividend.
  • Sentiment: below the 50-DMA ($69) and 200-DMA ($80.5); short interest 2.2%; average target $84.76 — implying ~36% upside if the guidance reset holds.

2. Business and moat

Netflix is the largest subscription streaming service (~300M+ paid households) with a growing advertising tier. Revenue comes from subscriptions (price × members) and advertising. The moat is content scale, recommendation data and global distribution; the risk is competition (Disney+, Amazon, YouTube) and the maturity of the pay-TV replacement cycle.

The ad tier is the most important new lever: it lowers the effective price, expands the addressable market and adds a second revenue stream. If Netflix can scale ads to 10%+ of revenue at high margins, the growth algorithm improves.

5. Catalysts

  • Q4 2026 results (late Jan 2027): subscriber/ARM trends and 2027 guidance.
  • Advertising tier scale — the key growth lever.
  • Content slate: the 2027 film/series lineup and live sports.
  • M&A: a Warner Bros./Fox asset deal would change the capital story.
  • Price increases and password-sharing enforcement.
