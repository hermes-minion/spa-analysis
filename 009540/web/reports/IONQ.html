IonQ, Inc. (NYSE: IONQ) — Equity Research Note

Price: $28.88 (NYSE close, 13 Nov 2026) · Market cap: $12.5B · EV: $11.6B · EV/Sales TTM: 30.4x · FCF: -$689M · Net cash: $915M Verdict: Bearish (Medium conviction) · 12-month probability-weighted value: ~$24 (-18%) Screening note; data as of 13-Nov-2026. Research, not investment advice.

1. The setup

  • The revenue exploded; the economics did not. TTM revenue is $380M (from $130M in FY2025 and $43M in FY2024), but gross margin is only 25% and the operating loss is -$1.17B. Q3 2026 revenue was $173.8M (+336% YoY), almost certainly acquisition-driven (Oxford Ionics, ID Quantique, Lightsynq), not organic quantum demand.
  • Cash burn is the binding constraint. TTM FCF -$689M against $1.07B of cash and $154M of debt — about 1.3 years of runway at the current rate. The share count rose 47% YoY. Another large raise is a question of when, not if.
  • The reported profit is accounting, not operations. TTM net income is +$255M because of mark-to-market gains on investments (Q3 net income +$551M on an operating loss); the operating business loses over $1B a year.
  • The valuation is still a venture-capital price: 30x EV/sales for a 25%-gross-margin hardware/services mix, with a 3.4 beta and 11.7% of float sold short. The stock is 65% below its high but still capitalises the company at $12.5B.
  • Leadership churn is a fresh red flag: CFO Inder Singh exited "effective immediately" on 13-Nov; the company appointed Merrill as CFO and Austin as CAO. The stock fell 8% that day as quantum names slid.
  • The technology is real: trapped-ion quantum computers with high fidelity, a roadmap to logical qubits, and government/enterprise contracts. The question is whether the revenue and margin structure can ever justify the price.

2. Business and moat

IonQ builds trapped-ion quantum computers and sells systems, cloud access and services. It has a differentiated modality (trapped ions have high gate fidelity and all-to-all connectivity) and a growing patent portfolio. The 2025-26 acquisition spree bought photonics, networking and quantum-safe cryptography assets — a bet on becoming a full-stack quantum platform.

The moat is technical (ion-trap IP, error-correction know-how) and relational (government labs, hyperscaler cloud partnerships). It is not yet commercial: revenue is small relative to the market cap, gross margins are low because systems are built to order, and the path to profitability depends on scale that does not exist yet.

5. Catalysts

  • Q4 2026 results (Feb 2027): organic revenue (ex-acquisitions), gross margin and cash burn.
  • A financing event: the most likely near-term catalyst; size and price will set the equity floor.
  • Logical-qubit/error-correction milestones — the technical proof point.
  • Government contracts (DARPA, DOE) and hyperscaler partnerships.
