1. The setup

  • A 46% drawdown in a business that just grew revenue 54%. FIX is 37% lower in three months and 28% lower in one month, yet Q3 2026 revenue was $3.77B (+53.6% YoY) and EPS $13.91 (+69%). The market is not selling the results; it is de-rating the entire data-center/electrical-contracting complex on AI-capex fears.
  • The numbers are extraordinary. TTM revenue $12.54B (from $9.10B in FY2025, +38%), net income $1.63B, EPS $46.30, FCF $2.44B (19.5% of revenue), ROIC 103%, net cash $1.85B. This is a capex-light contractor earning software-like returns on capital.
  • Backlog is the thesis. Comfort Systems builds the mechanical, electrical and plumbing systems for data centers, chip fabs, hospitals and industrial plants. Its backlog has been at record levels; the AI build-out is the single largest driver, now a material share of revenue.
  • Valuation has reset to 17.8x forward earnings from over 30x at the peak, with net cash equal to ~5% of the market cap. For a business compounding revenue >30% with 100% ROIC, that is a rare combination.
  • The risk is cyclical, not operational. If hyperscaler capex pauses, bookings slow with a lag; contractors are late-cycle. The 46% drawdown is the market pricing that risk early.
  • Sentiment: below the 50-DMA ($1,469) and 200-DMA ($1,605); short interest 2.5%; JPMorgan names FIX among top engineering picks; Zacks upgraded it to Strong Buy. The crowd is bearish on the tape but bullish on the fundamentals.

2. Business and moat

FIX operates through regional mechanical/electrical contractors (Comfort Systems, Walker, MMC, etc.), executing design-build and installation work. The model is low-capital-intensity: customers fund projects, FIX is paid progress billing, and working capital is a source of cash when backlogs grow.

The moat is (1) scarce skilled labour — electricians, welders, pipefitters are the bottleneck in data-center build-outs; (2) local density — regional contractors with union labour and long-standing relationships; (3) design-build capability — FIX can take a data-center shell from design to commissioning. Scale and reputation let it win the largest, most complex jobs.

The cyclical risk is real: data-center construction is a capex cycle, and FIX's revenue is concentrated in a handful of hyperscaler programs. The 2022-23 backlog boom was driven by supply-chain reshoring; the 2025-26 boom is AI.
