0. Classification (mandatory)

Profile D — cash-burning and levered. Q3 FY2026 revenue $70.2M, GAAP gross margin 31.8%, operating margin −55.5% and FCF −$23.1M; net debt $310.1M plus $415M of converts. The equity is the residual of a leveraged balance sheet and depends on the Wuxi sale and radar ramp. Business model: fabless automotive semiconductor (ADAS, radar, in-cabin, lighting). Metrics that matter: EV/sales, cash runway, convertibles, dilution and net debt.

1. Executive summary

  • The headline growth is flattered by a business being sold. Q3 FY2026 revenue was $70.2M (+31% YoY), but $29.5M (42%) came from Wuxi indie Micro, a Chinese commodity display/touch-IC subsidiary that indie agreed to divest on 27 Oct 2025 for ~$135M (10-Q, Note 19). Core ADAS revenue was $40.7M, +14% QoQ, guided to $47M in Q4. Core, not consolidated, is the investable company.
  • The core is inflecting for the first time since 2023. Radar completed PPAP and is in high-volume production; the chipset was selected by one of the top three North American OEMs; the DRAM-less iND880 vision processor has 40+ design wins in 2026 with <20% ramped; quantum photonics had record revenue and bookings (Q3 release, Q3 call). Management sizes radar at 50–70M units/year at $5–6 at maturity — a claim, not an order book.
  • The P&L is far from paying for itself. TTM revenue $247.7M, GAAP gross margin 35.6%, operating margin −58.9%, FCF −$96.5M, SBC ~$74.5M (30% of revenue). The −$7.2M "non-GAAP operating loss" is pre-compensation: 81% of the $39.0M GAAP operating loss is SBC and amortisation.
  • The 2027 profitability promise does not close post-divestiture. Ex-Wuxi, $40.7M of core revenue at ~50% gross margin yields ~$20M of gross profit against ~$34M of quarterly opex. Breakeven needs $67–73M of core revenue per quarter — 65–80% above today's run-rate — against guidance of non-GAAP profitability in Q2 2027 and positive cash flow in Q3 2027.
  • The balance sheet, not the technology, is the binding constraint. $415.0M of converts ($26.0M due 2027 at $8.65, $218.5M due 2029 at $5.14, $170.5M due 2031 at $3.87) against $134.2M of cash. The 2029s trade at 76% of par (~13% YTM, my calc) — the credit market is pricing refinancing risk the equity market ignores. Altman Z −0.28; no going-concern qualification.
  • Sentiment is hostile and insiders are sellers. Short interest 64.4M shares = 26.7% of shares out, 28.4% of float, 11.9 days to cover. Across 44 Form 4s since May 2026 there were 45 sales and zero open-market purchases, at $5.31 down to $2.05. Eight analysts say "Buy" with a $4.32 target; post-Q3 cuts were $1.55, $3 and $4.
  • Physical-AI purity is about half, not all. ~48% of consolidated revenue is perception-and-act silicon or software today because Wuxi is 42% of sales; ~85% post-divestiture. Robots, drones and humanoids specifically are under 2% — real wins (AGIBOT, Unitree, Figure AI), no material revenue. The stock is the cheapest in its history at 3.3x EV/sales and 1.7x book, but EV is 1.6x market cap, so each turn of EV/sales moves equity ~50% ($0.79 at 2.0x, $2.87 at 4.0x).

Verdict: BEARISH — conviction Medium. Probability-weighted 12–24 month value ~$1.74 (bear $0.37, base $1.85, bull $3.90). The product cycle is real and Wuxi is a genuine catalyst, but the equity is a levered option on a radar ramp that must clear a ~$70M/quarter breakeven, financed by a balance sheet the bond market already discounts. I would revisit on a closed Wuxi sale plus two quarters of core revenue above $55M.

2. Business overview

indie designs mixed-signal SoCs and system solutions for automotive perception — radar, vision, LiDAR, ultrasound — plus in-cabin user-experience silicon and, since TeraXion/Exalos, photonics. Fabless except photonics assembly in Quebec and Switzerland; ~800 employees; 360+ issued patents (FY2025 10-K).

3. Physical AI exposure

indie is a sensing-layer company with a compute component — not a systems or software company.

SENSING COMPUTE/SILICON DATA SOFTWARE/PERCEPTION SYSTEMS Radar SoC+MMIC (own) iND880 DRAM-less ISP none emotion3D in-cabin none Vision SoC (own) radar baseband (no fleet, perception (acquired) LiDAR components (own) photonics (own) no maps) Ultrasound (own) fabbed at TSMC/others

Sensing is the strongest layer: four modalities in-house is genuinely broad at this size. Compute: the iND880's DRAM-less architecture is the most commercially interesting asset — with DRAM prices up 3–4x in 2026 (management's figure), a processor that avoids external DRAM wins on bill-of-materials grounds, and management says some sockets went from first contact to first shipment in "two or three months". Data: none — no crowd-sourced map, no fleet, no proprietary dataset. Software: emotion3D in-cabin perception at $4–8 per vehicle royalty (management, Q3 call). Systems: none; indie sells components to Tier-1s, not ECUs, robots or vehicles.

Treat non-automotive Physical AI as a free option, not a revenue line.

4. Competitive position & moat

indie is a sub-scale challenger in automotive mixed-signal. FY2025 revenue of $217M compares with NXP's $12.3B, onsemi's $6.0B and Infineon's automotive segment at roughly $8B. No fab, no proprietary process node, no software ecosystem. What it has is socket-by-socket design wins in growth niches and a cost-optimised architecture that matters more in a memory shortage than in a boom.

  • Medium-strong — qualification/PPAP. Re-sourcing a qualified part costs a Tier-1 12–18 months, so wins persist 5–8 years.
  • Medium and time-limited — DRAM-less vision. A real BOM advantage while DRAM is scarce and 3–4x dearer; it erodes if memory normalises or Ambarella/NXP ship equivalents.
  • Weak — cost and capital. Fabless, 800 staff, $247M revenue, negative margin after amortisation; rivals amortise R&D over 10–50x the revenue and can fund a price war. Multi-modal breadth helps sourcing but without scale is a cost disadvantage, not a moat.

Competitors: NXP (radar incumbent, 58% automotive revenue — high threat), Texas Instruments (radar and mixed-signal cost leader with own fabs — high), Infineon (77 GHz radar, MCUs — high), onsemi (sensors and power — medium-high), Ambarella (vision processors; 1.9x indie's revenue and 5x its market cap — medium-high), Mobileye/Horizon/Qualcomm/NVIDIA (low-medium at indie's price points, high if OEMs consolidate on central compute), and Chinese local suppliers in the market that is 63% of Q3 revenue — high, and the reason Wuxi's products commoditised.

OPINION. This is a socket moat, not a company moat. indie can hold individual programmes for years but cannot defend a price umbrella — which is why non-GAAP gross margin fell from 52.5% in FY2023 to 41.6% in Q3 FY2026. Management's promise of "low 50s" post-Wuxi is a mix argument, not evidence of pricing power.

Design wins vs revenue. Strategic backlog is $6.2B (10-year rolling), "slightly up" YoY, excluding Wuxi and excluding Physical AI wins. Management reported $7.1B in late 2024 (Q3 FY2024 call summary — secondary source, flagged), implying a ~13% decline in two years as old programmes rolled off faster than new ones landed; management concedes it has "not won the follow-on designs for those yet". A 25x-coverage number that the 10-K explicitly disclaims as an estimate of expected revenue from expected wins is a sales pipeline, not an order book. The gap between wins and revenue is the story: 40+ vision wins with <20% ramped, radar material only from Q4 2026, humanoid wins at zero revenue.

What the numbers say

  1. Three years, no growth. $223.2M (FY2023) → $216.7M → $217.4M → ~$267M (FY2026E). Q3 FY2026 revenue of $70.2M is essentially identical to Q4 FY2023's $70.1M. The 2026 acceleration is real in the core (+37%, my estimate) but cross-subsidised by a Wuxi consolidation that will disappear.
  2. Gross margin is structurally lower than the story. GAAP GM fell from 42.5% (Q4 FY2024) to 31.8% (Q3 FY2026) on Wuxi mix and $16.2M of acquired-intangible amortisation inside COGS (up from $12.3M). Non-GAAP GM of 41.6% versus 52.5% in FY2023 is a 1,090bp erosion mix alone does not explain.
  3. Most of the loss is excluded from the loss. Q3 GAAP operating loss −$39.0M, plus back $24.3M SBC and $7.3M amortisation → −$7.2M non-GAAP. SBC is a real transfer of ownership: diluted shares 219.3M vs 199.3M, +10% YoY. FCF was −$23.1M in the quarter and −$96.5M TTM, with inventory up $31.4M and receivables up $21.2M in nine months — growth currently consumes cash.
  4. Reconciling the aggregator's negative gross margin. stockanalysis.com reports −28.4% TTM. That is not the filed margin: revenue less reported COGS gives $88.1M of gross profit (35.6%). The aggregator appears to load non-COGS items into cost of revenue. Use the filed figures; the direction is right, the magnitude is not.

The 2029s were issued at a $5.14 conversion price (27.5% premium to a $4.03 stock) in Dec 2024 with a capped call at $8.06. The stock is $2.10; the notes are 59% out of the money and trade at 76c. That is the most important number in this report — the credit market is pricing a company that may not refinance $218.5M in 2029 without equity issuance or asset sales. (The 2031s at 106.8% look inconsistent; both are attributed to quoted prices in inactive markets, so I weight the 2029 print more heavily.)

Runway. Cash $134.2M + $51.2M ATM capacity + ~$135M gross Wuxi proceeds = ~$320M against $415M of converts, of which only $26M matures before 2029. At ~$96M annual burn, standalone cash lasts ~17 months, into Q1 2028 — which is why Wuxi is existential to the equity rather than merely helpful. Management claims "ample liquidity… independent of any proceeds from the Wuxi divestiture" (MANAGEMENT CLAIM), contingent on the Q2 2027 inflection arriving on schedule.

Dilution. Shares outstanding 238.6M (225.1M Class A + 13.4M Class V), up from 220.6M at 31 Dec 2025 (+8.2%), +9.07% YoY. The ATM has $98.8M raised cumulatively at an average $7.11 and $51.2M available; 9M FY2026 sold 2.75M shares at $3.13 for $8.6M. The $50M buyback has never been used since 2022. If all converts converted, dilution is 89.6M shares (+37%) — but every strike is above the current price, so refinancing, not conversion, is the risk. SBC at ~30% of revenue is ~15% of market cap per year in economic dilution, and nothing offsets it.

14. What would change my mind

Bullish signals (would move me to Neutral/Buy): 1. Wuxi closes by Q1 2027 at or near $135M, with proceeds explicitly applied to the 2029 converts. That single action removes risks #1 and #3 and would likely re-rate the equity regardless of the operating story. 2. Core revenue ≥$55M in two consecutive quarters (versus $40.7M in Q3 FY2026 and $47M guided for Q4) — the minimum trajectory consistent with breakeven without Wuxi. 3. Radar at a $100M+ annualised run-rate with a disclosed second OEM, validating the 50–70M unit / $5–6 ASP claim. 4. Non-GAAP operating breakeven delivered in Q2 2027 on a post-Wuxi basis, with opex at or below $34M/quarter. 5. Gross margin ≥50% for two quarters post-divestiture, proving the margin story is mix, not pricing.

Bearish signals (would move me to Sell outright): 1. Wuxi termination or regulatory rejection. Without $135M, the 2029 maturity becomes a live solvency question and the equity is worth the option value of a refinancing. 2. Another equity raise at or below $2, or ATM usage above $25M in a quarter — converting a leverage problem into a permanent dilution problem. 3. Core revenue below $45M in Q4 FY2026 (a miss against the $47M guide), signalling a radar ramp slower than the design-win narrative implies. 4. A 2029 convertible exchange at a material discount to par, crystallising the credit market's view in the P&L. 5. Further insider selling with no open-market purchases through the next two reporting cycles.
