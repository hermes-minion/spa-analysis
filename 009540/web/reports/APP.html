1. The setup

  • The most violent round trip in the batch. APP is up 56% in the last month and 30% in three months, yet still down 34% YTD and 44% from its 5-year high. The trigger was a blow-out Q3 (4-Nov): revenue $2.185B (+55% YoY), EPS $4.30 vs $4.03 consensus, and the stock's best day ever (+31%, versus a ±17.5% implied move).
  • The P&L is exceptional. TTM revenue $7.61B, net income $5.01B (65.9% net margin), operating margin 77.8%, gross margin 88.8%, FCF $4.9B (64% of revenue). ROE 210%, ROIC 138%. Few software businesses in history have printed margins like these.
  • Valuation is no longer absurd, but not cheap. At 20.8x forward EPS and 17.9x EV/sales, APP is priced as a high-growth, high-margin platform — which it is — with no margin of safety for the concentration and regulatory risks.
  • The business is now effectively one product: the AXON advertising engine. AppLovin sold its Apps gaming studio business (to Tripledot, 2025) and is using the cash and cash flow to expand from mobile-game advertising into e-commerce and self-serve advertisers. The bet is that AXON's recommendation engine works outside gaming.
  • Governance and credibility remain the swing factor. The 2025 short-seller attacks (Muddy Waters, Fuzzy Panda) and the resulting disclosure scrutiny never fully disappeared; insiders still own ~20%, and the company pays no dividend. A 2.5 beta and 3.3% short interest mean the stock will keep trading as a high-beta AI-advertising proxy.
  • Momentum is extended. Price $409 vs 50-DMA $302 and 200-DMA $406: the stock has reclaimed its 200-day but is 35% above its 50-day. Post-earnings gaps of this size usually consolidate before the next leg.

2. Business and moat

AppLovin's core is AXON, a machine-learning ad-recommendation engine that matches advertisers to mobile inventory. It earns a take rate on ad spend, plus software-platform fees. The economic magic is that AXON's models improve with data density: more spend → better predictions → higher return on ad spend → more spend. That flywheel produced 55% growth at 78% operating margins.

The moat is real but narrower than the margin implies: (1) proprietary models trained on a large mobile-gaming dataset; (2) direct advertiser relationships and a self-serve platform; (3) scale economics in a market where the alternative is Google/Meta. The risks are (a) the dataset is gaming-heavy while the growth story is e-commerce, (b) Apple/Google control the plumbing and privacy rules, and (c) the company's disclosures have been challenged.

The bull case is that AppLovin becomes the "third pole" of performance advertising alongside Google and Meta. The bear case is that its edge is a gaming-data artefact, e-commerce CAC rises, and the multiple mean-reverts to the high-teens.

5. Catalysts

  • Q4 2026 results (Feb 2027): first full quarter of e-commerce/self-serve disclosure; watch ad-spend growth and take rate.
  • E-commerce advertiser count and spend per advertiser — the only proof the model generalises beyond gaming.
  • Index/ownership flows: APP's weight in growth and AI-advertising baskets; ETF flows were net sellers on 11-Nov, a headwind.
  • Any regulatory or disclosure development: the short-seller file is dormant, not closed.
