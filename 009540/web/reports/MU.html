1. The setup

  • The most extreme memory cycle in history, at a single-digit multiple. Micron's TTM revenue is $133.2B (from $37.4B in FY2025), net income $85.0B, EPS $74.33. Q4 FY2026 revenue was $54.2B (+379% YoY), EPS $32.85, gross margin 80.7%, operating margin 74.6%.
  • The stock is up 228% in a year and 147% YTD — yet it has fallen 36% from its high as the AI-semiconductor complex de-rated. At 10.5x trailing and 4.4x forward earnings, it looks optically cheap; at peak-cycle margins, it is not.
  • The driver is HBM/DRAM scarcity: AI accelerators need high-bandwidth memory, and supply is tight. Micron's HBM3E/HBM4 ramp and the DRAM price cycle produced record margins. The risk is the same cycle turning: memory has historically been the most volatile semiconductor segment.
  • The balance sheet is transformed: $68.3B net cash, up from net debt two years ago. FCF $59.0B. This cycle has repaired the balance sheet and funded capacity.
  • The valuation debate is about normalized earnings: at $778 and 4.4x forward EPS (~$177), the market expects the boom to continue. If EPS normalizes to $60-80, the stock is at 10-13x — fair for a cyclical, not cheap.
  • Sentiment: below the 50-DMA ($944) but above the 200-DMA ($766); short interest 2.1%; average target $1,562. The sell-side is still extrapolating the cycle.

2. Business and moat

Micron sells DRAM, NAND and HBM memory to data-center, mobile, PC and automotive customers. The moat is technology/manufacturing scale in a consolidated oligopoly (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron). Memory is a commodity with cyclical pricing, but HBM is more differentiated (custom packaging, qualification with Nvidia/AMD).

The risk is capacity additions by competitors and a demand air-pocket in AI servers. Memory pricing can halve in a downturn, as it did in 2023.

The stock is a leveraged bet on memory pricing. The base case is fairly valued; the bear case is severe.

5. Catalysts

  • HBM4 qualification with Nvidia/AMD.
  • DRAM/NAND contract prices (monthly).
