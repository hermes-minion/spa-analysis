1. The setup

  • A marketplace with 33% FCF margins trading at 11.8x forward earnings. CarGurus is the largest US car-shopping marketplace, monetising dealer subscriptions and advertising. The stock is 28% below its high but up 16% in the last month after a solid Q3.
  • Q3 2026 revenue was $258.4M (+11.5% YoY), EPS $0.59, and TTM revenue reached $994M with net income of $184M (18.5% margin) and FCF of $325M (32.7% margin). This is a high-quality, cash-generative marketplace.
  • Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly: the share count fell 10.3% YoY through buybacks, and the company has negligible net debt ($17M). At 10.5x FCF, buybacks are highly accretive.
  • The growth story is international + CarOffer: the US marketplace is mature (~10% growth), while UK/Canada and the instant-offer (CarOffer) business are the incremental drivers. Dealer churn and the used-car cycle are the swing factors.
  • Valuation is cheap on forward earnings but not on trailing: the gap between 22.7x TTM and 11.8x forward implies consensus expects EPS to roughly double — a forecast that needs verification. The forward number may reflect a one-off benefit; treat it with caution.
  • Sentiment: price near the 50-DMA ($32.1) and 200-DMA ($32.9); short interest 10.5% of float; analysts' average target $41.55 with Neutral/Outperform ratings.

2. Business and moat

CarGurus operates a car-shopping marketplace: consumers search listings, dealers pay for leads/subscriptions, and manufacturers pay for advertising. Revenue is mostly US dealer subscriptions (~70%), with international and product revenue (CarOffer, financing) making up the rest.

The moat is consumer traffic and dealer network effects: more listings attract more shoppers, which attracts more dealers. The risk is competition from Carvana/Cars.com/Autotrader and the possibility that AI shopping agents disintermediate the marketplace.
