Alphabet Inc. (NASDAQ: GOOGL) — Equity Research Note

Price: $295.77 (Nasdaq close, 13 Nov 2026) · Market cap: $3.64T · EV: $3.52T · P/E TTM: 15.4x · Fwd P/E: 21.5x · EV/EBITDA: 19.0x · FCF yield: 0.5% Verdict: Bullish (Medium conviction) · 12-month probability-weighted value: ~$331 (+12%) Screening note; data as of 13-Nov-2026. Research, not investment advice.

1. The setup

  • The cheapest mega-cap on operating earnings, with the most contentious cash-flow line. Alphabet trades at 15.4x trailing GAAP EPS, but that EPS is inflated by mark-to-market gains on its AI stakes (Q2 2026 EPS alone was $9.11 on a $112B quarterly net income). On normalized operating earnings the multiple is ~27x; on forward EPS it is 21.5x.
  • The capex number is the story. TTM operating cash flow $179.6B, capex $159.8B, FCF only $19.75B — a 4% FCF margin versus 25-30% historically. Alphabet is spending like a utility to build AI data centers, and the market is trying to decide whether that is investment or value destruction. The stock is 26.5% below its high.
  • Operating momentum is excellent. Q3 2026 revenue $127.6B (+24.7% YoY), operating margin 33.3%, net income $26.5B (EPS $2.14). Search, YouTube and Cloud are all growing; Gemini is a genuine franchise; TPUs are a structural cost advantage.
  • The balance sheet absorbs the capex: $265B cash/investments, $150B debt, net cash $115B, Altman 9.3. Even at this spend level, Alphabet is not funding growth with distress.
  • The bear case is FCF compression plus regulatory pressure (the €1.66B EU fine, the ad-tech remedies, AI-content litigation). The bull case is that AI capex creates a durable cloud/search moat and the FCF trough is 2026-27.
  • Sentiment: below the 50-DMA ($328) and 200-DMA ($338); short interest just 0.6%; 112 analysts with a $400 average target. This is a consensus long that has been de-rated, not abandoned.

2. Business and moat

Alphabet is three businesses: Google Services (Search, YouTube, Maps, Play, subscriptions), Google Cloud (GCP, Workspace, TPU/AI infrastructure) and Other Bets (Waymo, Verily, etc.). Advertising is still ~75% of revenue; Cloud is the growth and margin engine.

The moat is distribution (billions of users, default search, Android/Chrome), data (query and video engagement), and now vertical integration in AI silicon (TPUs) plus models (Gemini). No competitor has the same combination of distribution, compute and model ownership. The vulnerability is regulatory: search defaults, ad-tech bundling and app-store rules are all under attack in the US and EU.

5. Catalysts

  • Q4 2026 results (late Jan 2027): capex guidance for 2027 and Cloud backlog are the key numbers.
  • Gemini model releases and enterprise adoption.
  • Waymo expansion — a real option, currently valued at near zero by most sums-of-parts.
  • Regulatory outcomes: EU ad-tech remedies, the €1.66B fine, US search case.
  • Buybacks: $70B+ annual repurchase capacity even at peak capex.
