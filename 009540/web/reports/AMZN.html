1. The setup

  • The largest revenue base in the batch, with the weakest cash conversion. Amazon's TTM revenue is $797B, net income $134.8B — but free cash flow is negative $46.9B because capex is running at ~$200B a year for AI data centers, fulfillment and logistics. The market is paying 27x forward earnings for a business in a capex supercycle.
  • The trailing P/E of 21.6x is misleading. TTM net income includes a large one-off gain (Q2 2026 net income was $62.6B on an EPS of $5.75, versus $1.89 in Q3), likely from an investment mark-to-market. The forward P/E of 27x is the cleaner, and less flattering, number.
  • Operating momentum is good: Q3 2026 revenue $201.9B (+12.1% YoY), net income $20.7B, EPS $1.89. AWS is re-accelerating on AI workloads; retail margins are expanding on advertising and logistics efficiency.
  • The capex is the thesis: Amazon is building the AI cloud, and the spending is so large that FCF has turned negative. If AWS revenue compounds at 25%+ and capex plateaus, FCF inflects sharply in 2028; if not, the equity is a capital sink.
  • Balance sheet: $114.2B cash, $304.8B debt (including leases), net debt $190.6B. Altman 3.8 — leverage is real but investment-grade. No dividend.
  • Sentiment: near its 5-year high (only 6% below), above the 50-DMA ($255) and 200-DMA ($245); short interest 0.9%; average target $327. The market is giving Amazon credit for AWS AI.

2. Business and moat

Amazon has three engines: North America retail (e-commerce, advertising, logistics), International retail and AWS (cloud infrastructure, database, AI services). Advertising is the highest-margin piece and is growing >20%; AWS is the profit pool (~60% of operating income).

The moat is logistics scale (same-day/next-day delivery), Prime membership, the marketplace network and AWS's installed base. The risks are antitrust (FTC case), labour costs, and the possibility that AI shifts cloud workloads to specialized neoclouds.
