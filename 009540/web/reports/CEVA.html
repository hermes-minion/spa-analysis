1. Executive summary

  • A connectivity-royalty business wearing an AI label. Q3 FY2026 revenue $29.06M (+2.4% YoY); 9M $85.12M (+8.7%). By the company's own use-case split, 73% of 9M revenue is "Connect" (Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, cellular-IoT, 5G baseband), 17% "Infer" (NPUs, vision, AI DSPs) and 10% "Sense" [1][2]. AI-compute revenue is ~$19M annualised; AI royalties were $1.0M in 9M FY2026 [2].
  • AI licensing momentum is real and lumpy. Record AI licensing at >35% of licensing revenue in Q3 (~$5.7M), 6 of 10 new deals; a NeuPro NPU win with "one of the world's leading PC OEMs" (Q4 FY2025) and a NeuPro-M selection by "a leading global AI and computing platform company" (Q2 FY2026) [1][4][6]. Management's time-to-royalty is 18–24 months; first NPU royalties guided for end-2026/early-2027 [5].
  • The equity already round-tripped the narrative: $17.02 (30 Mar 2026) → $51.60 (22 Jun 2026, the week SOXX peaked) → $25.59. −50% from the high; +4.2% over 12 months vs SOXX +55.2% and QQQ +11.7% [14].
  • The biggest near-term swing factor is a smartphone modem, not AI. Mobile units 82M (+19% YoY), mobile royalties +20%, with a "very significant ramp" guided for Q4 and 2027 [5]. Q4 guidance of $34–38M (midpoint +15% YoY, above the $34.39M all-time high set in Q1 FY2022) depends on it [1][5].
  • Thin profitability, poor cash generation. TTM GAAP operating margin −9.5%, net loss −$10.8M, SBC 17–18% of revenue, TTM FCF $5.7M (4.9%), FY2025 operating cash flow −$3.4M [3][8]. CEVA raised ~$63M net at $19.50 in Nov 2025, explicitly for "potential acquisitions" [7].
  • The balance sheet is the floor: $220.7M cash/deposits/securities, no funded debt, 28% of market cap in net cash, current ratio 10.9x [2][8].
  • Valuation is mid-range on its own history, not cheap: 4.52x EV/sales sits inside a five-year 3.8–7.5x band, for ~34x FY2027E non-GAAP earnings and 93x EV/FCF — on revenue that has compounded at ~1.2% a year since FY2021 ($113.8M → $121.0M FY2026E) [8][12].

Verdict: NEUTRAL — conviction Medium. 12-month fair value ~$28.8 (base case, +13%). Net cash and the modem/NPU royalty inflection put a floor near $19–20; no GAAP profit, 57% China revenue and 4.5x sales on 10% growth cap the upside. I would need Q4 delivery plus a disclosed NPU royalty line to turn constructive.

2. Business overview

What they sell

CEVA licenses silicon and software IP that other companies embed in their own chips. It sells no chips. Three layers, which are also the revenue disclosure:

Layer Products Customer use
Connect (73% of 9M FY2026) Bluetooth (incl. BT 7.0/HDT), Wi-Fi 4/5/6/7, UWB, cellular IoT, 5G/5G-A (PentaG2, PentaG-NTN), 5G RAN (PentaG-RAN, XC22) The radio/baseband of an SoC
Sense (10%) SensPro AI DSPs, MotionEngine sensor-fusion software, RealSpace audio, UWB ranging Motion, audio and position from sensor data
Infer (17%) NeuPro-Nano/Prime/Max/Ultra NPUs, SensPro AI DSPs, NN toolchain On-device CNN/transformer inference

The next-generation NeuPro family launched 28 Oct 2026; general availability Q2 2027, with Ultra taking CEVA into data-centre inference at "hundreds to thousands of TOPS" [2][5].

To whom, and how they get paid

400+ customers — fabless semis, ASIC houses, OEMs and platform companies. Named licensees include Microchip, Renesas, NXP, Sony, Samsung, STMicroelectronics, Realtek, Socionext, Toshiba, Unisoc and Synaptics [3]. FY2025: 54 licensing agreements — 33 consumer, 10 industrial, 7 automotive, 3 PC, 1 infrastructure; 10 direct with OEMs, 12 customers licensed multiple technologies [4].

Three revenue components:

  1. Up-front licence fee, recognised at a point in time — $42.1M of the $52.4M licensing revenue in 9M FY2026 (80%) [2].
  2. Support/services/NRE over time — $10.3M in 9M FY2026 (20% of licensing, ~12% of total). There is no separate NRE line [2].
  3. Per-unit royalty (percentage of chip price or fixed per unit), recognised on shipment — $32.7M in 9M FY2026 (38% of revenue); $46.0M in FY2025 (42%) [2][3].

Structurally, licensing leads and royalties follow 18–24 months later, then persist over a 5–8 year production life. The P&L is therefore a lagging indicator of design wins, and any quarter is hostage to one or two contracts. Q3 FY2026 proved it: a "significant multi-year connectivity agreement" slipped into early Q4, licensing fell 10% sequentially, and FY2026 growth guidance fell from 13–15% to ~11% [5][6].

3. Physical AI exposure

Where it sits in the stack

CEVA occupies two of the five layers — and the three it does not own are where the durable value sits:

SENSING COMPUTE/SILICON DATA SOFTWARE/PERCEPTION SYSTEMS MotionEngine, UWB, NeuPro NPU, SensPro none NN compiler/toolchain none SensPro DSP, AI DSP, baseband DSP RealSpace audio (licensed IP, not chips)

It sells ingredients, not systems. A CEVA NPU is RTL plus software inside someone else's SoC: no device relationship, no data flywheel, no runtime, no developer ecosystem beyond its own toolchain. That is a weaker position than Mobileye's full stack or Arm's instruction-set gravity. The one genuine asset is switching cost at SoC level — re-architecting a baseband or NPU means re-verification, re-qualification and a mask respin, 18–24 months and millions of dollars. That is why 30+ customers license both Bluetooth and Wi-Fi and 40+ have licensed Wi-Fi 6 [5].

% of revenue tied to Physical AI

Management claims CEVA "enables Physical AI" and that 86% of FY2025 revenue came from "smart edge" markets [4]. That is definitional, not measurable: a Bluetooth earbud is not Physical AI. I use a stricter test — IP whose function is machine perception or inference:

Bucket 9M FY2026 (annualised) % Physical AI?
Infer (NPU, vision, AI DSP) ~$19.2M 17% Yes
Sense — perception share (sensor fusion, UWB ranging) ~$3.5M 3% Partly
Sense — motion UI, audio, white goods ~$8.0M 7% No
Connect (BT/Wi-Fi/cellular baseband) ~$82.9M 73% No — a radio is not AI

Today: ~20% (range 17–27%); TTM revenue is $116.4M because Q4 is seasonally the strongest quarter. The top of the range counts all of Sense; MotionEngine in a TV remote is a user interface, not physical intelligence.

Scepticism check. The company's "$170 billion Physical AI and Edge AI TAM by 2030" against $121M of FY2026E revenue implies a 0.07% share of its own TAM [2]. That is not a falsification — IP is a thin layer of a large market — but TAM rhetoric here is decorative. 4. Competitive position & moat

Market position. 68% of the wireless connectivity IP market by revenue in 2025, ranked #1 by IPnest for the third consecutive year [2][5] — the hardest number in the story and the basis of the franchise. >23 billion cumulative devices, 400+ customers, 30+ licensing both Bluetooth and Wi-Fi, 40+ licensed for Wi-Fi 6 [2][5]. There is no comparable share claim in NPUs; management cites "more than 50 NeuPro customers" in prepared remarks and "more than 15" in Q&A on the same call — an inconsistency I treat as unverified [5].

Source of advantage Strength Why
Design-in inertia Strong 2.1B units/yr, 23B cumulative, reference designs, trained engineers — mid-programme swaps are rarely worth it.
Standards time-to-market Strong First-to-market on BT 7.0/HDT, Wi-Fi 6/7 and 5G-A is worth real money to a customer racing a launch.
Portfolio breadth ("combo") Medium-strong The only credible one-stop shop for BT + Wi-Fi + UWB + NPU; content per design is rising.
Price Weak IP is compared on PPA and cost; competitors are "reducing their licensing and royalty fees" [3].
Ecosystem gravity Weak vs Arm Arm has the ISA and developer base; CEVA has blocks. In NPUs, Arm Ethos, Cadence Tensilica/Vision, Imagination and NVDLA contest every socket.
Financial strength Medium $204M net cash is ample — and evidence the model does not self-fund growth.

Competition. Arm (CPU + Ethos NPU, named first in CEVA's own list), Cadence (Tensilica/Vision DSPs), Imagination, VeriSilicon (the closest model analogue: CNY 4.08B revenue, +39% TTM, 14.2x EV/sales [13]), Andes/SiFive/RISC-V (explicitly cited as pricing pressure), Expedera/Quadric/DMP/Cambricon/Ambarella/NVDLA in NPUs, Mindtree in short-range wireless, and in-house teams at NXP, ST, Marvell, Broadcom, Qualcomm and Apple. CEVA's 10-K concedes customers "may choose to license their wireless connectivity or proprietary signal processing IP cores to third parties and, as a result, become direct competitors" [3].

Customer concentration. UNISOC was 15% of total revenue in both FY2025 and FY2024; two royalty customers each ≥10% of royalties, together 39% in FY2025 (46% in FY2024) [3]. In 9M FY2026 one customer was 10% of revenue; in Q3 a different customer was 11% [2]. China is 57% of revenue and 67% of Q3.

Design-cycle sensitivity. A single large licence slipping one quarter moved FY2026 growth guidance ~300bp and, by my read, drove the ~8% one-day drop on 9 Nov 2026 [1][5][15]. Conversely Q4 FY2025's $31.3M and Q2 FY2026's +13% were each driven by one or two deals. CEVA must be valued on a rolling multi-year royalty base, not quarterly licensing prints — which is precisely what the sell-side does not do.

Runway is not a question. $204M of net cash against a TTM burn of effectively zero (FCF +$5.7M) means no solvency or refinancing risk. The live question is what the cash is for: the November 2025 prospectus says proceeds will "fund potential acquisitions of, or investments in, complementary technologies or businesses, although we have no present commitments" [7].

The November 2025 raise is the most informative capital-allocation datapoint. CEVA sold 3,000,000 shares at $19.50 plus a 450,000-share greenshoe — $67.3M gross / ~$63M net — against a $20.16 prior close, while FY2025 operating cash flow was negative [4][7]. As financing it was well timed (the stock is 31% above the issue price); as a signal it says the business does not fund its own R&D and working capital through the cycle.

No convertibles, no warrants, no ATM drawn. A shelf (File No. 333-281323) is in place, so further issuance needs no new filing, but nothing is utilised.

Buyback: no shares repurchased in 2026; 684,486 remain authorised (~2.4% of shares out) [2]. In 9M FY2025 CEVA bought 340,295 shares at an average $21.01 ($7.15M) [2]. Holding powder for M&A at $25.59 is defensible, but it leaves SBC dilution unhedged.

Dilution: weighted diluted shares 24.30M (FY2025) → 28.32M (Q3 FY2026), +16.5%; shares outstanding 28.53M, +15.05% YoY; Q4 guided 30.1M. Three forces: the 3.45M-share offering (+14%), RSU vesting (~2–3%/yr) and option exercises. SBC of ~$20M a year on a $730M market cap is 2.7% of market cap in annual economic dilution. An investor today owns ~11% less of the company than in 2024 for the same revenue base.

14. What would change my mind

Bullish (would move me to Buy): 1. Q4 FY2026 revenue ≥$36M and FY2027 guidance ≥$140M, with mobile royalties above $7M in the quarter — proof the modem ramp is real and the guidance cut was timing. 2. A disclosed NeuPro royalty line of even $1–2M a quarter, plus a named AI-infrastructure customer. This is the one fact that converts "AI licensing" into "AI revenue". 3. Gross margin stabilising at ≥87% while growing >12%, showing the system-level strategy is accretive rather than dilutive. 4. China below 50% of revenue and a second ≥10% customer outside China — that would remove the largest structural discount. 5. A buyback or an accretive cash-funded acquisition — evidence the $204M is being deployed for per-share value.

Bearish (would move me to Sell): 1. FY2027 guidance below $130M, or a second consecutive quarter of licensing below $17M — 3.5x sales ($21) comes into play. 2. Mobile units below 80M/quarter or mobile royalty dollars flat YoY — the modem ramp stalled at one model. 3. NeuPro royalties pushed beyond 2027, or AI-infrastructure licensing revealed as one-off NRE rather than a royalty-bearing platform. 4. SBC above 20% of revenue, or another equity issuance, without a commensurate revenue step-up. 5. A large acquisition at >6x sales funded with stock — the classic value-destruction path for a sub-scale IP licensor.
