1. The setup

  • The second-largest AI franchise in the batch, at 20x forward earnings. Broadcom's TTM revenue is $89.1B (+39.5%), with Q3 FY2026 revenue of $29.59B (+85.5% YoY), EPS $2.68 and operating margin 48.8%. The stock is 28% below its high, flat YTD, and trades at 19.9x forward EPS.
  • The AI semiconductor business is the growth engine: custom accelerators (Google TPU, Meta, ByteDance), networking (Tomahawk/Jericho switches) and optical components. AI revenue is now a material share of the total and growing >60%.
  • VMware is the cash cow: infrastructure software provides recurring, high-margin revenue and funds the dividend/buybacks. The integration has been successful; software margins are ~70%+.
  • The P&L optics are distorted by acquisition accounting: GAAP EPS $7.83 includes amortisation of intangibles; non-GAAP EPS is much higher, which is why the trailing P/E (44x) and forward P/E (20x) diverge.
  • Balance sheet: $24.0B cash, $59.4B debt, net debt $35.4B — investment-grade, comfortably serviced by $39.4B FCF.
  • Sentiment: price at the 50-DMA ($345) but below the 200-DMA ($367); short interest 1.1%; average target $529. The stock is a consensus AI long that has de-rated with the sector.

2. Business and moat

Broadcom sells semiconductors (custom ASICs, networking, wireless, broadband, storage) and infrastructure software (VMware, CA, Symantec enterprise). The moat is design-win lock-in: custom AI chips are co-designed with hyperscalers over years, and networking silicon is the industry standard. VMware has high switching costs.

The risk is customer concentration (a few hyperscalers) and the possibility that AI capex shifts to in-house/merchant GPUs, bypassing Broadcom's custom-ASIC model.
