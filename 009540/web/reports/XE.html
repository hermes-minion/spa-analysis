X-Energy, Inc. (NASDAQ: XE) — Equity Research Note

Price: $11.08 (Nasdaq close, 13 Nov 2026) · Market cap: $4.50B · EV: $2.97B · EV/Sales TTM: 16.9x · FCF: -$489M · Net cash: $1.53B Verdict: Neutral (Medium conviction) · 12-month probability-weighted value: ~$12 (+10%) Screening note; data as of 13-Nov-2026. Research, not investment advice.

1. The setup

  • A post-IPO nuclear de-rating, not a business failure. X-energy IPO'd on 24-Apr-2026 at $23/share (44.25M Class A shares, upsized); the stock now trades at $11.08, 62% below the IPO and 69% below its post-IPO high. The entire advanced-nuclear complex has been sold off as rates rose and AI-power hype cooled.
  • There is real revenue, unlike most SMR peers. TTM revenue is $175.4M (+86% vs FY2025), driven by TRISO fuel and government/industrial contracts. Q3 2026 revenue was $51.6M (+95% YoY) and Q2 was $50.1M — a genuine two-quarter run-rate.
  • But the P&L is deeply unprofitable: gross margin is negative (-72.9%), operating margin -194%, net loss -$190M TTM, FCF -$489M (heavy capex on the TRISO-X fuel facility). The company is building a fuel supply chain before the reactors exist.
  • The balance sheet funds the build: $1.56B cash, $27M debt, net cash $1.53B — about three years of runway at the current burn. The IPO proceeds were sized for exactly this.
  • The strategic assets are the Xe-100 (80 MW high-temperature gas-cooled reactor), the TRISO-X fuel plant and a DOE cost-share programme. Amazon is an investor and offtake partner; Dow's Seadrift site is the lead industrial deployment. These are real agreements, but not yet a booked order book.
  • Sentiment: no 200-DMA (listed April 2026), price below the 50-DMA, short interest low. The IPO lock-up has expired or is expiring — a supply overhang.

2. Business and moat

X-energy designs small modular high-temperature gas-cooled reactors (Xe-100) and manufactures TRISO fuel through TRISO-X. Revenue today comes from fuel sales, engineering services and government contracts; the long-term model is selling reactors and/or fuel to industrial customers (Dow, Amazon) and utilities.

The moat is (1) TRISO fuel IP and the only US commercial-scale TRISO plant — fuel is the recurring revenue and the bottleneck; (2) DOE partnership and licensing progress; (3) industrial offtake relationships (Dow Seadrift, Amazon). The risks are regulatory timelines (NRC licensing), construction cost overruns, and the fact that no Xe-100 has been built commercially yet.
