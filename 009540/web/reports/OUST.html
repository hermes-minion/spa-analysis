0. Classification (mandatory)

Profile C — growth, near adjusted-EBITDA break-even. Q3 FY2026 revenue $59.3M, GAAP gross margin 42.6%, operating margin −32.5% and FCF −$35.0M; net cash $404.4M. The company is not pre-revenue, but GAAP profitability is not close; the equity is valued on EV/sales and unit economics. Business model: digital-lidar hardware plus Perception/BlueCity software, sold to industrial, robotics, automotive and defence customers. Metrics that matter: units, ASP, gross margin, adjusted EBITDA and cash burn.

1. Executive summary

  • The growth is real, and it is faster than any peer. Q3 FY2026 revenue $59.3M (+50.1% YoY, +8.6% QoQ), the 15th consecutive quarter of product-revenue growth, on >27,000 sensors shipped (≈7,000 lidar + 20,000 cameras) — both records (Q3 FY2026 release, 3 Nov 2026). Q4 guidance is $61–64M, implying FY2026 revenue of $223.5–226.5M (+32–34% total, +~48% ex-royalties). Consensus FY2027 is $299.5M (+34.7%) (stockanalysis).
  • But the reported growth is not what it looks like. FY2025 included $22.8M of patent royalties at 100% gross margin, of which $16.1M was a one-time catch-up booked in Q4 FY2025 (FY2025 10-K). TTM royalties are $26.7M, 11.9% of revenue — so FY2026's +33% headline is really +48% on product. Strip the royalties and the product gross margin is 39.1%, not 46.3%. That is the single most important adjustment in this file.
  • Lidar unit volume is roughly flat; the incremental growth is cameras and systems. FY2025 shipped >25,000 lidar units for $146.6M of product revenue ≈ $5,860 per lidar. Q3 FY2026 shipped ~7,000 lidar — fewer than Q4 FY2025's 8,100 — alongside 20,000 Stereolabs cameras. The stereovision acquisition (closed 4 Feb 2026, $32.4M cash + 1.85M shares) is now a material and under-modelled part of the story.
  • Operating leverage is appearing in opex, not gross margin. GAAP gross margin was 42.6% in Q3 FY2026 vs 42.2% a year earlier and ~39% underlying in Q2 FY2026 (ex the $5.4M tariff refund). Opex grew 8.8% against 50% revenue growth, so GAAP operating margin improved from −61.4% to −32.5%. Adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed to −$3.4M (−$8.4M a year ago) — adjusted EBITDA breakeven is ~$66.6M/quarter, i.e. roughly Q1 2027. GAAP operating breakeven is ~$103M/quarter, i.e. 2028.
  • The balance sheet is strong; the dilution habit is not. $421.2M cash and investments, $15.4M of debt (all leases), $404.4M net cash = 16.5% of market cap. But the company has raised ~$385M across three equity facilities in 18 months ($95.6M at $20.88, $98.0M at $27.40, $191.8M at $55.22) and shares are +21.8% YoY (59.95M → 73.00M). Authorised shares were doubled from 100M to 200M on 17 Jun 2026. Management now says it does "not expect to need additional capital."
  • The valuation already discounts a lot. At $33.48 the stock trades at 9.1x EV/TTM sales, 9.1x EV/FY2026E sales, 6.8x EV/FY2027E sales and 26.3x EV/product gross profit. That is 3.6x Hesai's 2.5x despite Hesai being 2.4x larger, and near the top of Ouster's own post-SPAC range (P/S 3.8x in FY2023, 5.5x FY2024, 7.7x FY2025, 10.9x now). Free cash flow is −$97.5M TTM (−43% margin).
  • Concentration and competition are unresolved. One customer (Customer E) was 25% of 9M FY2026 revenue (26% in 9M FY2025) — persistent, not transient (10-Q Note 2). Benchmark Electronics is a single-source manufacturer at 25% of purchases. Hesai and RoboSense compete on price at 1.9–2.5x EV/sales and 2–4x the unit volume.

Verdict: NEUTRAL — conviction Medium. This is the best-executing pure-play Western lidar franchise, with a genuine technology lead in native-colour lidar and a regulatory moat (NDAA/BABA/Blue UAS) that excludes Chinese competitors from US defence and drone procurement. It is also a 9x-sales, cash-burning hardware company whose volume growth is now coming from cameras rather than lidar, trading at a 3.6x premium to the scale leader. Probability-weighted fair value is ~$32.6 — about 3% below today's price. I would want either ~$22–24 (≈6x FY2027 sales) or two quarters of positive adjusted EBITDA before turning constructive.

Ouster sells sensing and perception hardware plus software to machines that need to see. Four product families:

To whom, and how they get paid

Sales are direct and through distributors to OEMs, integrators, government DOTs and defence primes. Revenue is almost entirely product revenue recognised at shipment ($157.0M of $162.5M in 9M FY2026, 96.6%); software is bundled into hardware or sold as a term licence and is not separately disclosed. The one non-product line is patent royalties.

The royalty line — read this before any gross-margin chart

Ouster collects royalties under long-term IP licences, "primarily in the Asia and Pacific region." The FY2025 10-K discloses that the Hesai arbitration tribunal ruled on 15 Sep 2025 that Ouster is bound by the 2020 Velodyne–Hesai global cross-licence, which "requires Hesai Photonics to pay royalties" (10-Q Note 7; Ouster separately paid $6.4M of arbitration fees). The royalties are therefore, with high confidence, paid by Ouster's largest Chinese competitor — a genuine IP asset, but one the company itself says is non-core: "we do not consider this revenue to be reflective of the core operations of our business… we expect that the amounts recognized in the year ended December 31, 2025 will likely exceed amounts recognized in future annual periods" (FY2025 10-K, MD&A). Management guides ~$3M/quarter in Q4 FY2026.

3. Physical AI exposure

Where it sits in the stack

SENSING COMPUTE / SILICON DATA SOFTWARE / PERCEPTION SYSTEMS / DEPLOYMENT Lidar (own digital ZED Box edge AI None. No REM-like Gemini, BlueCity, None. Sells components SPAD SoC + VCSEL) computers on NVIDIA crowdsourced map; ZED SDK, Isaac ROS and software; does not Cameras (Stereolabs Jetson Orin / Thor no proprietary integration, ML operate fleets or stereo + mono) fleet data asset training pipeline networks

Ouster occupies three of five layers — and is anchored in the bottom one. That is the honest read. It is a sensing company that has bolted on compute and perception software to raise content per machine. It has no data network effect (unlike Mobileye's REM), no silicon of its own beyond the lidar SoC (the ZED Box uses NVIDIA silicon), and no deployment/systems business.

The strategic logic of the Stereolabs deal is precisely to escape the sensor commodity trap: "if NVIDIA is the brain of a robot, then we are the eyes and the visual cortex" (Pacala, Q3 call). That framing is honest about the position — eyes, not brain.

Reasoning for the 3-year figure. Lidar units grow from ~28k in FY2026 to ~55k by FY2029 at roughly flat $6,000 ASP against capacity of 100k units at Benchmark by end-2027 — that is the company's own capacity guide, heavily haircut. Cameras scale from ~$28M to ~$120M as Stereolabs OEM wins convert; the Q3 run-rate of 20,000 units/quarter annualises to 80,000 at ~$350, i.e. ~$28M, and management says the step-change in demand is structural. Software is my estimate of BlueCity/Gemini reaching ~8% of revenue as DOT contracts mature; Ouster has never broken it out. I would not underwrite more than 60% of that software number.

So: ~97% of revenue is tied to Physical AI today (88% shipped product, 12% lidar patent royalties), and ~96–98% in three years — the mix barely changes because the whole company is Physical AI. The more useful question is the sub-split: roughly 75% of revenue is from machines that move (industrial, robotics, automotive) and ~20–25% from fixed infrastructure, and that ratio is unlikely to shift much.

Scepticism check. "Physical AI" is doing heavy marketing work here — the phrase appears in the first sentence of every press release. But unlike most claimants, Ouster's revenue is genuinely 100% physical-AI product: there is no legacy non-AI business to strip out, no services arm, no licensing model to net off. The problem is not purity; it is size, margin and price. A $225M-revenue sensor company at 9x sales needs the unit economics to work, and today they do not: 39% product gross margin, negative $97M free cash flow.

4. Competitive position & moat

Market position

There is no reliable third-party share data for non-automotive lidar, and I will not invent one. The observable facts:

  • Ouster: ~28k lidar units/year, ~$190M lidar revenue, 46% blended / 39% product gross margin, 9.1x EV/sales.
  • Hesai (HSAI): $538M TTM revenue (+31% in CNY), 40.4% gross margin, $48M net income, EV $1.36B, 2.50x EV/sales (stockanalysis, 13 Nov 2026). Roughly 2.4x Ouster's revenue at 0.27x the multiple.
  • RoboSense (2498.HK): ~HKD 2.51B revenue (~$322M), 24.5% gross margin, loss-making, EV HKD 4.87B ≈ $625M, 1.94x EV/sales.
  • Luminar: bankrupt. Now LAZRQ at $0.018, market cap $1.5M, EV $95M, revenue $66M, gross margin −118% (stockanalysis). This is the most important competitive datapoint in the sector: the automotive lidar TAM did not materialise at the assumed price points, and the best-capitalised Western pure-play went to zero.
  • Aeva: $24.7M revenue, 31% gross margin, 28x EV/sales (FMCW, mostly defence/industrial).
  • Innoviz: $50M revenue, ~0% gross margin, 0.57x EV/sales — effectively a distressed asset.

The moat: narrower than the narrative, wider than the bear case

Source of advantage Strength Why
NDAA / BABA / Blue UAS compliance Strong Chinese lidar is excluded from US federal, defence and drone procurement. Ouster has Rev7 Blue UAS certification and NDAA/BABA-compliant Rev8, and is explicitly marketing it (GeoCue, Seneca, Forterra). This is a regulatory moat that no amount of Chinese cost advantage crosses. It is the single most durable thing Ouster owns.
Digital lidar architecture (SPAD SoC + VCSEL) Medium-strong Two chips instead of hundreds of discrete parts; cost curve tracks CMOS. Real, patented, and the reason Ouster can sell at $5–6k with 39% product margin. But the PTAB invalidated three of four challenged patents (one survived, IPR2023-01422) — the IP estate is less impregnable than the pitch.
Native colour (Rev8) Medium, decaying First-to-market fused RGB+3D is genuinely differentiated for survey, agriculture, defence and mapping. But it is an engineering lead, not a physical law; expect 12–24 months before a Chinese competitor ships something comparable.
Ruggedness / reliability Medium Real and hard-won (the "only thing still working after the robot crashed" anecdotes). Matters in mining, ports, defence.
Switching costs Weak-to-medium A lidar is a component. Integration takes 6–24 months, but re-sourcing is routine and Chinese vendors actively design out incumbent sensors. There is no software lock-in comparable to Mobileye's REM or a CUDA-style toolchain.
ZED SDK developer ecosystem Medium Hundreds of thousands of robotics developers use ZED; Isaac ROS native support helps. A modest network effect, not a fortress.

Customer concentration. Customer E was 25% of 9M FY2026 revenue and 15% of Q3; it was 26% of 9M FY2025. Two customers exceeded 10% in FY2025. Customer E is not named in any filing — unverified. A 25%-of-revenue customer with no long-term contract disclosed is the largest single fundamental risk in the equity.

Supplier concentration. Supplier B (Benchmark Electronics, named in the risk factors) was 25% of Q3 FY2026 purchases and 52% of accounts payable. Single-source contract manufacturing is a real execution risk during a Rev8 ramp that is supposed to 3.5x capacity by end-2027.

What the numbers say

  1. Revenue has compounded at 43% over three years ($83M → $111M → $169M → ~$225M) — genuinely rare in hardware. Fifteen consecutive quarters of product growth is an execution record, not luck.
  2. Gross margin has stopped improving. 47.8% in FY2025 was royalty-inflated; the underlying 2026 run-rate is ~42–43% GAAP / ~47% non-GAAP, flat year-on-year through a 50% revenue increase. There is no gross-margin operating leverage — the company says it is "committed" to a 35–40% long-term gross margin framework, which is below where it trades today and a tacit admission that mix is getting worse, not better, as cameras and systems grow.
  3. All the leverage is in opex, and it is real. Q3 FY2026 GAAP opex $44.5M, +8.8% YoY against +50% revenue. FY2026 opex is guided +5–8%. This is why operating margin went from −61% to −33%.
  4. The breakeven math is now specific. Adjusted EBITDA breakeven ≈ $66.6M/quarter at a 46.7% incremental margin — i.e. Q1 FY2027. GAAP operating breakeven ≈ $103M/quarter at 43% gross margin — i.e. ~2028, and that is before the $46M/yr SBC burden. Investors who treat "adjusted EBITDA positive" as "profitable" are being sold a $0.63/share non-cash addback.
  5. Cash conversion is poor and getting worse. Q3 FY2026 operating cash flow −$29.4M against a $14.2M net loss: a ~$15M working-capital drag from inventory (+$11.0M in 9M) and receivables, plus $5.7M capex. FCF −$35.0M in the quarter is the worst since Q4 FY2025's building purchase. Growth is consuming cash.
  6. The FY2025 "record" was an accounting event. Q4 FY2025's +$11M adjusted EBITDA and +$4M GAAP net income exist because $16.1M of previously deferred royalties were recognised once the Hesai arbitration resolved. Absent that, FY2025 adjusted EBITDA was roughly −$23M and the company has never had a profitable quarter.

Runway

At the TTM free-cash burn of −$97.5M and $404.4M of net cash, runway is ~4.1 years — but that understates the risk, because the burn is rising (Q3 FY2026 alone was −$35.0M as inventory and Rev8 tooling build) and because the 100,000-unit capacity expansion at Benchmark will consume capex through 2027. Management's claim that it will "not need additional capital to achieve our operating objectives" is credible only if adjusted EBITDA turns positive in Q1–Q2 2027 and working capital stops absorbing cash. I would treat that claim as a MANAGEMENT CLAIM, not a fact — this management team has raised equity in each of the last two years.

14. What would change my mind

Bullish signals (would move me to Buy): 1. Two consecutive quarters of positive adjusted EBITDA and positive operating cash flow — Q4 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027 — without a working-capital build. That would prove the model scales and retire the dilution risk. 2. Lidar units above 10,000/quarter with Rev8 more than half the mix and gross margin ≥47% non-GAAP. This is the specific test of whether Rev8 is a genuine step-up in value (native colour at a premium) or just a product cycle that dilutes margin. 3. A named US defence programme of record, or a Tier-1/OEM design win, worth >$50M — the NDAA/BABA moat converted into contracted dollars. This is the highest-quality version of the bull case and the one the market is not pricing. 4. BlueCity/Gemini disclosed at >$50M annualised — proof that the software attach is real and that revenue can become recurring rather than lumpy. 5. A price below ~$22–24 (≈6x FY2027 sales, ≈18x product gross profit) with the growth thesis intact. Cheapness alone is not a thesis, but at 6x sales the risk/reward inverts.

Bearish signals (would move me to Sell): 1. Gross margin below 40% GAAP for two consecutive quarters as Rev8 and cameras ramp — proof that mix is structurally dilutive and the 35–40% framework is a ceiling, not a floor. 2. A fourth equity facility (new ATM or offering) before adjusted EBITDA turns positive — the "no additional capital" claim fails, and 20%/yr dilution continues into a de-rating. 3. Customer E declining below 20% of revenue without replacement — the concentration risk materialising in the worst way. 4. Rev8 shipments below "thousands" in Q4 FY2026, or the 100k-unit Benchmark capacity slipping past 2027 — the ramp is the whole 2027 story. 5. Royalty revenue falling below $2M/quarter while gross margin does not improve — confirms that the 2025–26 margin profile was an artefact.
