2. Business Model

How candidates are identified. Lantern does not run large discovery programs. It in-licenses / re-acquires known small molecules (often discontinued or shelved by prior owners), then uses RADR to pick an indication and a biomarker-defined subpopulation where the molecule may be active; it then runs small, cheap trials (16-110 patients), frequently with academic investigator sponsorship (e.g., the bladder trial is run by Rigshospitalet, Copenhagen). Legacy context [VERIFIED]: LP-100 (irofulven) global rights were reacquired from Allarity in 2021; LP-300 is repositioned BNP7787/Tavocept (originally developed by BioNumerik; two legacy Phase 3 trials 2001-2010, NCT00039780 / NCT00966914); LP-184 and LP-284 are acylfulvene-class molecules (illudin lineage). Exact license terms for LP-300: data unavailable in reviewed filings.

RADR platform. Per FY2025 10-K: ">200 billion oncology-focused data points, >130,000 patient records, >200 ML algorithms"; purpose = biomarker/gene-signature discovery, drug positioning, patient selection [MGMT CLAIM -- unaudited self-reported scale]. Concrete outputs actually visible in the clinic [VERIFIED]: (a) PTGR1-overexpression hypothesis for LP-184 (now embedded in trials and patents); (b) EGFR ex21 L858R enrichment hypothesis for LP-300 (now the basis of the amended HARMONIC design); (c) the allowed 3-gene selection patent (PTGR1/PTPN14/ASPH, USPTO Notice of Allowance 23-Jul-2026, App 17/230,821). Public evidence on the platform itself remains: publications mostly preclinical, mid-tier journals, heavy internal authorship (Bhatia/Biyani/Kathad/Sharma); no blinded external benchmark of RADR predictions; no third-party customers for the core RADR engine.

Is there a genuine competitive advantage? [OPINION] No -- not at platform level. 4. Clinical Data Quality

Checklist applied to every material dataset (the brief's questions, answered bluntly):

(A) LP-300 HARMONIC L858R data (mPFS 8.4-8.9 mo, HR 0.37). - Sample size statistically meaningful? No. n=16 (n=9 completers); CIs wide (HR CI 0.15-0.89 spans a 6x range). - Control arm? No longer (was randomized; control discontinued after FDA Type C). - Clinically meaningful endpoint? PFS yes in principle; but in single-arm form it needs a benchmark; the benchmark here is historical and cross-trial. - Durable? Partially -- 1 CR >2 years; >70% tumor reduction in evaluable; DOR data immature. - Patient/biomarker selection explanatory? Plausibly: L858R is the stated enrichment; multivariable analysis addresses (not removes) selection effects. - Statistically significant? The HR 0.37 CI excludes 1, but it is an exploratory subgroup comparison, not a powered test. - Bias sources? Single-arm + historical control + small n + potentially favorable site mix; survivorship in "6-cycle completers" (completing cycles selects better-prognosis patients -- a classic immortal-time-type bias). - Abstract or peer-reviewed? Conference/PR only (data furnished as 8-K exhibit); design paper in Clinical Lung Cancer (May-2025). Independent replication: none. - Missing/immature? OS immature; DOR not quantified; randomized-arm data never disclosed. Where management may be more optimistic than the evidence: presenting "mPFS 8.9 (n=9 completers) vs 8.4 (n=16)" emphasizes the smaller, more selected subset; the company itself concedes medians "can move up or down". The frame "benefit deepens with treatment duration" is selection-sensitive.

(B) LP-184 Phase 1a (SD 40%, no ORR, RP2D 0.39). - Sample meaningful? N=63 across 12 dose levels -> per-dose n small; single-arm FIH is a safety read, not efficacy. - Control arm? No. Endpoint meaningful? Safety/MTD/RP2D yes; DCR is soft in a heterogeneous refractory population. - Durable? 3 SDs >12 months, notable for the mix. - Selection? Enriched enrollment of DDR-prevalent tumor types (stated in CT.gov); responders reportedly DDR-altered -- hypothesis-consistent, not hypothesis-proving. - Significant? No formal efficacy test. - Bias? Reporting focus on the at/above-dose subset; the company's DCR figure moved from 54% (Oct-2025 PR) to 45% (Jul-2026 PR) for that subset -- small-n volatility or definitional drift, either way a yellow flag on narrative stability. Zero CRs/PRs as monotherapy. - Peer-reviewed? Yes (Invest New Drugs, 7-Jul-2026) -- the strongest evidence artifact in the company. - Replicating? The new bladder/TNBC cohorts are the prospective test. Management optimism delta: DCR framing (when best response was SD-only); "exceptional sensitivity" language (Q2 call) vs n=1-subgroups.

(C) LP-284 (1 CMR). Single-patient anecdote; selection bias near-certain (best case published first); conference (LL&M Congress) not peer-reviewed; no cohort denominator disclosed; durability update overdue. Treat as signal of tolerability + anecdote, not efficacy.

(D) Cross-cutting. No randomized data exists anywhere in the portfolio; every efficacy dataset is small-n, single-arm and exploratory; OS is immature everywhere; no independent replication of any efficacy claim; all company PRs carry forward-looking disclaimers explicitly conceding small cohorts and that cross-trial comparisons "should not be interpreted as direct evidence of comparative safety or efficacy" (Q2-26 slides).
