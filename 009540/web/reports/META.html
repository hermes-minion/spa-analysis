Meta Platforms, Inc. (NASDAQ: META) — Equity Research Note

Research, not investment advice.

1. The setup

  • The cheapest quality mega-cap after Alphabet. META trades at 19.5x forward EPS and 14.4x EV/EBITDA while growing revenue >20% with an 81% gross margin. The stock is 19% below its high and flat YTD — a de-rating driven by AI-capex fears, not by ad-revenue weakness.
  • Q3 2026 revenue was $64.6B, +26% YoY, with net income of $9.86B (EPS $3.83). The 15% net margin is below the 31% TTM average on higher costs/one-offs, but net income still rose sharply YoY off a depressed Q3-2025 base; advertising momentum remains strong.
  • The capex cycle is the debate. TTM FCF is $28.6B (11.9% margin) versus $46-54B in 2023-25, as Meta spends aggressively on AI data centers and GPUs. The market is questioning the return on that spend — the same debate hitting Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon.
  • The franchise is a cash machine at the operating level: TTM operating margin 37.8%, net income $75.2B, EPS $29.33, buybacks reducing shares 0.8% YoY. Advertising is a toll road on global attention, and Reels/WhatsApp monetisation is still scaling.
  • Balance sheet: $82.5B cash/investments, $114.8B debt (largely low-coupon), net debt $32.4B — manageable against $75B of net income.
  • Sentiment: below the 50-DMA ($680), above the 200-DMA ($631); 2.95% of shares short; the average price target is $850. The stock is a consensus long that has been given a valuation reset.

2. Business and moat

Meta sells advertising across Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger and Threads. Revenue is ~98% advertising; Reality Labs (VR/AR) is a loss-making option. The moat is the largest social graph and the most sophisticated ad-targeting/measurement engine outside Google, plus 3B+ daily users.

AI is both a cost and a product: recommendation models improve engagement (Reels, feed), and generative tools lower ad-production costs. Meta's Llama models are open-weight, which builds developer mindshare but not direct revenue. The risk is regulatory (EU DMA, US youth-safety rules, privacy) and the possibility that AI assistants disintermediate social feeds.

Scenario math. Forward EPS ≈ $32.9. Bear: ad growth slows to 10% and AI capex stays high, EPS $30, multiple 17x → $510. Base: growth 18-20%, capex peaks in 2027, EPS $33, multiple 22x → $726. Bull: Reels/WhatsApp monetisation plus AI ad tools, EPS $36, multiple 26x → $936. Probabilities 25/45/30 → ~$735 (+15%).

Meta is the best combination of growth, margin and multiple among the mega-caps. The FCF trough is the risk, not the franchise.

5. Catalysts

  • Q4 2026 results (late Jan 2027): 2027 capex guidance and ad-revenue growth.
  • WhatsApp/Threads monetisation — the next leg of ad inventory.
  • AI products: Llama releases, Meta AI assistant usage, business AI agents.
  • Buybacks: ~$60-70B annual capacity at current FCF.
  • Regulatory: EU DMA, US youth-safety legislation.
