0. Classification (mandatory)

Profile B — profitable but cyclical. TTM revenue $6.37B, gross margin 41.4% (up from a 36.0% trough), FCF $1.62B (25.4%) and minimal net debt. The P&L is mid-recovery rather than at a peak, so trailing multiples still flatter the cycle; I anchor on mid-cycle EPS. Business model: IDM/fab-lite — own fabs plus foundry — selling power, analog, sensor and SiC devices into automotive, industrial and AI data centre. Metrics that matter: EV/EBITDA, P/E on mid-cycle EPS, utilisation, inventory days and FCF.

1. Executive summary

  • The cyclical inflection is real and is now in the numbers. Q3 FY2026 revenue $1,722.3M (+11.1% YoY, +7% QoQ), GAAP gross margin 41.4% (the fifth consecutive quarterly expansion, +190bp QoQ), GAAP operating margin 22.0%, non-GAAP EPS $0.90 (+43% YoY) and free cash flow $489M (28% of revenue) (Q3 FY2026 release, 2 Nov 2026; 10-Q). Q4 guidance of $1.715–1.815B implies +15.3% YoY at the midpoint — the fastest growth since mid-2022 and a clean break from ten consecutive quarters of decline.
  • But the fastest-growing business is not Physical AI. AI data-centre revenue was $180.2M in Q3 (+175% YoY, +39% QoQ), 10.5% of revenue, and management guides ~$620M for FY2026 (+140%) and "double" in 2027 (~$1.24B) (Q3 call, 2 Nov 2026). That is power conversion for GPU racks — digital-AI infrastructure, not AI that perceives and acts in the physical world. Counting it as Physical AI would be the single easiest way to inflate this company's purity, and I do not.
  • Physical AI is ~16% of revenue today, and that is the honest number. Intelligent Sensing Group (image sensors, SiPM, iToF) was $235.1M, or 13.6% of Q3 revenue, plus roughly 3% of PSG/AMG revenue shipped into ADAS, robotics and drone power. My estimate for FY2029 is ~21% (range 18–24%) as sensing outgrows the corporate average, robotics/drones scale from a small base and Synaptics' Astra edge-AI MCUs consolidate from mid-2027. Reasoning is shown in §3; it is an estimate, not a disclosure.
  • Automotive — 45% of revenue — is still shrinking. Auto was $776.7M in Q3, −1% YoY and −1% QoQ, because management deliberately reallocated constrained supply to AI data centre and industrial, and guided auto down high-single-digits in Q4 before normalising in Q1 2027 (10-Q MD&A; Q3 call). Utilisation is 83%, inventory 186 days (down 6), distributor inventory 9.9 weeks. Management calls this timing, not share loss — a claim I accept directionally but cannot verify.
  • The Synaptics acquisition is the largest strategic variable and it is under-appreciated. Revised on 1 Oct 2026 from $7B all-stock to $5.7B all-cash ($123/share), closing mid-2027, funded with cash on hand plus a committed $2.45B senior secured term loan (10-Q; Quartz/Yahoo, 2 Oct 2026). It buys a genuine edge-AI asset (Astra NPU-enabled MCUs) at ~4.75x sales, but takes onsemi from ~net-debt-neutral to an estimated ~$6.3B net debt (~2.8x pro forma EBITDA) and pauses buybacks.
  • Valuation is mid-cycle, not distressed. At $71.06 the stock is on 4.2x FY2026E EV/sales, 21.6x FY2026E non-GAAP EPS ($3.29) and 15.0x FY2027E ($4.74 consensus), with a 5.9% FCF yield. That is rich versus STMicro (2.9x EV/sales) and cheap versus Texas Instruments (13.6x), and it sits in the top third of its own five-year EV/sales range but only the 50th percentile of its forward P/E range — because margins are still 1,200bp below the 53% target.
  • Position in the watchlist: a high-quality, fab-lite power-semiconductor cyclical with a real sensing franchise and a fast-growing AI-power kicker — not a Physical AI pure play.

Verdict: NEUTRAL — conviction Medium. 12–18 month fair value ~$78 (base case), ~10% above the current price. The earnings cycle, margin trajectory and AI-power ramp are all pointing the right way; the auto destocking, the Synaptics leverage and a 4.3x sales multiple cap the risk/reward. I would turn constructive below ~$60 or on evidence that auto re-accelerates while gross margin holds above 43%.

2. Business overview

What they sell

onsemi sells power semiconductors and sensing silicon — components, not systems or software. Three reportable segments (Q3 FY2026, 10-Q):

Segment Q3 FY2026 revenue % of total What it is Gross margin
Power Solutions Group (PSG) $889.9M 51.7% SiC MOSFETs/modules (EliteSiC), silicon MOSFETs, IGBTs, GaN, discrete power, SiC JFETs 31.4% (9M)
Analog & Mixed-Signal Group (AMG) $597.3M 34.7% Gate drivers, PMICs, controllers, sensors, connectivity (10BASE-T1S), signal conditioning 52.3% (9M)
Intelligent Sensing Group (ISG) $235.1M 13.6% CMOS image sensors, image signal processors, SiPM (lidar), indirect time-of-flight 37.3% (9M)
Total $1,722.3M 100% 41.4%

The strategic pivot since 2020 has been to exit commodity discrete businesses, concentrate on automotive and industrial, and shift manufacturing to a fab-lite model: capex was 1.6% of revenue in Q3 and is guided to ~2% for FY2026, versus 18.6% in FY2023 (10-Q; StockAnalysis cash flow). The company closed the sale of a Philippines back-end plant in Q3 and is exiting the Mountaintop fab by end-2027 (Q3 call), and on 6 Nov 2026 cancelled a planned $2B silicon-carbide expansion in Rožnov, Czech Republic (Investing.com/Yahoo, 6 Nov 2026). Management's own explanation — cheaper Chinese wafers make owning capacity uneconomic — is as good a summary as any of the SiC pricing environment.

To whom, and how they get paid

Essentially all revenue is product sales (~100%; NRE/product-development agreements were 1% a year ago). Sales run through distributors (54% of FY2025 revenue) and direct OEM/Tier-1 customers (46%); one distributor accounted for 11% of FY2025 revenue and 15% of Q3 FY2026 revenue (10-K; 10-Q). No end-OEM is disclosed above 10%.

3. Physical AI exposure

Where it sits in the stack

onsemi occupies the bottom two layers of the physical-AI stack and nothing above them:

SENSING COMPUTE / SILICON DATA SOFTWARE/PERCEPTION SYSTEMS Image sensors (ADAS, Power delivery for AI none none none machine vision) compute (SiC/GaN/Si) SiPM / iToF depth Motor drive & power for robots, drones, EVs ▲ └── Synaptics Astra (from mid-2027): edge-AI MCUs with NPU — the only "compute" asset

It is a component supplier to the perceivers and actors, not a perception-system vendor. That is a different position from Mobileye (full stack) or a humanoid maker: onsemi sells the camera that sees and the transistor that moves, and its content per robot or vehicle is measured in dollars to low hundreds of dollars, not thousands.

% of revenue tied to Physical AI

onsemi does not disclose Physical-AI revenue, so this is my build. I define Physical AI strictly: silicon whose end application perceives the environment and acts on it (ADAS/AV, robots, drones, machine vision). I exclude AI data centre (digital infrastructure), EV traction power (electrification), grid/ESS (energy infrastructure) and consumer/medical sensing.

Three-year view (FY2029E): ~21% (range 18–24%). Reasoning: (i) total revenue ~$8.8B, a ~10% CAGR from $6.6B, in line with management's "10 points above the market" Investor Day framing and consensus's 12.4% three-year growth; (ii) ISG grows ~11%/yr to ~$1.30B (14.8% of revenue) as ADAS camera content per vehicle rises and machine vision recovers — 85% counted, so ~12.6% of revenue; (iii) power into robotics/ADAS/drones roughly doubles to ~$0.44B (5.0%), driven by humanoid motor drive and drone power; (iv) Synaptics Astra adds ~$0.20B (2.3%) of edge-AI MCU revenue post-close. Sum ≈ 20–21%. If humanoids surprise, 24%; if ISG's industrial recovery stalls, 18%.

What this means. onsemi is a power-semiconductor cyclical with a real but minority Physical-AI franchise. The bull case that matters for the next 12 months is AI data-centre power and the auto/industrial cycle — not robotics.

Side-note: Synaptics (SYNA — separate ticker)

The watchlist pairs "ON Semiconductor / Synaptics"; they are now the same trade. onsemi agreed on 25 Jun 2026 to acquire Synaptics, revised on 1 Oct 2026 to $123.00/share cash, ~$5.7B, closing mid-2027 (10-Q). SYNA trades at $118.14 (13 Nov 2026), a 4.1% discount to the cash price — the market assigns a high completion probability. Synaptics brings ~$1.20B of TTM revenue at a 46% gross margin and, importantly, the Astra SL-series, an NPU-enabled MCU family aimed at edge AI in consumer, automotive and industrial devices (StockAnalysis SYNA). It is also a low-growth (9.5% three-year consensus), consumer-skewed business bought at ~4.75x sales — strategically logical, financially full.

4. Competitive position & moat

Market position

  • SiC power: onsemi is one of four credible Western suppliers (with Infineon, STMicroelectronics, Wolfspeed and ROHM), vertically integrated from SiC crystal growth through modules, and claims wins at NIO (1,200V traction inverter for a flagship sedan) and Geely's ZEEKR export models, plus silicon-carbide and silicon MOSFETs in Google AI data-centre PSUs (Q3 call). onsemi does not disclose SiC revenue or share; any "top-2 in SiC" statement should be treated as a management claim.
  • Automotive imaging: ISG is one of a handful of automotive-qualified image-sensor suppliers (with Sony, Omnivision and Samsung), and in Sep 2026 partnered with Valens on a cost-optimised 3MP A-PHY sensor for high-volume auto cameras (Valens/Yahoo, 22 Sep 2026). Management claims "market-leading positions in automotive and industrial" (10-K); I could not verify a specific share figure and flag it as unverified.
  • Analog/power breadth: the 10-K names Texas Instruments, ADI, Infineon, STMicro, Renesas, MPS and NXP as AMG competitors — a crowded field where onsemi is a specialist, not the scale leader.

The moat: real, but narrower than the narrative

Source of advantage Strength Why
Qualification and design-in cycle Strong AEC-Q100/ISO 26262 qualification plus 2–4 years to SOP and 5–10 years of production creates procedural switching costs. This is the single best defence in auto semis.
SiC vertical integration Medium-strong Owning crystal growth through module (and the Qorvo SiC JFET technology bought for $118.8M in Jan 2025) supports cost and supply control. But Chinese substrate capacity is collapsing wafer prices — the reason the $2B Czech expansion was cancelled.
Packaging IP (EPP) Medium, rising Embedded Power Platform uses the silicon wafer as the package; management claims 50% smaller and 20% cooler solid-state breakers, patents on process as well as idea (Investor Day, 16 Sep 2026). Unproven at volume.
Portfolio breadth across the power tree Medium-strong Being able to sell from grid (SiC JFET, SST) to core (Vcore, smart power stages) is a genuine systems-selling advantage in AI data centre; management argues only two companies can do the full tree. Claim.
Scale/cost in discrete power Weak Chinese and Japanese competitors price aggressively; power discretes are the most commoditised part of the portfolio.
Image-sensor IP Strong but challenged Automotive dynamic range and LED-flicker handling are hard; Sony is the technology leader overall and is investing in auto.

Customer concentration

Moderate and stable: one distributor at 15% of Q3 FY2026 revenue (11% in FY2025), distributors 54% of revenue, no disclosed OEM above 10%. Auto Tier-1 concentration means underlying end-customer concentration is higher than the filing suggests — a structural feature of auto semis, not a red flag.

What the numbers say

  • Q1 FY2025 was the trough, and it was self-inflicted as much as cyclical. Revenue fell 22% and GAAP gross margin collapsed to 20.3% on $235.8M of excess and obsolete inventory charges (of which $230.3M hit ISG), plus $496M of manufacturing impairment for the year (10-Q MD&A; FY2025 10-K). Ex-charge, Q1 FY2025 gross margin would have been ~40%.
  • The recovery is margin-led, not just volume-led. Five consecutive quarters of gross-margin expansion from 36.0% to 41.4%, on utilisation recovering from 77% to 83% and a richer mix (AI data centre, new products). Management's rule of thumb is 25–30bp of gross margin per point of utilisation — which implies another ~400–500bp from running the fabs fuller (Q3 call).
  • Operating leverage is visible. Q3 revenue +11% YoY produced +43% non-GAAP EPS growth, because opex is being held roughly flat (R&D $145.0M vs $141.9M; SG&A $66.8M vs $62.8M) while gross profit rose $126M.
  • Cash conversion is exceptional. TTM FCF $1.62B on $6.37B of revenue (25.4%), 28% in Q3. Capex at 2% of revenue means almost all operating cash flow drops through — the payoff from the fab-lite strategy and the 2023-25 capex peak ($1.54B in FY2023).
  • SBC is modest and falling: 2.15% of revenue in Q3 versus 2.5% a year ago and 1.5% in FY2023. Total SBC $149.6M TTM against $1.62B of FCF — not a dilution problem.

6. Balance sheet & dilution risk

Pre-Synaptics, the balance sheet is close to net-debt-neutral (2 Oct 2026): cash and short-term investments $4,367.7M; gross debt $4,504.7M (0.50% converts due 2029 $1,500M; 0% converts due 2031 $1,500M; 0% converts due 2027 $804.7M; 3.875% notes due 2028 $700M); carrying value $4,463.0M → net debt ~$137M, or ~0.07x TTM EBITDA. Liquidity is $5.9B including a $1.75B revolver (upsized from $1.5B on 12 Nov 2026 — 8-K). Debt/EBITDA is 2.3x gross, interest coverage 22.6x (StockAnalysis).

Post-Synaptics it will not be. The $5.7B cash consideration plus ~$0.5B of assumed Synaptics net debt, funded with a $2.45B senior secured term loan and ~$3.25B of cash, takes my estimated pro forma net debt to ~$6.3B, or ~2.8x pro forma EBITDA (estimate; Synaptics is not consolidated until close). Management has said deleveraging will be the top priority after closing (Q3 call). That is investment-grade-manageable but removes the balance-sheet optionality that supported the stock through the downturn.

Dilution mechanics: - 2027 0% converts ($804.7M, conversion price $52.97) are deeply in the money at $71 and are classified as current debt; holders can convert this quarter. The concurrent capped call covers dilution up to $74.34, so at today's price there is no economic dilution — onsemi settles principal in cash and the hedge absorbs the shares. - 2029 0.50% converts ($1.5B, $103.87) and 2031 0% converts ($1.5B, $161.30) are both far out of the money; the $351.6M hedge and $281.0M of warrants sold cap 2031 dilution at $211.54. - Share count is shrinking: 389.26M shares outstanding at 2 Oct 2026 versus 396.74M at 31 Dec 2025 and 408M diluted a year ago — −4.07% YoY. onsemi bought back $1.21B TTM and $1.41B in FY2025, but only $20M in Q3 FY2026, constrained by trading restrictions around the Synaptics process. Buybacks are effectively paused until the deal closes; expect them to resume in H2 2027 only after leverage falls. There is no ATM and no preferred stock.

14. What would change my mind

To turn bullish (any two of these): 1. Q1 FY2027 auto revenue returns to double-digit YoY growth while gross margin holds ≥43% — proof that the Q3/Q4 auto weakness was supply allocation, not demand. 2. AI data-centre quarterly revenue exceeds $350M by Q2 FY2027 (versus $180M in Q3 FY2026), with a disclosed design win at a second hyperscaler beyond Google. 3. Synaptics closes on schedule with pro forma leverage below 2.5x and no guidance cut, and the buyback restarts within two quarters. 4. Gross margin reaches 44–45% by mid-2027, keeping the 53% target credible and forcing consensus EPS upgrades rather than multiple compression.

To turn bearish (any two): 1. Gross margin stalls below 41% for two consecutive quarters, particularly if PSG (31.4% GM) deteriorates on SiC pricing. 2. Auto revenue declines again in 2027 despite normalising supply — evidence of share loss to Chinese or in-house suppliers. 3. AI data-centre growth decelerates below 50% YoY or a major PSU customer re-sources, which would remove the only growth engine supporting the multiple. 4. Synaptics is re-cut a second time, blocked by a regulator, or financed with more debt than the $2.45B term loan, taking leverage above 3x.
