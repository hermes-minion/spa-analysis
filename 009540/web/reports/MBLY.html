0. Classification (mandatory)

Profile C — transition to profitability. FY2026 revenue $1.894B, GAAP gross margin 47.7%, GAAP operating margin −16.7% but adjusted operating margin +18.1%; Q4 FCF +$72M and net cash $1.41B. GAAP EPS is distorted by the $3.788B goodwill impairment in Q1 FY2026. Business model: fabless ADAS/AV SoCs plus software (SuperVision, Chauffeur, REM mapping), sold to OEMs through Tier-1s. Metrics that matter: EV/sales, adjusted operating margin, design wins, FCF and net cash.

1. Executive summary

  • The core franchise is intact and cheap, but it is no longer a growth stock. Q3 FY2026 revenue $492M (−2% YoY) on 9.5M EyeQ units (+3%), with 9M revenue +8% and 9M EyeQ shipments +11% against global light-vehicle production down ~1% (Q3 FY2026 release, 29 Oct 2026). FY2026 guidance is $1.995–2.02B (+5–7%) and adjusted operating income $380–420M — but ~$190M of that is an Israeli R&D tax credit, so ex-credit FY2026 adjusted operating income (~$210M) is down from $280M in FY2025. At $5.56 the stock trades at 1.63x EV/sales, 10.3x EV/FCF and 0.58x book, the cheapest multiple in its listed history.
  • The de-rating is a growth-and-trust problem, not a solvency problem. The shares are −55% in 52 weeks and −44% YTD; the company took a $3.788B non-cash goodwill impairment in Q1 FY2026 after a 35% share-price decline, and still carries $4.911B of goodwill against a $4.73B market cap (10-Q Note 12). Net cash is $1.41B and FY2026E FCF is ~$290M, so there is no financing risk.
  • ASP is the swing factor, and it is currently negative. Blended ASP fell ~$2 in FY2026 (to ~$51 from $53) on China-OEM mix and a second-chip content shift; management guides 2027 ASP "more flat" (Q3 call, 29 Oct 2026). The re-rating case rests on Surround ADAS (2.5–3x base ASP of ~$46, i.e. ~$125) and SuperVision (~$1,300) launching 2027–28, with management targeting ASPs of $80–90 as 30–40% of volume converts (Canaccord fireside, 12 Aug 2026 — claim, not contracted revenue).
  • Customer concentration is the single largest fundamental risk. Customer A was 30% of FY2025 revenue and 28% of 9M FY2026; Customer B 14–17%; five customers ≈ 82% of revenue (FY2025 10-K, Note; Q3 FY2026 10-Q Note 9). Customer A is widely inferred to be Volkswagen Group (not named in filings — unverified), and VW is simultaneously (i) the lead launch partner for SuperVision (Porsche, Q1 2027) and Chauffeur (Audi, H1 2028), and (ii) reportedly selecting Wayve for its next-generation autonomy stack (heise, 4 Oct 2026 — report, unconfirmed by VW, Wayve or Mobileye).
  • Governance is in flux. Founder-CEO Amnon Shashua announced on 23 Jul 2026 that he will step down once a successor is appointed; the board has formed a search committee and he has been offered the chair (Times of Israel, 23 Jul 2026). Intel still owns 76.2% of the economics and 96.8% of the votes (10-Q, 26 Sep 2026), a structural overhang with no announced disposal plan.
  • Physical-AI purity is effectively 100%, but "advanced autonomy" revenue is only ~5%. ~90% of revenue is EyeQ SoC sales into ADAS; SuperVision is ~$85M (~4%) in FY2026E; robotaxi (Drive) and humanoid (Mentee) revenue is ≈0. This is a real perception-and-act franchise — but today it is an ADAS chip-and-software business with an autonomy option, not an autonomy business.
  • Position in the 15-name watchlist: the cheapest quality asset in the basket on EV/sales and EV/FCF, with the weakest near-term growth and the most unresolved governance.

Verdict: NEUTRAL — conviction Medium. 12-month fair value ~$6.25 (base case). The balance sheet and 2028 product cycle put a floor under the equity; the CEO vacuum, VW concentration and 2027 ASP flatness cap the upside. I would need either a named credible CEO plus a SuperVision/Chauffeur launch on schedule, or a VW program confirmation, to turn constructive.

2. Business overview

What they sell

Mobileye sells perception and driving-decision systems for vehicles, delivered as a tightly coupled stack of purpose-built silicon (EyeQ SoC), software (perception, REM mapping, RSS driving policy) and, increasingly, full ECUs/systems.

To whom, and how they get paid

Sales go through Tier-1 suppliers to OEMs, plus direct OEM programs. Revenue is essentially product revenue (SoC + ECU + licensed software bundled into the unit price); there is no meaningful SaaS line yet. EyeQ SoC sales were ~90% of revenue in Q3 FY2026 and 9M FY2026 (10-Q). The economics are NRE-light and volume-heavy: design win → 3–5 year lead time → 5–8 year production life.

3. Physical AI exposure

Where it sits in the stack

Mobileye is vertically integrated across four of the five layers, which is unusual in this watchlist:

SENSING COMPUTE/SILICON DATA SOFTWARE/PERCEPTION SYSTEMS/DEPLOYMENT Camera (3rd-party) EyeQ2→EyeQ6 Lite/High REM: ~8M vehicles Perception, RSS, SuperVision ECU, Imaging radar (own) ASIC designed in-house crowd-sourced maps driving policy, DXP Chauffeur, Drive, Lidar (de-emphasised) fabbed at STMicro (own data asset) validation toolchain robotaxi service (2027), Mentee VLA stack Moovit orchestration

  • Sensing: buys cameras from third parties; designs its imaging radar in-house (management's bet: cameras + imaging radar, no lidar long term — Q3 call). This is a deliberate cost choice: it removes lidar BOM but concedes the sensing layer to suppliers.
  • Compute: EyeQ is a purpose-built ASIC, not a general-purpose GPU. Single-sourced from STMicroelectronics for SoCs and Quanta for ECUs, with TSMC flagged as a possible additional source (10-Q, supply-chain risk). That is a cost/margin advantage and a geopolitical/supply vulnerability.
  • Data: REM is the most under-appreciated asset — crowdsourced road intelligence from ~8M vehicles, which cannot be replicated without fleet scale. Management claims Tesla is now building the same thing, implicitly validating it (claim).
  • Software: perception + RSS (formal safety model) + DXP (OEM customisation). This is where the moat and the ASP expansion live.
  • Systems: SuperVision/Chauffeur ECUs, and — new for 2026 — Drive plus vehicle platform plus operations: a vertically integrated robotaxi service from 2027.

% of revenue tied to Physical AI

By the definition used in this watchlist (AI that perceives and acts in the physical world), Mobileye is ~100% Physical AI today: every dollar is perception software running on silicon that actuates a vehicle. That is not the interesting number. The interesting number is the share of revenue from high-autonomy content (L2+/L3/L4):

4. Competitive position & moat

Market position

  • ~40%+ of global front-camera ADAS by unit share is the working industry estimate; the company says its EyeQ is "present in the majority of vehicles produced in North America and Europe in 2026" within the L2+ segment, and it has content on "the majority of vehicles produced in North America and Europe" as a front-camera perception supplier (Q3 FY2026 release and call — company claim, directionally consistent with 39M units vs ~90M global light vehicles).
  • Installed base: ~1,400 models, >230M vehicles with EyeQ (FY2025 10-K); "more than 250 million" by Q3 FY2026.
  • Pipeline: $24.5B eight-year expected automotive revenue at YE2025, +42% since YE2022 (Q4 FY2025 release, 22 Jan 2026). This is an OEM-volume-based estimate, not a backlog, and the company itself disclaims it.

Company-specific questions

(a) EyeQ volumes, ASP trend, customer inventory. - Volumes: FY2025 35.7M systems (29.0M in 2024, 37.4M in 2023 — FY2025 10-K). FY2026E ~39M (+~10%); Q3 FY2026 9.5M (+3% YoY, top of 9.3–9.5M guide); 9M +11% vs production −1%. - ASP: blended ~$51 in FY2026E vs $53.1 in FY2025. Drivers: (i) China OEM mix — Chinese customers buy less software per unit, ~−$1; (ii) a second chip sold in 2026 that was not sold in 2025 (~−$1); (iii) memory-cost pass-through (+$7M revenue at zero gross margin in Q3). Management: "2027 more flat… not the same headwinds" (Q3 call). Base ADAS ASP ~$46 (Aug 2026). - Inventory: ~5 weeks at Tier-1s vs a normal 4–5 (Canaccord, 12 Aug 2026). Q4 FY2026 guidance implies 8.8–8.9M units vs 9.5M in Q3 and revenue −8.5% QoQ ($450M) — management calls this intentional safety stock unwinding. My read: this is normalisation, not a demand break; the risk is that Tier-1s de-stock more than guided in Q4 and again in Q1 FY2027, which would put the "flat 2027" ASP/volume assumption at risk.

(b) Advanced products. - Surround ADAS: 3 wins (VW, Mahindra, large US OEM), all SOP 2028, 19M units of expected volume from the first two (Jan 2026), ~30–40% of each OEM's volume. This is the single most important product in the equity story: it is the bridge from $46 to $125 content. - Cloud-Enhanced ADAS: Stellantis (US 2027, expanded to Europe), plus a Top-5 Asian and a Top-10 Japanese OEM. Gross profit per unit "roughly equivalent to Surround ADAS and more than double base ADAS" (Q2 FY2026 release) — a cheaper, faster-to-market route to content growth. - SuperVision: ZEEKR live today; Porsche Q1 2027 first launch, volume ramp H2 2027, target 300–350k/yr by year three; Mahindra is a second SuperVision customer. - Chauffeur (L3): Audi, H1 2028. Management calls it a potential "industry first" high-volume L3. This is the highest-risk, highest-value program. - Drive / robotaxi: >100 ID. Buzz AVs testing in LA, Austin, Orlando, Munich, Berlin, Hamburg; MOIA public ridepooling with safety drivers in Hamburg; Orlando closed-user-group testing; Holon permit in Norway; Deutsche Bahn selected HOLON Urban; Beep Series B (Mobileye-led). Mobileye claims >60% of German L4 test permits are on Drive-equipped vehicles (company estimate, Aug 2026). Own service: 100–200 vehicles in one US city in 2027, 17k vehicles in five years (management, Q2 call).

(c) Intel ownership. See §6 and §10.

(d) Competition. Above. The short version: Mobileye wins on cost and validation at L0–L2, is at parity in L2+ systems, and is behind on L2++/L3 compute. That is precisely why the multiple has collapsed from 16x sales (2023) to 1.6x.

What the numbers say

  1. Revenue is cyclical and lumpy, not compounding. FY2024 fell 20% on customer inventory correction; FY2025 recovered 15%; FY2026 grows 5–7%. Three years of revenue: $2.08B → $1.65B → $1.89B → ~$2.01B. That is a 5% CAGR from FY2023, i.e. no growth in three years.
  2. Adjusted gross margin is eroding slowly: 70.0% (FY2023) → 68.4% (FY2024) → 67.7% (FY2025) → ~65% in 9M FY2026. Mix (China, SuperVision hardware) and memory costs are the causes. This is the quiet bear case: the "software margin" story is leaking.
  3. The FY2026 "record" adjusted operating income is largely a tax-credit artifact. FY2025 adjusted operating income was $280M (14.8% margin); FY2026 guidance of $400M includes ~$190M from the Israeli R&D Law incentive, so ex-credit FY2026 adjusted operating income is roughly $210M — down ~25% YoY. Mentee opex consolidation and SuperVision's lower gross margin explain the gap. This is the single most under-discussed fact in the bull case.
  4. SBC is 15–20% of revenue and rising (20.1% in Q3 FY2026). Deducting $385M of FY2026E SBC from the $210M ex-credit adjusted operating income leaves the business roughly break-even on a fully loaded basis. Any "12x forward P/E" argument is a non-GAAP, credit-assisted construct.
  5. The R&D credit is doing a lot of work. The Israeli R&D Law incentive contributed $51M in Q3 and $144M in 9M FY2026 to non-GAAP operating income (~$190M FY2026E). It has no expiry, but it is a policy variable, and the cash arrives ~2028.
  6. Cash conversion is genuinely good: 9M FY2026 operating cash flow $303M on a $3.9B GAAP loss, because the loss is non-cash (impairment, amortisation, SBC).

  • Runway: not applicable in a solvency sense — the company is FCF-positive and has $1.4B net cash, ~5 years of current burn even in a stress case. The relevant "runway" question is how much cash the robotaxi vertical integration consumes in 2027–28. Management will not quantify it ("some of it can be funded through our own cash, and some through external funding", Q2 call). My estimate: $150–250M/yr of capex/opex at 100–200 vehicles, i.e. ~10–15% of annual FCF. Not existential; dilutive to returns.
  • Convertibles / warrants / ATM: none. No convertible notes, no warrants, no ATM program disclosed in the 10-Q. This is a clean capital structure.
  • Buyback: $250M authorised April 2026; $33M repurchased at an average $9.36 through Q3 (Q3 call). That is a poor use of cash in hindsight (−41% versus today's price), and it is explicitly framed as offsets to dilution, not as a value signal.

SBC of ~$385M/yr against a $4.7B market cap is ~8% of market cap per year in economic dilution if not offset — the buyback covers only a fraction.
  • Goodwill risk: $4.911B of goodwill exceeds the entire market cap. If the share price stays at $5–6, the Q4 FY2026 annual impairment test is likely to force another write-down. This is non-cash and does not affect covenants (there are none), but it will keep GAAP earnings ugly and headlines negative.

14. What would change my mind

Bullish signals (would move me to Buy): 1. A named, credible CEO with operating scale experience (semis or auto Tier-1), appointed before mid-2027, with Shashua moving to chair and retaining the technology roadmap. This removes the largest governance discount. 2. Porsche SuperVision ships on schedule in Q1 2027 and management guides SuperVision revenue >$150M for FY2027. That would validate the $1,300 ASP and the EyeQ6 High platform. 3. A fourth Surround ADAS nomination (ideally a Japanese or Korean OEM) and confirmation that the three existing wins remain on 2028 SOP. Surround is the bridge from $46 to $125; a slip below 30% of target volume breaks the 2029 model. 4. VW publicly reaffirms the Chauffeur/Audi program through 2028 and MOIA reaches driver-out with a funded fleet plan. This directly de-risks risk #1. 5. FY2027 guidance with ASP flat-to-up and adjusted gross margin ≥66%, proving the mix/China headwind is cyclical, not structural.

Bearish signals (would move me to Sell): 1. SuperVision slips out of 2027, or Porsche launch volumes are materially below the 300–350k/yr trajectory. That would push the entire premium stack right by 12–18 months and make the 2029 model unreachable. 2. A second large customer loss or VW formally selects a third-party stack for its 2028+ architectures, beyond the reported Wayve selection. 3. Another year of ASP decline (blended below $48) with Surround still not in production — i.e. the content-growth story fails before it starts. 4. Robotaxi capex guidance above $300M/yr without a partner funding commitment, or a delay to the 2027 own-service launch. 5. Intel announces a large secondary (e.g. >100M shares) without a strategic buyer, or the buyback is suspended to preserve cash.
