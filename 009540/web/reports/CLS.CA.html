1. The setup

  • The AI-hardware ODM that doubled revenue in a year — and just fell 48% from its high. Celestica's TTM revenue is C$18.23B (from C$12.39B in FY2025, +47%), driven by hyperscale networking (800G switches) and AI compute hardware. Q3 2026 revenue was C$5.83B (+82.5% YoY), EPS C$3.26 (+41%).
  • The business is low-margin but high-return: gross margin 11.5%, operating margin 8.3%, net margin 6.8%, but ROIC 41.9% and ROE 30.2% because capital turns are high. Net cash is C$4.16B (10% of the market cap).
  • Valuation is the debate: 13.2x forward earnings and 15.7x EV/EBITDA for a hardware manufacturer at peak AI-capex growth. The stock has de-rated 48% as the market prices a peak in AI hardware spending.
  • The customer concentration is extreme: a handful of hyperscalers (Google, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft) drive most of the growth. A single program pause would hit revenue hard.
  • The balance sheet is strong: C$5.63B cash, C$1.46B debt, net cash C$4.16B. Altman 4.5, Piotroski 5.
  • Sentiment: far below the 50-DMA (C$437) and 200-DMA (C$456); short interest 1.6%; average target C$609 (in USD terms from US-listed peers). The stock is a high-beta AI-capex proxy.

2. Business and moat

Celestica provides design, manufacturing and supply-chain services to OEMs and hyperscalers: networking switches, servers, storage, aerospace/defense electronics and industrial hardware. The AI networking business (800G/1.6T switches) is the growth engine.

The moat is manufacturing scale, engineering collaboration and qualification with hyperscalers. It is not a software moat: customers can dual-source, and margins are thin. The key is execution and capacity allocation in a supply-constrained market.
