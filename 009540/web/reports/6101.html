1. The setup

  • A record-earnings machine-tool maker trading at 9.5x, with 20% of its market cap in net cash. Tsugami's FY2026 (ended Mar-2026) was a record: revenue ¥129.1B (+20%), net income ¥16.7B (+54%), EPS ¥356. TTM EPS is now ¥407.50.
  • The stock has just been cut 37% in three months (and 31% in one month), despite Q1 FY2027 revenue of ¥43.0B (+43% YoY) and EPS of ¥140 (+85%). The sell-off is a cyclical de-rating: machine-tool orders are a leading indicator, and China/auto capex fears are rising.
  • The valuation is deep value: 9.5x trailing earnings, 4.2x EV/EBITDA, 6.8x P/FCF, 2.5% dividend yield, ¥35.8B net cash (20% of the market cap). Ex-cash, the operating business trades at ~7x earnings.
  • The business: precision automatic lathes, machining centers and grinding machines, sold mainly to automotive, semiconductor-equipment and medical-device customers. Precision Tsugami (China) is a major subsidiary and the growth driver of the last cycle.
  • The risk is the cycle, not the balance sheet. Machine-tool demand is capex-driven and China-exposed; the 2023 downturn cut earnings sharply, and a repeat would hit the multiple and the dividend.
  • Sentiment: below the 50-DMA (¥4,880) and 200-DMA (¥5,135); no analyst target on the vendor page; short interest negligible. The stock is cheap because the cycle is turning, not because the company is broken.

2. Business and moat

Tsugami designs and manufactures precision machine tools — automatic lathes (the core), machining centers and grinding machines — plus related services. Customers are in automotive (fuel injection, drivetrain), semiconductor equipment, medical devices and general precision parts.

The moat is engineering precision and the installed base/service network; Tsugami's lathes are known for high rigidity and accuracy in small-part machining. The China subsidiary gives it cost and market access, but also exposes it to Chinese industrial policy and competition from local machine-tool makers.
