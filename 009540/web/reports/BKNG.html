Research, not investment advice.

1. The setup

  • The cheapest large-cap OTA in a decade — for a reason. BKNG trades at 13.6x forward earnings and 12.6x EV/EBITDA, roughly half its historical multiple. The de-rating is not a cyclical accident: Q3 2026 revenue fell 1.3% YoY ($8.89B vs $9.01B) and net income slipped to $2.72B, the first outright revenue decline in years.
  • The AI-gatekeeper threat is the whole debate. If travellers book through ChatGPT/Gemini agents instead of Google/Booking, the OTA's paid-search moat weakens. Booking is responding with its own AI trip planner and direct-connect deals, but the market is pricing structural disintermediation, not a soft quarter.
  • The franchise is still enormous and cash-generative. TTM revenue $28.1B, net income $7.18B, FCF $9.24B (32.8% margin), 87% gross margin. Share count is down 5.2% YoY; the company buys back ~$6-7B a year.
  • Balance sheet is levered by choice: cash $15.8B, debt $21.3B, net debt $5.2B — the result of decades of buybacks. Interest coverage is comfortable; this is not a solvency story.
  • Sentiment is washed out: the stock is 31% below its 5-year high, below both the 50-DMA ($163) and 200-DMA ($175), with a 2.3% short interest and 98% institutional ownership. A stabilisation in room nights would be enough to re-rate it.
  • The portfolio: Booking.com (Europe/global), Priceline (US), Agoda (Asia), KAYAK (metasearch), OpenTable (restaurants) and Rentalcars. Merchant model (Booking collects and remits) is ~75% of revenue; agency model is the rest.

2. Business and moat

Booking earns a commission (roughly 15-20% of gross booking value) from hotels and other accommodation providers. Its moat is demand aggregation: 30M+ listings, brand, loyalty (Genius), and the largest paid-search budget in travel. Hotels value the incremental demand; travellers value selection and price transparency.

The threat is that AI assistants become the new demand aggregator. If a user says "book me a hotel in Rome under €200", the model can query inventory directly, compare and book — bypassing the OTA's interface. Booking's defence is (1) supply relationships and merchant-of-record capabilities, (2) its own AI planner, and (3) the fact that hotels still need third-party demand. None of these is a guaranteed moat against a well-funded agent platform.

Base: growth resumes at mid-single digits, EPS $11.7, multiple 16x → $187. Bull: direct-connect and AI planner work, EPS $12.5, multiple 19x → $238. Probabilities 30/45/25 → ~$179 (+12%).

At 13.6x, the market is already assuming real erosion. The bull case does not require heroic growth — only that Booking remains the transaction layer for a large share of global travel.

5. Catalysts

  • Q4 2026 results (Feb 2027): room-night growth and gross bookings are the numbers to watch; a return to positive revenue growth would be the first re-rating signal.
  • AI partnerships: distribution deals with assistant platforms (Booking as the booking engine behind an agent) would flip the narrative.
  • Summer 2027 booking curve: European travel demand and pricing.
  • Buyback pace: at 12.9x FCF, repurchases are highly accretive.
