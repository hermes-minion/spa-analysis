What the market is pricing. At $243 the stock is 9.9x FY2026 non-GAAP EPS, 8.9x FY2027, 9.0x TTM free cash flow and 11.2x free cash flow after charging stock compensation as a cash cost. The reverse DCF (§3.6) says those prices embed a 0.85% revenue CAGR for ten years — not slower growth, but the effective end of growth. That is not a valuation of a company growing ARR 11.2% with a 40.8% FCF margin; it is a valuation of a franchise the market expects generative AI to erode. The entire debate is whether seat-based creative software survives agents. Current reported numbers do not yet show the erosion: revenue +12.9% in Q3, ARR +11.2%, non-GAAP EPS +15%, 6.2% of shares retired in twelve months. But the forward-looking disclosures do show deceleration: RPO +8% (first single-digit growth since early FY2023), a Q4 guide implying customer-group subscription growth of +10.7% to +11.5% versus +14% in Q3, and an FY27 consensus (9.1% revenue, 12.0% EPS) that the sell-side bear case calls too high.

2. Business & fundamentals

2.1 Segments — the three-segment table no longer exists

In Q1 FY2026 Adobe collapsed Digital Media, Digital Experience and Publishing & Advertising into a single reportable segment (10-Q Q3 FY26, Note 15: "we combined our former segments … into a single operating and reportable segment"). Segment disclosure is now consolidated revenue, cost of revenue and net income only; the CODM reviews revenue and gross margin, and Adobe does not report segment operating income. Anyone quoting Digital Media/Digital Experience margins for FY2026 is using a third-party model, not company data (StockAnalysis' FY26 segment rows are Fiscal.ai estimates — I have not used them).

Narrative, not data: "agentic software leader", "1 billion MAU is a defining moment", "AI-first ARR growing 150%". None of it is false; none of it yet shows that AI is additive to revenue rather than a defence of the existing base.

The disruption case, stated at its strongest. Adobe sells seats; agents are priced per task. If a marketing team can brief an agent that produces and edits the work, five Creative Cloud seats become one plus token spend — and Adobe's 89% gross margin is precisely the margin that attracts substitution. The evidence that this is already happening: (a) growth is increasingly price-led — list prices rose 12–25% into 2026 renewals while ARR growth slowed to 10.2% guided; (b) RPO growth fell to 8%, the weakest since early FY2023; (c) AI-first ARR is only 2.4% of the base after three years, i.e. the new products are not yet replacing what the old ones might lose; (d) management stopped disclosing the metrics (Digital Media ARR, net new ARR, retention) that would settle the question.

The counter-case, stated at its strongest. The workflow, file formats and brand-governance layer are stickier than the rendering engine. Adobe's own answer — MCP plus third-party models inside its apps — monetises other companies' models through its distribution, and 1B MAU is the largest distribution in creative software. Acrobat/Express (+16%) shows AI expanding the user base where the job-to-be-done is documents, not craft. And the financials are not those of a melting ice cube: 40.8% FCF margin, 89% gross margin, 57% ROIC, and net debt of just $724M (0.7% of EV).

Peer reality check: Figma trades at 6.3x EV/Sales with a −40.5% operating margin and a $10.4B market cap; Adobe trades at 3.67x EV/Sales with a +36.3% operating margin and a $94.6B market cap. The market is paying more per dollar of revenue for the challenger's losses than for the incumbent's profits. That is a narrative price, not an arithmetic one.

2.6 Four claims the disclosure no longer supports

Bull cases for Adobe tend to arrive as a package: a diversification story ("three legs"), a volume story, an AI story and an alignment story. Each was checkable three years ago. Each has lost the disclosure that made it checkable.

Claim What Adobe reports now Why it no longer holds
"Three legs diversify the business" One reportable segment since Q1 FY2026. The three-way split (Digital Media / Digital Experience / Publishing & Advertising) was last published in the FY2025 10-K. The only live cut is two customer groups: Creative & Marketing Professionals $4,651M (71% of Q3 subscription revenue) and Business Professionals & Consumers $1,905M (29%) The "three legs" rests on a segment split Adobe stopped reporting. On the cut it does report, 71% of subscription revenue sits in a single group that contains both Creative Cloud and Digital Experience — concentration, not diversification.
"Growth comes from volume" ARR $27.50B, +11.2%. No seats, no retention rate, no product-level net new ARR; Digital Media ARR discontinued at $19.20B (FY25) List prices rose 12–25% into 2026 renewals [T3]. ARR +11.2% alongside price rises of that size is arithmetically consistent with flat-to-negative unit growth — and the metrics that would settle it are no longer published.
"AI-first ARR is growing 150%" >$650M, i.e. 2.4% of total ARR The increment is ~$390M — 14% of the $2.76B of net new ARR added in the last four quarters. 150% off a 2.4% base is not yet a second engine.
"Insiders are aligned" Form 4s: the CEO sold 200,000 shares for $49.6M in 2026; the only open-market purchase in twelve months is $1.95M from a director; insider ownership 0.17% The largest seller is the CEO stepping back to Executive Chair; the largest buyer is a board member buying a rounding error against that supply. Detail in §7.2.

The timing is uncomfortable even if it is innocent: retention, Digital Media ARR and net new ARR were discontinued in the same period in which price increases started carrying the growth line. From outside the company there is no way to distinguish cause from coincidence, and that is the point. §3 shows the stock is cheap on the numbers Adobe still publishes; §2.6 is the reminder that the numbers Adobe stopped publishing are the ones that would decide whether the cheapness is real.

8. Conclusion

8.1 Verdict

Undervalued. Confidence: medium.

The valuation work is unambiguous: at $243 the market capitalises Adobe at 9.9x FY26 earnings and 9.0x free cash flow, and the reverse DCF implies 0.85% revenue growth for a decade. The business is currently growing 12.9% with a 40.8% FCF margin, 89% gross margin, net debt at 0.7% of EV, and a buyback authorization equal to 26% of the market cap. Three independent methods cluster at $285–328.

The confidence is medium, not high, for three reasons that are not in the multiples: (1) Adobe stopped disclosing the seat-level metrics that would settle the AI debate; (2) the Q4 guide already implies a ~3pp deceleration; (3) the leadership team is being rebuilt at exactly the moment execution matters most — a new CEO from 1-Dec, an interim CFO since June, and David Wadhwani, the creative-business president, stepping down effective 27-Sep-2026 — while the CEO sold 200,000 shares ($49.6M) in 2026 and the only insider purchase in twelve months was $1.95M from a director. A cheap multiple on numbers that are about to be reset is not the same as a cheap multiple on numbers that will hold.

8.3 Invalidation

  • Price: a daily close below $190.10 (the 18-Jun-2026 low) on above-average volume invalidates the basing structure; below it there is no traded volume shelf in five years until the low-160s, and the measured move of the 224–294 range points to ~$155.
  • Fundamental: FY27 revenue growth guided below 4%, ARR growth below 8%, or any disclosed net revenue retention below 100%. Those would confirm the "hard reset" and make 7x, not 9x, the correct multiple.

8.4 Where I disagree with consensus

  1. Consensus FY27 revenue (+9.1%) is too high; consensus EPS (+12.0%) is about right. I model +8% revenue with a flat 44.5% margin and get $27.40 — essentially the consensus EPS — because the buyback does the work. Lower revenue, same EPS, lower multiple risk.
  2. The Street's Hold/average-target ($267) is not a thesis. At 9.7x FY27 EPS — barely above today's 8.9x — the average target assumes almost no re-rating. If the franchise is impaired, the answer is Sell at $180–210 (Citi's side). If it is not, the answer is $300+. "Hold" is the option value of not deciding.
  3. Citi's $207 is internally coherent but self-defeating. At 8x, Adobe's FCF retires roughly a third of the shares in three years; the bear case manufactures its own floor. The bear target needs revenue to decline, not just decelerate.
  4. The bull case over-claims the AI evidence. AI-first ARR of $650M is 2.4% of the base after three years of Firefly; the +150% headline is ~$390M of incremental ARR, 14% of the $2.76B added in the last four quarters; the "1 billion MAU" headline is mostly free users. The bull case should be built on Acrobat/Express (+16%, +900M MAU) and Digital Experience (>20% sub-segment ARR), not on Firefly replacing Creative Cloud.

8.5 The 30-second version

Adobe trades at 9.9x FY26 earnings and 9.0x free cash flow while growing revenue 13%, ARR 11% and retiring 6% of its shares a year. The reverse DCF says the price assumes 0.85% growth for a decade — the market is pricing the end of the seat model, not a slowdown. The bear case is real: RPO growth has fallen to 8%, the Q4 guide implies a 3pp deceleration, pricing is doing the heavy lifting, and the CEO, CFO and creative leadership are all in transition. But the cash generation is intact, the buyback authorization is 26% of the market cap, and three valuation methods land at $285–328. I would buy in tranches at $230–237 and add at $210–225, size for the 9-Dec print (options imply ±11.4%), and cut on a close below $190 or an FY27 guide under 4% growth. Medium confidence, 18% expected 12-month return, and the next 25 days decide whether that number is too low or too high.

Method appendix

  • Prices/technicals: Nasdaq api.nasdaq.com/api/quote/{ADBE,SPY,IGV}/historical (2016-11-14 → 2026-11-13, 2,514 sessions each). All indicators (SMA, RSI-14 Wilder, MACD 12/26/9, stochastic 14/3, ATR-14, OBV, A/D, volume profile, VWAP/AVWAP) computed from that series; scripts in ~/stocks/ADBE/_data/ (tech.py, levels.py, opts.py, model.py, final_numbers.py).
  • Earnings reactions: next trading session after each after-close release (release date itself is the pre-earnings session); 11 events, Mar-2024 to Sep-2026.
  • Forward multiples: FY26 non-GAAP EPS $24.48 = 9M actual $18.15 + Q4 guide midpoint $6.325. FY27 $27.41 (S&P Global consensus, 12-Nov-2026). NTM revenue $28.87B [E] = Q4 FY26 guide $6.825B + 9M FY27E $22.04B.
  • EBITDA: GAAP operating income + D&A ($484M TTM). SBC add-back variant adds TTM SBC $2,052M.
  • DCF: 10-year explicit forecast, mid-year not applied, terminal value on year-10 FCF grown at g; SBC deducted from FCF; net debt at 28-Aug-2026; 389.2M shares.
  • Reproducibility: model.py regenerates every multiple, the DCF, both sensitivity grids and the reverse DCF from the stated inputs.
  • Insider and institutional flows: Form 4 XML parsed for 1-Nov-2025 → 31-Oct-2026 (65 filings, 282 non-derivative transactions; codes M/A vesting, F/D tax withholding, S sales, P purchases); 13F-HR information tables filtered on CUSIP 00724F101 for the four largest holders, Q2 and Q3 2026. Alpha Vantage INSIDER_TRANSACTIONS and INSTITUTIONAL_HOLDINGS used as cross-check and for the 1,192-filer Q3 breadth cut. Scripts: _data/form4_parse.py, _data/fetch_13f.py, charts.py (figures adbe_insiders.png, adbe_institutions.png).
