1. The setup

  • The most expensive "safe" mega-cap. Microsoft trades at 25.2x forward EPS and 19.3x EV/EBITDA — a premium to Alphabet (21.5x), Meta (19.5x) and Amazon (27x on depressed EPS) — for a business growing revenue ~18%. The premium is deserved by quality, but it leaves little room for disappointment.
  • Momentum is strong: Q1 FY2027 (Sep-2026) revenue $92.3B (+18.8% YoY), net income $36.6B (+32%), EPS $4.92. Azure and AI services are the growth engine; the commercial cloud backlog remains enormous.
  • The capex/FCF squeeze is the same as everywhere in AI: TTM FCF $60.0B (17.3% margin) versus $67-74B in FY2025-26, as Microsoft builds data centers. Capex is the swing factor for the multiple.
  • The "SaaSpocalypse" is a real overhang: AI agents threaten per-seat Office/CRM pricing, and Microsoft's own Copilot monetisation is still early. The stock is only 6% below its high because investors treat it as the safest way to own enterprise AI.
  • Balance sheet: $64.5B cash, $133.9B debt, net debt $69.4B; ROE 34.2%, ROIC 25.4%, Altman 8.3. AAA-like credit, 0.75% dividend, 20% payout.
  • Sentiment: above the 50-DMA ($519) and 200-DMA ($441), near its high; short interest 0.8%; 56 analysts with a $636 average target. A widespread outage on 13-Nov briefly hit the stock — operations risk is back in the headlines.

2. Business and moat

Microsoft sells productivity software (Microsoft 365, Teams), cloud infrastructure (Azure), developer tools (GitHub), gaming (Xbox/Activision), LinkedIn and Windows. The moat is enterprise switching costs, the Windows/Office standard, and Azure's hybrid-cloud footprint. AI is being layered across all of it (Copilot, Azure OpenAI, Fabric).

The risk is that AI agents commoditise the Office seat: if an agent can draft documents, analyse spreadsheets and book meetings, the value of a $30/month seat falls. Microsoft's defence is that it owns the identity, data and compliance layer — and can bundle Copilot into the seat.
