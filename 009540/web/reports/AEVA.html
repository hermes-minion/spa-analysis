2. Business overview

Aeva designs FMCW 4D lidar — sensors measuring velocity per point as well as range — on a silicon-photonics "lidar-on-chip" (CoreVision) plus a custom SoC (Aeva X1) (FACT, 10-K).

3. Physical AI exposure

Where it sits in the stack

Aeva is a sensing company with a thin software attach and a new component business one layer below:

Sensing (Atlas/Ultra/Eve/Omni FMCW sensors) → compute/silicon (Aeva X1 SoC, CoreVision photonics module, designed in-house) → no data (no fleet, no crowdsourced map) → software (CoreVision perception algorithms bundled into the sensor, no standalone licence) → no systems (sells sensors and NRE to OEMs; does not integrate). Plus UHP laser chipsets for AI data-centre optics — a component business, not physical AI.

A single-layer supplier — no data asset, no licensed stack, no systems integration — structurally weaker than Mobileye's four-layer position, and why gross margin is 33.6% rather than 65%.

4. Competitive position & moat

A sub-scale challenger: FY2025 revenue $18.1M vs Hesai $538M and Ouster $225M TTM. Its bet is not Chinese ADAS volume — Hesai owns that on cost — but Western L3/L4 programmes where velocity-per-point matters and Chinese suppliers are excluded. Three marquee wins, no volume yet.

Source of advantage Strength Assessment
Velocity per point + silicon photonics Medium-strong Velocity classification without tracking, immunity to mutual interference and sunlight, and a laser technology proven well enough to sell into AI data centres. But the moat is in photonic integration — a layer with many capable players — and no independent OEM has published a comparison.
Design-win lock-in Strong where won Exclusive Tier-1 on a global L3 programme is a 5–7 year, high-switching-cost position; VDA audit passed first attempt (CLAIM), ISO 9001, TISAX. Qualification cycles are the real moat here.
Manufacturing cost at scale Weak Product GM −6.2% in 9M FY2026, +4.4% in Q3. Hesai earns 40% at 20x the volume; unit economics only work above volumes Aeva has never shipped.
Staying power / scale Weak-medium $147.5M cash + $125M facility vs $30.6M quarterly use: ~8–9 quarters — enough for the 2027 Daimler launch, not the 2028 L3 SOP. 239 employees; $135M annual net loss.

FMCW versus time-of-flight (the core technical debate). ToF lidar — Hesai, Ouster, most Chinese suppliers — measures range from photon time-of-flight and infers velocity by tracking across frames; FMCW measures both coherently, so every pixel carries radial velocity instantly and is immune to interference from other lidars and sunlight. That is a genuine performance edge at highway speed and in dense-traffic L3 hand-off scenarios, and it is why Aeva won programmes where the OEM's safety case depends on velocity. The cost is manufacturing complexity: FMCW needs narrow-linewidth lasers and coherent detection, which is precisely why Aeva's product gross margin is 4% while ToF leader Hesai earns 40% at scale. OPINION: FMCW is a real differentiator in a niche, not a cost advantage in a commodity — and the lidar market has so far rewarded cost.
