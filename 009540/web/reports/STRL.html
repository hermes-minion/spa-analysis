1. The setup

  • A 70% drawdown in a company that just grew revenue 127%. Sterling is an infrastructure contractor focused on e-infrastructure (data centers, semiconductor fabs), transportation and building solutions. Q3 2026 revenue was $1.564B (+127% YoY), EPS $6.92 (+133%). The stock peaked near $1,000 and now trades at $292 — the most extreme disconnect in the batch.
  • The numbers are exceptional: TTM revenue $4.31B (from $2.49B in FY2025), net income $554M, EPS $17.82, FCF $471M, ROE 45%, ROIC 44%, Piotroski 8. Net cash $51M.
  • Valuation is deep value: 10.8x forward earnings and 9.8x EV/EBITDA for a business growing >70%, with a record backlog and raised full-year guidance. The market is pricing a peak in data-center construction.
  • The business mix: e-infrastructure is now the largest segment (data centers, fabs, power), transportation is the stable base, and building solutions is the cyclical piece. The e-infrastructure backlog is the key leading indicator.
  • Why the crash? The stock was a 2023-25 momentum favourite; the AI-capex de-rating plus a high starting multiple (30x+) produced a violent unwind. At 10.8x, the valuation now embeds a severe downturn.
  • Sentiment: far below the 50-DMA ($435) and 200-DMA ($549); short interest 4.6%; average target $680. Zacks notes "rising estimates and cheap valuation".

2. Business and moat

Sterling provides site preparation, earthwork, foundations, paving and infrastructure construction. Its e-infrastructure segment builds the civil works for data centers and semiconductor fabs — a scarce capability with high barriers (permits, equipment, skilled labour).

The moat is execution and geography: large civil projects require local permits, heavy equipment and experienced crews. The risk is cyclicality and customer concentration (hyperscalers and fab owners).
