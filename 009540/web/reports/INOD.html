0. Classification (mandatory)

Profile A — profitable, but not "stable" in the strict sense. TTM revenue $350.2M, GAAP operating margin 15.0%, net income $48.5M and FCF $83.0M (23.7% of sales); net cash $160.4M and no funded debt. EPS and FCF have been positive for three consecutive years, but top-two customer concentration of 74% and 30–90-day termination rights make the earnings stream less durable than a mature compounder's. I use the profitable-company toolkit (P/E, EV/FCF) with a concentration discount. Business model: data-engineering and AI services (pre-training data, post-training/RLHF, evaluation, federal/enterprise). Project SOWs, not software licences; no backlog. Metrics that matter: EV/sales, EV/FCF, GAAP P/E, gross-margin quality and customer concentration.

1. Executive summary

  • Momentum is real; the stock halved anyway. Q3 FY2026 revenue $95.6M (+53% YoY), the 13th straight growth quarter; 9M $277.9M (+55%); FY2026 guidance raised to "42% or more" (~$357M) [1][2]. Shares are −42.3% over 52 weeks and −71.7% from the $125.14 high, because growth quality deteriorated in the same quarter.
  • Concentration worsened, despite the "diversification" line. Two customers were 43% and 31% of Q3 revenue — 74% combined [2]; a year earlier one customer was 56% with no second above 10%. In mid-October the historically largest customer terminated a programme worth ~$12M of Q4 revenue, ~15% of the guided quarter [4].
  • A third of Q3's revenue increase came from buying content, not selling labour. Of a $23.1M rise in direct costs, $21.0M was "content acquisition" [2] — hence GAAP gross margin 37.2% versus 40.8% and adjusted margin at the 40% floor after 49% in Q2. On a zero-margin pass-through assumption the other $74.6M earned 47.6% (my calculation).
  • Genuinely profitable and debt-free. TTM operating margin 15.0%, net margin 13.8%, FCF $83.0M (23.7% of revenue), SBC only 6.5%, $164.0M cash vs $3.5M debt [9]. Not a cash-burning AI story.
  • Physical AI is ~2% of revenue. Mocap lab (Sept 2026), egocentric collection, 620,000 robot-action labelling jobs YTD, pilots that "could" produce $7M of 2027 revenue [4]. Against $350M TTM: a call option, not a business.
  • Insiders sold ~1.6M shares (~$155M) at $86–$113 and bought nothing — Abuhoff 1.23M, COO 282k, three directors [8].
  • Valuation is fair, not cheap. 3.03x EV/TTM sales, 2.30x EV/FY2027E, 12.8x EV/FCF; services peers trade at 0.7–1.4x sales.

Verdict: NEUTRAL — conviction Medium. Base-case 12-month value ~$32–36, i.e. today's price. A high-quality income statement attached to a low-quality revenue base. Constructive below ~$26 (1.6x forward sales), or on evidence that top-two concentration is falling while gross margin holds ≥40%.

2. Business overview

Innodata is a data engineering services company re-positioned around generative AI. Four capability blocks [3]:

Capability What it is
Pre-training data Rights-cleared corpora; "off-the-shelf datasets" where Innodata retains IP and resells to multiple customers; video, charts, legal, real-world data
Post-training & alignment Preference data, expert SFT, RLHF, "RL gyms", "temptation gyms" for agent-integrity testing
Evaluation / trust & safety Model evals, red-teaming, CBRNE risk evaluation, agentic safety
Enterprise & federal Agentic implementation, RAG, data foundations via Anthropic / Databricks / Snowflake / Google Cloud

Legacy assets are small: Synodex (medical-record abstraction) and Agility PR Solutions (media-intelligence SaaS).

Model — services, not software. Master service agreements plus project SOWs that "generally do not obligate customers to purchase services in future periods" and are "generally terminable upon 30 to 90 days' notice" [3]. No backlog disclosure because there is no backlog. Delivery uses 10,107 employees at 31 Dec 2025 — "12,200 professionals across more than 70 countries," concentrated in India, the Philippines and Sri Lanka [3]. Revenue per employee $34,652, between EPAM ($89.9k) and TaskUs ($19.0k) [9][13][15].

Pricing is per-task, per-asset, per-hour and increasingly per-dataset licence. Q2's 49% adjusted gross margin came from "off-the-shelf datasets, where we retain intellectual property and monetize the same asset across multiple customers" [5] — the only evidence of software-like economics, and it did not survive one quarter. No platform or licence revenue is disclosed, and the company moved to a single segment in Q1 FY2026 [2].

Geography: 88–89% of revenue is booked in the US (Q3 FY2026: US $85.1M of $95.6M); non-US customers 11–12% [2][9]. Offshore delivery, onshore customer: the revenue base is effectively "US AI capex."

3. Physical AI exposure

Where it sits in the stack

SENSING (none) → COMPUTE (none) → DATA ← INNODATA → SOFTWARE/PERCEPTION (tooling only) → SYSTEMS (none)

Innodata is a pure data-layer supplier: no sensors, compute, robot software or deployed systems. Its physical-AI product is manufactured human and robot experience — stereo-camera headset capture in homes across the US and Latin America, six-camera capture of industrial tasks "across hundreds of businesses," UMI gripper data, Vicon motion capture (lab opened Sept 2026), and robot-action annotation [4].

The best technical claim is the real-to-sim-to-real pipeline: capture human motion → retarget onto humanoid kinematics in NVIDIA Isaac Sim → execute on an in-house Unitree G1 [4]. Real capability — but Isaac Sim, the G1 and the Vicon rigs are all bought, not built.

% of revenue tied to physical AI

Today: ~2% (my estimate; not disclosed). Management gave activity metrics, not revenue: 620,000 robot-executable labelling jobs YTD, one humanoid customer that is a "potentially multi-million dollar opportunity next year," and robotics-lab pilots [4]. Egocentric/teleop collection plus the Navy autonomy award plus early humanoid work plausibly totals $5–8M annualised against $350M TTM — 1.5–2.3%. I use 2%.

In three years (FY2029): ~10% (my model; range 7–12%).

Scepticism check

MANAGEMENT CLAIM: "capital is flowing into robotics and embodied intelligence faster than into any category since the LLM boom began," citing $47.4B of H1 2026 venture funding across 521 deals [4].

MY OPINION: the macro claim is likely right — there is no web-scale corpus of physical interaction, so data must be manufactured. But physical AI is 2% of revenue; the competitive set includes Scale AI (~$29B implied) [21], Surge, Mercor and every lab's in-house team; and demand today is a one-off pre-training corpus, not recurring infrastructure. Value physical AI as a free option, not a reason to pay a higher multiple.

4. Competitive position & moat

A second-tier player in a fragmented market. The 10-K names Appen, CloudFactory, Surge AI, Invisible Technologies, Turing, Mercor, Define.a, TELUS Digital, Scale AI, plus Accenture, Cognizant, EXL, Genpact, Infosys, PwC/QuantumBlack and TCS [3], and claims five of the "Magnificent Seven" as customers [3].

The scale gap is stark: Scale AI took Meta money at a reported $14.3B for 49% (~$29B implied) and won a $500M Pentagon contract in May 2026 [21][22]. Innodata's entire market cap is $1.22B. It competes on price, delivery scale and responsiveness — not technology.

Source of advantage Strength Why

Research credibility (Innodata Labs, benchmarks, RL/temptation gyms) Medium Real differentiation versus labour brokers [4]; a sales asset, not defensible IP
Retained-IP datasets Medium-weak Q2's 49% margin came from reselling one dataset to multiple customers [5]; Q3 showed how fast mix reverses

MY OPINION: the honest description is "a well-run, research-led staffing firm with a dataset-licensing kicker" — a compliment relative to Appen (which lost AUD 10.1M on AUD 362M of revenue [12]) and a criticism relative to Scale AI. The absence of multi-year contracts is what caps the multiple: you cannot capitalise recurring-infrastructure economics on 60-day notice.

The company-specific questions

(a) Concentration and renewal risk. FY2025: one customer 58% (FY2024: 48%), no other >10% [3]. Q3 FY2026: 43% + 31% = 74%; 9M 41% + 32% = 73%; two customers are 67% of receivables [2]. Terminable on 30–90 days with no purchase obligation [3]. Renewal risk is the business model: in mid-October the largest customer ended a programme worth ~$12M per quarter; the replacement is expected "by Q1" but is not signed [4]. Within the AI business, classic text work remains 75–85% of revenue, multimodal 10–15%, robotics ~2% — my estimate.

(b) Services or platform? Services with a licensing overlay. Gross margin has not trended up structurally: 38.2% (FY2023) → 40.2% (FY2024) → 40.2% (FY2025) → 42.1% TTM, but the quarterly path is 44.7% → 46.6% → 37.2%, entirely mix-driven. Management's own target is 40% adjusted, not higher [4] — it does not expect structural expansion.

(c) In-house hyperscaler efforts. High and rising, and a named risk [3]. Every lab is building internal data ops; what gets outsourced is the work with the least IP. Innodata's counter is research-led differentiation (RL gyms, temptation gyms, CBRNE safety) — exactly where Scale, Surge and Mercor also compete.

What the numbers say

  1. Real operating leverage — until it wasn't. Operating margin went from 1.0% (FY2023) to 18.8% (Q1 FY2026), then halved to 9.1% in Q3 FY2026 because SGA rose 96% YoY ($13.7M → $26.9M, of which $9.2M payroll) [2]. Management calls it investment; the market read discipline breaking.
  2. Growth decelerates hard into Q4. "42% or more" implies FY2026 ≈ $357M and Q4 of ~$79.5M — down 17% QoQ, only +9.8% YoY [1]. Consensus FY2026 revenue of $359.6M sits above the guide [11]. Tax also flatters: a ~12% 9M rate versus a 23–25% target adds back roughly $0.15–0.20 of EPS when normalised.

Own historical multiple

Its P/S has gone from 1.03x (FY2022) to 2.69x (FY2023), 6.72x (FY2024), 6.45x (FY2025) and 3.49x now; EV/Sales from ~0.9x to ~16x at the $125.14 high and 3.03x today — an ~81% multiple compression despite 40% revenue growth [9][10].

MY OPINION: the de-rating from ~16x EV/sales to 3.0x against 40% revenue growth is brutal but not irrational — the market moved from capitalising a narrative to capitalising a services P&L. At 12.8x EV/FCF the downside is far better protected than at $125, but 3x sales is still a premium for revenue cancellable on 60 days' notice.

On the sell-side: four analysts, consensus "Strong Buy," average target $111.50 (median $115.50, range $75–$140) [11][23] — 3.1x the price, implying ~53x FY2027 EPS. Targets built for a $100+ share price and never rebased; do not use them as an anchor.

14. What would change my mind

Bullish (would move me to Buy):

  1. Q4 FY2026 (late Feb 2027) guides FY2027 revenue ≥$480M with adjusted gross margin ≥42% — proving Q3's margin break was mix and the $12M/quarter gap was genuinely replaced. Consensus is $460M.

  3. A signed multi-year (3+ year) physical-AI or federal contract — the $5M/yr × 5-year verbal win converting, or a robotics lab committing to a multi-year programme.
  4. Insider buying, or a buyback using the $154M of net cash at 12.8x EV/FCF. Abuhoff or Singhal buying at $35 would beat any analyst target as a signal.

Bearish (would move me to Sell):

  1. Q1 FY2027 revenue below $85M — no sequential recovery from the guided $79.5M Q4 — confirming the termination is the start of deceleration.
  2. A second large customer cuts or pauses a programme, or the 43% customer's share rises further. One more cancellation is existential to the growth story.
  3. Adjusted gross margin below 38% for two quarters, meaning the content-resale mix is structural and the 40% target is fiction.
  4. Any ATM sales disclosed — particularly below $50 — or a cash-and-stock acquisition above $200M, or FY2027 consensus cut below $400M without a multiple reset.
